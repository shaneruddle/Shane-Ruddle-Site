The best area to live in Pattaya depends on your stage of life. In short: Central Pattaya if you're young and want the buzz, Jomtien if you're retired and want the beach, Pratumnak if you want a bit of both, and East Pattaya if you have kids or pets. Whenever someone asks me where they should live, my first question back is about them: are they single or married, do they have children, how old are they, and what do they want out of life here?

I've lived in Pattaya for over 20 years, and our agencies rent and sell property right across the city. Here's how I'd break it down in 2026.

## Where should a young single person live in Pattaya?

Central Pattaya. It's where the action is: restaurants, bars and the buzz of the city on your doorstep. If I were in my thirties again, that's where I'd be.

Space is the trade-off. Central is where your money buys the least, so for ฿20,000 a month you'd probably get a one-bed, but a small one.

## Where should retirees live?

I'd start a retired couple in Jomtien. It's quieter, there's less going on, and you're right by the sea. Day to day, it's the pace of life, the beach, dozens of restaurants to choose from, and plenty of like-minded people. There are lots of retired couples there, so it's easy to make friends.

Jomtien also has older buildings, which works in your favour: ฿20,000 a month will usually get you a one-bed that's a bit bigger than you'd find in Wongamat or Pratumnak.

If, after a few years, Jomtien still feels a bit too busy, the natural next step is East Pattaya.

## Is Pratumnak a good place to live?

Yes, and I'm biased, because I live there. Pratumnak sits between Pattaya and Jomtien and suits people in the middle: quieter than Central, but closer to the action than Jomtien.

Town is about a five-minute drive. Jomtien is just as close, since the road from Pratumnak Soi 5 now runs along Dongtan Beach, and I often head down that way for breakfast at Hemingways. Pratumnak itself has plenty of very good restaurants, and lots of people live here because it's mostly high-rise condos. For ฿20,000 a month, you'd typically get a small one-bed.

## What about Wongamat and Naklua?

Wongamat and Naklua, in the north of the city, are particularly popular with Russian residents, but anyone can enjoy living there. There are great restaurants, and Terminal 21 is within walking distance.

If you want a condo, it's really all about Naklua Soi 18 and Soi 19, which meet halfway along. Away from those, the area is mostly hotels. That's where you'll find buildings such as Sky Beach, Northpoint, Arom and Nova Mirage.

It's one of the pricier areas: ฿20,000 a month might only get you a small studio.

## Is East Pattaya good for families?

Yes. East Pattaya is quieter and suits families and older retirees. It's mainly houses, close to most of the international schools, and if you have pets, it's usually where you'll end up; few condos on the beach side of Sukhumvit Road allow them. I've written more about [raising a family in Pattaya](/articles/is-pattaya-good-for-families).

It's also where your money goes furthest: for ฿20,000 a month, you might get a two- or three-bedroom house, though without a pool.

The trade-off is that it's spread out. You can manage on a motorbike, but most people who live there drive a car. Traffic is fairly light on that side, and there's still plenty to do; it's just further apart.

## What does ฿20,000 a month rent in each area?

You could find something for around ฿20,000 a month in every area. What you get varies a lot (my rough guide, 2026):

- **Central Pattaya:** a small one-bed
- **Pratumnak:** a small one-bed
- **Wongamat:** possibly only a small studio
- **Jomtien:** a slightly bigger one-bed in an older building
- **Na Jomtien and Bang Saray:** a bigger one-bed, but usually set back from the beach rather than on it
- **East Pattaya:** possibly a two- or three-bedroom house without a pool

Huay Yai is mainly houses with almost no condos, and you'd struggle to find anything there for ฿20,000.

## What's the biggest mistake people make when choosing an area?

Not exploring enough. My advice: put your backpack on, grab the sun cream and sunglasses, and spend a whole day walking around the area you're considering. Get a feel for it: what restaurants there are, and who lives there. You can't walk East Pattaya, so drive around it instead.

## Why you'll probably move more than once

Most people here don't settle on one area for good. They move as their circumstances change: a partner, children, a pet, or a child starting international school. I started in Central Pattaya and then moved to Pratumnak, and if I moved again, it would probably be out to East Pattaya.

So my real advice is to treat it as a journey. Rent, live in an area for a while, and move on when it no longer fits your stage of life.
