After more than 20 years in Pattaya, I can honestly say that whether someone is transgender doesn't really register any more. Here it's just part of everyday life.

## Just employees like anybody else

I currently employ two transgender members of staff. I've never really looked at them as transgender. They're just employees like anybody else.

When we do a round of interviews and I ask a manager how it went, it's never even mentioned whether one of the candidates was transgender. We recruit on experience, and we don't discriminate. More in [hiring and firing in Pattaya](/articles/hiring-and-firing-in-pattaya).

## Part of everyday life

That's how it is across Pattaya. If we go to a new restaurant and we're served by a transgender waitress, or have a massage from a transgender therapist, nobody at the table remarks on it. It never comes up in conversation with my friends. People barely notice, let alone comment.

If anyone does comment, it's usually newcomers or tourists. For anyone who's lived here a while, it simply isn't something you talk about.

And I think Thai attitudes are much the same as those of Westerners who've lived here a long time. It's just part of life.

## What newcomers can take from it

If you're new to Pattaya, take the lead from the people who live here. My advice is simple: be respectful. If someone identifies as a certain gender, respect that, don't make a big deal of it, and just get on with it. Treat everyone the same, judge staff on how well they do their job, and you'll quickly find it stops registering for you too.
