If you get an offer for your business in Pattaya, take it seriously, because it may be the only one you get. I've sold somewhere between 12 and 15 businesses over the years, and that's the biggest lesson. More in [valuing a business in Pattaya](/articles/valuing-a-business-in-pattaya).

## The sale I had to undo

The one that sticks in my mind is Fairways, a driving range. I wasn't selling the whole thing, as I was still heavily involved, but I sold a 33% share to my manager at the time, Scott.

He transferred the money, and about a month later we had a problem with the lease.

In Thailand you often get leases of three years plus three plus three. Our first three years were coming up, and the landlady had agreed to renew. Then she decided not to. To be fair to her, there was a backstory: golf balls had been leaving the driving range, and we'd had a complaint from City Hall, which definitely added to her decision.

Either way, the lease was gone, so I gave Scott his money back. I'd sold it, and then I hadn't. More in [selling The Golf Club](/articles/selling-the-golf-club) and [business leases and landlords in Pattaya](/articles/business-leases-and-landlords-in-pattaya).

## Take the first offer

My main advice is to take your first offer. There are so few people who actually want to buy a business here that if someone makes an offer, you should be thankful.

It won't be what you want. I've definitely made the mistake of holding out. Part of it is psychological: you've put in money and effort, and accepting a big loss makes it real, when before it was only a loss on paper.

## Take the emotion out

If you've put your blood and sweat into building something, it's hard when a buyer comes along and doesn't value that effort. But they're not buying your effort. Try to take your emotions out and just look at the numbers.

## Imagine yourself a month later

My third tip: picture yourself a month after selling at a loss. Will you be happy or sad? Relieved to be free of it, or kicking yourself? That will usually tell you whether to accept the deal.

## Be realistic

Selling a business in Pattaya is rarely about getting what it's worth to you. It's about finding the one buyer who wants it and making a deal you can live with. More in [why the restaurant business is the toughest I've been in](/articles/why-the-restaurant-business-is-the-toughest).
