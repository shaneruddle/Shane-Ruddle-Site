Every business owner has a list of the ones that got away. I've got plenty. The lesson I've settled on isn't that I should have trusted my gut more. It's simpler than that: you can't win them all.

## The burger restaurant I walked away from

The biggest one still stings a little. A good friend of mine was looking for an opportunity, so I went round Pattaya with him looking at locations. I found him what I thought was the perfect spot.

He didn't have the money at the time, so he asked if I wanted to get involved, which had really been the idea from the start. But I wasn't sure. It wasn't the location. I just wasn't sure he could run it.

So I tried to spread the risk and brought someone else in. That person then pulled out, which knocked my confidence, and I backed out too.

Undeterred, my friend partnered with someone he knew who already had a couple of restaurants in a burger brand. They turned that location into one of theirs, and it was very successful. That brand has since grown into a well-established chain with dozens of locations, and my friend is now involved in several of them locally, doing very well.

## Daisy D's

Another one I can name is Daisy D's, an open-air bar and restaurant on the beach in Jomtien. I know Jomtien better than most, partly because of Hemingways Jomtien, and I didn't rate the location. It was beachfront, but I thought it was too far down. When I heard the rent and the fixed overheads, I wasn't interested at all.

Friends of mine did buy into it. They turned it around, and it's now very successful.

## The Pantry

Then there's The Pantry. A friend who had worked for me before came to me with it during COVID. I knew him well and trusted his work ethic and his ability to get things done. His argument was simple: people still need to eat.

I said no, purely because of the timing. It wasn't a big investment or a big risk, and it made money pretty much straight away. That one was a real mistake.

## It cuts both ways

It works the other way too. Hemingways Jomtien wasn't offered to me first. It was offered to a friend, and he turned it down. Not because he didn't believe in it, but because the timing was wrong for him. I took his place, and I know he regrets it.

That's usually what it comes down to for me as well. It's rarely about whether I think something will work. I'm fairly confident I can make most things work, not all, but most. It's about timing.

## What I actually need to work on

Missing the odd opportunity doesn't bother me too much. I just keep going and trying different businesses. The mistake I make more often is the opposite one: I don't exit quickly enough when a business isn't working. That's something I need to get better at.

## My take

You'll pass on things that go on to do brilliantly, and you'll take on things others turned down. You can't win them all. Don't dwell on the ones that got away. Keep looking, and be quicker to walk away from the ones that aren't working.
