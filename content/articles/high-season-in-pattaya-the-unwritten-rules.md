Every high season, hundreds of thousands of people arrive in Pattaya for the first time. That's great for the city and for businesses like mine. But it also brings a lot of frustration, because many first-timers don't know how things work here. Before you come, take ten minutes to learn the basics. It'll make your trip better, and it might keep you out of trouble.

## What changes in high season

Residents don't really change their routines. What changes is the mood. Ordinary days get disrupted, and you see people getting short with each other.

On the roads, you get people driving on the wrong side, or turning around wherever they like because they don't know where they're going. People step straight out into traffic. As I was talking this article through, someone walked out right in front of my car.

In the restaurants, it shows up in smaller ways. In high season, we get people walking in with no shirt on. That never happens in the low season, because everyone who lives here knows it isn't done. First-timers just don't know.

## Why residents get frustrated

You could argue that people who live here should help visitors more, and many do. But there are so many visitors, and many don't speak English. It isn't always easy to help someone you can't talk to.

The real issue is the unwritten rules. Long-timers know them without thinking. Visitors don't, and nobody tells them.

## A few of the unwritten rules

- **Feet and heads.** Don't point the soles of your feet at people, and don't touch a Thai person on the head.
- **Face.** Losing your temper or embarrassing someone in public goes down badly here. Stay calm, even when things go wrong.
- **Dress.** Keep your shirt on away from the beach, especially in restaurants and shops.
- **Tipping.** It isn't compulsory, but it's appreciated. Leaving the small change, or a little extra for good service, is normal. (More in [tipping in Pattaya](/articles/tipping-in-pattaya).)
- **Roads.** Thailand drives on the left. Don't make sudden turns, and never assume traffic will stop for you. (See [crossing the road in Pattaya](/articles/expect-the-unexpected-crossing-the-road-in-pattaya).)

## My advice

Before you come, read a book, a blog post or watch a YouTube video on the top ten things to know about Thai culture. It takes ten minutes. My own list is [cultural mistakes to avoid in Thailand](/articles/cultural-mistakes-in-thailand).

Learning the culture is part of the experience. It'll make your trip more enjoyable, it'll make the people around you warmer towards you, and it might stop you getting into trouble. Pattaya is a brilliant place to visit. Just come knowing the rules.
