I've said before that finding staff in Pattaya isn't the hard part, keeping them is. I've changed my mind. Finding good ones is hard too.

It surprises people. Anyone who hasn't tried to hire a foreigner here is usually amazed at how hard it is. And it's not just me. Friends of mine who've run businesses here for a long time struggle just as much.

## Work ethic is the problem

The biggest issue is work ethic. I don't know whether it's a generational thing, something happening all over the world, or just me getting older and looking back at how I was. Maybe work just isn't how people define themselves any more. But a strong work ethic is rare now, and if you have one, you really stand out.

## The lifestyle works against it

Pattaya makes it harder still. People come here for the lifestyle and a more relaxed pace of life, and that runs counter to a strong work ethic. If people wanted to work really hard, they'd be in London or New York, or at least Bangkok. Most of the really good people go to Bangkok. More in [Pattaya vs Bangkok](/articles/pattaya-vs-bangkok).

## The catch-22

You can't really spot work ethic in an interview. You only find out by working with someone. One of the best I've had was Mark, and I'd known him for a very long time.

That's the catch-22. To try someone out, you have to pay for a work permit and training, and if it doesn't work out, you've lost the money and the time. So I, and other business owners I know who hire foreigners, are guarded about taking on people we don't know. We rely on recommendations, but those are hard to come by too. More in [hiring foreign staff in Pattaya](/articles/hiring-foreign-staff-in-pattaya) and [hiring and firing](/articles/hiring-and-firing-in-pattaya).

## I'd have done anything to stay

What surprises me is the lack of hunger. When I first arrived, I'd have done any job, however humble, if it meant I could stay in Thailand. I'd have worked as hard as I needed to. Over the last 20 years life in the West has got much harder, so you'd think Thailand would be even more attractive. But people don't seem to want it as badly.

## CVs with no effort

We get CVs every week for our property companies, and many show no real effort. People blanket-email everyone and list experience that has nothing to do with the job. Last week one applicant for a sales role did have relevant experience, but it was buried under so many irrelevant jobs that it overshadowed everything. If someone won't put effort into applying for a job, it's not a good start.

## Think that's you? Get in touch

If you want to stay here and build a life, you want a job, you have a strong work ethic, you'll put the effort in and you're adaptable, [get in touch](/contact).

But do me a favour: put some thought into your application rather than sending it out to everyone. There aren't many jobs here, so it's worth getting it right.
