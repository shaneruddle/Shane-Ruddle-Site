I've run restaurants, bars, a car rental company and real estate agencies. Of all of them, real estate is the hardest. I don't think we've ever done a single transaction where everything went smoothly.

## It's much more than matching buyers and sellers

A lot of people think real estate is just marketing properties and putting a buyer together with a seller, or a tenant with a landlord. That's actually a very small part of the job. There are an awful lot of moving parts, and no real formula.

## Building relationships fast

One of the biggest challenges is building a relationship with someone in a very short space of time. That's genuinely difficult.

In Pattaya it's even harder, because there's no exclusivity. Owners don't list their home with just one agency to sell or rent, and buyers and tenants don't stick with one agency either. It's a free-for-all. So while you're building a relationship with someone, you know there's a good chance they're doing the same with another agent at the same time.

## Is the customer even interested?

Just working out whether someone is serious is hard. We've had customers whose body language said they weren't interested at all, only to find out later they'd bought through another agency. And we've had people raring to go, then something happens and it all falls through.

## A minefield from deposit to completion

Once someone does want to buy, there are a lot of steps between the first sign of interest, or the deposit, and actually completing. Then there's some after-sales work too. You have to be really careful not to miss any of them:

- The Land Office transfer process
- Making sure the money moves safely and correctly
- Keeping every party on track through to completion

There is a process, but it changes depending on who the buyer is, who owns the property, which other agency is involved, or who the tenant is. You get pulled in every direction.

## Compared with my other businesses

In a restaurant or the car rental business, it's common for a customer to have a great experience from start to finish. They come in, get served straight away, enjoy the food, the music and the atmosphere, and leave thinking that was a really good time.

That just doesn't seem to happen in real estate. Something always comes up along the way.

## My take

If you're thinking of getting into real estate in Pattaya, go in with your eyes open. And if you're buying or renting, be patient with your agent. A lot more is going on behind the scenes than you might think.
