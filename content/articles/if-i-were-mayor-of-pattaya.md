Pattaya is a lot better than it used to be, and it keeps improving. But I walk the city a lot, and there are things I'd fix tomorrow if I were mayor.

## 1. Litter

First, I'd tackle litter. It's a problem across Thailand, and Pattaya is no exception. People flick things out of car windows and drop rubbish wherever they are. Walking along the beach this morning, I passed plenty of bottles just left on the sand.

There are people who clean the beach, and they'll eventually collect it. But if the wind picks up first, it ends up in the sea or caught in the hedges and trees, and it looks bad.

You could pay more people to clean up, but the real fix is education: teaching locals and tourists to take pride in the place and clean up after themselves. And make it easy by putting bins everywhere. A cleaner city would also do more for Pattaya's image than anything else.

## 2. Neglected land

There's a lot of empty land around the city that's overgrown and becomes a dumping ground for rubbish and fly-tipping. I'd fine owners whose land doesn't look managed.

## 3. Pavements

One of my good friends uses a wheelchair, and I often think about him when I'm walking around. With electricity poles, junction boxes and lamp posts in the way, parts of the pavement are hard enough for someone able-bodied. For him they're impossible. When he goes out, he just uses the road.

To be fair, the pavements are much better than they used to be. They're just not good enough yet.

## 4. Roundabouts

People might expect me to mention the flooding, but I think that's improved a lot. We still get some in a really heavy downpour, but with that volume of water I don't think much more can be done. More in [the rainy season in Pattaya](/articles/rainy-season-in-pattaya).

What I'd add is roundabouts. (As I tell our rental customers, [you'll use U-turns instead](/articles/driving-in-pattaya).) In the UK and most of Europe they're everywhere, and I think they keep traffic flowing. Here it's stop-start at traffic lights. More in [Pattaya traffic](/articles/pattaya-traffic).

## 5. Do the job properly the first time

When work does get done here, it often isn't done well. A road gets laid, and a year later it's being dug up again because it wasn't done right. Drains often go in the middle of the road, so you're driving over them all the time, instead of at the side. Eventually someone digs it all up and moves them. Too much of the work around the city feels like it's done with half the care it needs.

## 6. More Thai culture

Pattaya is good at attracting tourists. I'd put more emphasis on traditional Thai culture and add more Thai cultural attractions.

## 7. Dual pricing

This one isn't really a mayor's decision, but it's my biggest bugbear. I understand that taxpayers might pay a bit less, but I don't see it in most other countries I visit, like Vietnam, Japan, Singapore or Dubai. If they have it, it isn't obvious. Here it's carved into wooden signs at national parks, in English, and the foreigner price can be ten times the Thai price. It puts a negative spin on people's trips. I'd scrap it.
