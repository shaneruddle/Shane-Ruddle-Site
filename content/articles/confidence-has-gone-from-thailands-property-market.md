Thailand's property market has a confidence problem. Buyers haven't disappeared, but almost everyone is waiting. From where I sit, running property agencies in Pattaya, the government's handling of the nominee crackdown has been a sledgehammer to the market.

*I'm not a lawyer or financial adviser, and this is my opinion, not advice.*

## Everyone is waiting

Consumer confidence is the lowest I've seen it. People who were looking to buy are now saying they'll wait until there's more clarity. Even buyers who could buy a condo outright in foreign ownership are holding back.

I understand why. Many think prices could fall further, and that's a fair assumption. Prices are going down, but there are very few buyers. More in [the Pattaya property market](/articles/pattaya-property-market).

## A sledgehammer

The nominee crackdown has hit everyone, Thais as well as foreigners, because there are simply fewer people in the market. More in [nominee companies in Thailand: should you panic?](/articles/nominee-companies-in-thailand)

It's also been very public. The crackdown has been covered again and again on TV news and in the papers, with the authorities announcing the cases they uncover. I understand part of the aim: if Thais understand what being a nominee means, fewer will agree to be one. But the side effect has been a big drop in confidence.

That has knock-on effects. Fewer sales mean less in transfer fees and taxes for the government. And agents' commissions have fallen, and most agents are Thai.

The people affected aren't only large investors. An ordinary foreigner who bought a house for himself and his wife or partner is worried too, even if he's unlikely to be the target. More in [is the nominee crackdown really about the OECD?](/articles/is-the-nominee-crackdown-really-about-the-oecd)

## How I'd have done it

If I'd been in the room, I'd have enforced the law quietly. Investigate the cases that matter, but without turning it into a public campaign that frightens the whole market.

## My advice right now

My honest advice is to sit on the fence. Even those of us in the industry don't know what's around the corner.

- **Thinking of buying a house?** Rent for now and wait for more clarity. More in [renting a condo in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-condo-in-pattaya).
- **Want a condo in foreign name?** There are some good deals, so if that's what you want, by all means go ahead.

## The other side

The government would argue that nominee structures break Thai law, and that enforcing it firmly and publicly is the only way to stop them. Some would say a short-term dip is a price worth paying for a cleaner market, and that buyers who own property legally have nothing to fear. Others would add that falling prices and global economic conditions were already weighing on the market before the crackdown.
