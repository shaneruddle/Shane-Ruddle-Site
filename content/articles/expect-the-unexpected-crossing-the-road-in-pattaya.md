Shortly after midnight on 16 November, a 39-year-old Russian woman was hit by a car on a pedestrian crossing on Jomtien Second Road. She later died in hospital. My thoughts are with her family and friends. I want to use it to make one point as clearly as I can: things don't work here the way they do back home, and assuming they do can be fatal.

## A zebra crossing is not a promise

At home, you'd be well within your rights to step onto a zebra crossing and expect the traffic to stop. Here, it really doesn't happen.

A lot of drivers on the roads here have never been properly taught how to drive, and certainly not what a zebra crossing means. As I was talking this article through, I stopped at a crossing to let someone over, and the car next to me nearly ran them down. Just like that.

So my advice is simple. When you get to a zebra crossing, imagine it isn't there. You're just crossing a road. Make no assumptions.

## The roads aren't set up to keep you safe

This isn't only about drivers. The roads themselves are part of the problem.

- Street lights often don't work, and lighting is poor in many places.
- Crossings are sometimes right on junctions, where they shouldn't be.
- There are no railings, like you get in the UK or Dubai, to funnel people to safe crossing points.
- There are hardly any footbridges in Pattaya, so most of the time you have to cross at street level.

In this case, the driver reportedly said tree shadows blocked his view, and police are reviewing video of the crossing. I wasn't there and I don't know the details. But I can easily believe a driver doing the right speed, sober and paying attention, might simply not see someone in those conditions. That's what makes it so dangerous.

The same road has seen this before. In January 2025, a pedestrian was killed by a motorbike on a crossing on Jomtien Second Road, after a car had stopped to let him and his wife cross. In March 2026, another pedestrian was killed crossing the same road. And it isn't just that road. Pedestrians are hit across Pattaya far more often than most visitors realise, and not every case makes the news. This is not a one-off.

## How to cross safely

- Cross in a group where you can, rather than on your own. Sadly, that isn't always possible late at night.
- Treat every crossing as if it isn't there. Wait for a real gap.
- Assume the driver hasn't seen you, especially at night.
- If a car stops for you, don't assume the lane next to it will. Watch for motorbikes coming up the inside or outside.
- Be extra careful near junctions and in dark stretches.
- Keep your head on a swivel, all the time.

## If you have children

Be extra, extra cautious. Don't let your kids go out and cross the road without you. When you come up to a crossing, make sure they're holding your hand. Children assume cars will stop even more than adults do. (More in [is Pattaya good for families?](/articles/is-pattaya-good-for-families))

## Expect the unexpected

Thailand is a wonderful place to visit and to live. But the roads are chaotic and unforgiving. If you're here on holiday, leave your home-country assumptions at the airport.

Expect the unexpected, and get home safely.
