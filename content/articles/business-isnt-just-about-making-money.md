When I started out in business, it was mainly about making a profit and reaching financial independence. That was the goal. For now, at least, that part is solved. Things can change quickly, but I keep going anyway, because business has given me a lot more than money.

## Relationships

Business has given me relationships. I call them relationships rather than friendships, because I can't honestly say all my staff are my friends. They're friendly to me and I'm friendly to them, but it's a step or two down from friendship.

It's still a real relationship, though. If I walk into Cajun Life Cafe, they all know me and I know them. We know each other's names, and we usually have a bit of a joke if I'm collecting food. That matters to me.

## What it taught me about myself

Business has taught me how stubborn and persistent I can be, and how determined I get when someone tells me I can't do something.

That started with golf, not business. At 14 or 15, I told someone I wanted to be a professional golfer. He laughed and said it was never going to happen. Maybe it was because I was small at the time. I went on to turn pro and start the PGA's training. I didn't finish the course, but I was classified as a pro, and I represented England at university level, playing for English Universities.

In business, I can't point to one person who doubted me. But in Pattaya, and especially online these days, there are plenty of people saying it can't be done. You're wasting your money. You're wasting your time. You'll only ever lose money in Thailand. It's nice to prove all of them wrong.

## A sense of purpose

More than anything, business gives me a sense of purpose, and for me that comes down to continual progress. I think that's what humans are built for: to keep evolving, keep improving and keep becoming a better version of ourselves. I've written more about that in [small improvements, big results](/articles/small-improvements-big-results).

Maybe there's a little fear at the back of my mind too, because financial independence can always change. But when you look at people who are extremely wealthy and still keep going, I think it's the same thing. It's a mission of continual improvement.

## My take

Money might be why you start, but it's rarely why you keep going. Build something that gives you relationships, teaches you about yourself and gives you a reason to keep getting better.
