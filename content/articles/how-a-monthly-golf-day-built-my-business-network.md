For about 10 years I ran the Pattaya Amateur Golf Series, or PAGS as it became known. I set it up to make money on the day. What I actually got out of it was worth far more: a business network I'm still benefiting from today.

## What PAGS was

It started when I had Fairways Golf Range and Resort. It was a monthly tournament, always on the last Wednesday of the month, so everyone knew exactly when it was. We moved around different courses, although for a couple of years we played at just one, Phoenix.

The biggest day we ever had was about 168 golfers at Phoenix, teeing off from 6:30 in the morning right through the day. It was a ridiculous number.

I was pretty much the sole organiser. I had some help on the day, but after 10 years it was a well-oiled machine. There weren't many problems I hadn't already come across.

## Why it built my network

I was the founder and the focal point. I sorted out all the tee times, so if you wanted to play, you had to come to me. And because I was a good golfer with a reputation for it, I had a fair amount of respect from the players, which definitely helped. (More on my golf background in [my first round of golf in Thailand](/articles/my-first-round-of-golf-in-thailand).)

Through PAGS I met an awful lot of people, and a lot of them were business owners. Even now, I still bump into people I first met through the series.

## The deals you never see coming

There are too many to list. Some were small, like car rentals. Some were property. Some property deals didn't come from a player at all, but from someone who knew someone who played, or a recommendation from someone I'd met through PAGS.

Sometimes you don't even know that's where a deal came from. That's how a real network works.

## How it ended

COVID finished it off. I thought about reviving it afterwards, but sponsorship was what made it worthwhile, and after COVID sponsorship was very hard to find.

## My advice: build a real network

I think building a network is underrated. A lot of people do it through social media now, and that has its place. But I don't think you can beat face-to-face.

PAGS ended up being a bit like a loss leader. I didn't plan it that way. I started a golf tournament because I felt Pattaya needed one, and I didn't think the ones already running were being run particularly well. The network just came out of it.

If I'd sat down back then and asked how to build a business network in Pattaya, I'd probably have decided that something like PAGS was an excellent way to do it. So if you're starting out here, find something you're good at, bring people together around it regularly, and be the person they have to come to. The money on the day is the least of it.
