This one goes against my own interests. I run a motorbike rental business, and electric bikes are probably the future. But I think if silent, fast electric motorbikes took off here on the scale they have in China, Thailand would see a big rise in accidents and deaths. Unless something changes, it could be catastrophic.

## What I saw in China

When I visited China, I was shocked by how many electric bikes there were. Literally thousands of them. (More on that trip in [Silent Shanghai, noisy Bangkok](/articles/silent-shanghai-noisy-bangkok).)

China has tried to manage the risk. Many cities have dedicated lanes for bikes. But where there aren't lanes, the bikes go everywhere: on the roads and on the pavements.

As a tourist, I nearly got clipped several times. They're quick, and you can't hear them coming. Walking along a pavement while bikes zip past you silently is unnerving. I assume there are speed limits, but some riders were going very fast.

To be fair, the riders there seemed fairly competent. And almost everyone on the street is local. They know silent bikes are everywhere and how to deal with them. Visitors don't.

## Why Thailand is different

Thailand has far more tourists mixed in with local traffic, people who don't know how things work here. Add in what I've written about before: roads that aren't set up for safety, poor lighting, and drivers who were never properly taught. (See [two motorbike accidents in 250 yards](/articles/two-motorbike-accidents-in-250-yards).)

There are already huge numbers of motorbikes on the roads. At least today's petrol bikes make a noise. If we switched to silent bikes that accelerate fast, I think people would be clipped left, right and centre: pedestrians, other riders, and tourists who've never ridden one.

## Why the usual fixes won't work here

- **Speed limits** would mostly be ignored.
- **Dedicated lanes** would be fantastic, and ordinary motorbikes could use them too. But the roads aren't built for it. In places like Bangkok, many roads are simply too narrow. It would take massive investment.
- **Licensing** only works if it's enforced. Plenty of people already ride with a revoked licence or none at all.

## What I'd do: make them audible

The one thing I think would actually work is a compulsory warning sound. Some kind of whistle or hum, so you can at least hear them coming.

The idea isn't new. In the EU, every new electric car has had to make a warning sound at low speeds since 2021, and the US has a similar rule. Thailand should apply the same idea to electric motorbikes, before they become common, not after.

## My verdict

Electric motorbikes aren't the problem on their own. Silent, fast bikes on Thailand's roads, with Thailand's levels of training and enforcement, are a dangerous mix. If they're coming, make sure people can hear them.
