One text message can take you from a relaxed evening to a sleepless night in a heartbeat. That's a side of owning businesses people rarely talk about, and after 20 years I still haven't found a way to stop the big ones getting to me. What I have found is what gets me through.

## The night at the cinema

A while back I was at the cinema with a friend, properly relaxed, enjoying the film. Then my phone buzzed. It was a message from a customer, who's also a friend, telling me there had been a serious incident involving a customer at one of my venues.

I won't go into the details. It ended with me having to let a manager go. But what I remember most clearly is how fast my mood changed. One message, and the evening was over. So was my sleep that night, and at least the next two or three days.

## It doesn't just affect you

It's very hard to control your emotions in that moment, and it doesn't only affect you. It affects the people around you.

I know from experience that over the years I've been moody and short with my kids after bad news at work. It's difficult to compartmentalise when your head is running through every possible scenario. That's something I'm not proud of, but I think a lot of business owners will recognise it.

## The noise and the big ones

Experience does help. These days I only really let the big ones affect me. The small problems, the noise, might rattle someone with less experience, but I mostly just see them through, almost ignore them to some degree.

The big ones are different. They still get to me.

## What calms me down

For me it's a plan. The stress and anxiety mostly come from not having the full picture and not knowing how you're going to handle it. Once I start to get some clarity and put a plan in place, I calm down.

I'll probably shoot myself in the foot saying this, but things are never as bad as they first seem. I've very rarely had a problem that turned out worse than it was first described. Human nature is to overreact rather than play things down, and the person delivering the news often makes it sound worse than it is.

Talking about it helps sometimes too, depending on who you talk to. But mostly it's two things: time and the plan.

## If you're in the middle of one

If you've just had that message and you're lying awake, you probably don't have the full picture yet. Get the facts, make a plan, and give it a few days. It's very likely not as bad as it feels tonight.
