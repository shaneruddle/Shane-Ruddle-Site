Some foreigners, especially those who don't know Pattaya, assume most Thai women here work in the bars, or are "available". It's wrong. Only a very small percentage work in that industry, and even then, working in entertainment doesn't mean someone sells sex. More in [why Pattaya's old reputation is out of date](/articles/pattaya-old-reputation-out-of-date).

## Why it may once have been different

Twenty years ago, there were far fewer hotels and restaurants. For many people arriving in Pattaya from elsewhere in Thailand, there weren't many jobs outside the entertainment industry, so it made sense for a lot of them to work there.

Today it's a different city. You can get a well-paid job with a corporation, a hotel or a small business. Across my seven companies, Thai women work in every kind of role, including managers and property agents. ## A question I never forgot

Years ago, when I worked at the Sportsman, a customer quietly asked me whether the women in the bar were "available". I was genuinely shocked. If you live here, it's usually easy to tell who works in that industry and who doesn't. For a tourist it isn't always obvious, and I think he was honestly asking. But it shows how strong the assumption can be. More in [from bar manager to 7 businesses](/articles/bar-manager-to-7-businesses).

## How people signal it themselves

Over the years, it's become clearer from the outside. Women working in the bar scene tend to make it obvious, because that's their business. Many other women, aware of the stigma, deliberately dress to make it clear they have a career outside it. It's not a perfect rule, but it's a big part of how locals tell the difference.

## A Western stereotype, not a Thai one

Most of my Thai staff probably aren't even aware the stereotype exists. It comes from the West. In my experience, most Thais don't look down on people in the way some cultures do. You might see some of that among the very wealthy in Bangkok, but rarely in everyday life.

It's also an old stereotype, 20 or 30 years old, held mainly by some long-time Western visitors. Newer visitors who've only been coming for the last decade often have no idea it exists.

## Where it comes from

Films played a part. One famous line people still quote comes from Full Metal Jacket, a Vietnam War film, and the character who says it is Vietnamese, not Thai. That shows how loosely the image got pinned on Thailand.

It also lingers because some Westerners who first came here 20-odd years ago still describe Pattaya the way it was then. People have short memories for some things, but they remember this when it suits them.

## My advice to visitors

Don't assume anyone works in that industry unless they make it very obvious. Don't get overfriendly. Treat everyone, and women especially, with respect. That goes for people who do work in the bar scene too. If you've treated someone with respect and got it wrong, you can always back out politely.
