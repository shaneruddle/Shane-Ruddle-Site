People say a hybrid gives you the best of both worlds: petrol and electric in one car. After owning one in Thailand, I'd say it's the worst of both worlds. Next time, I'll buy a full EV.

## Problems on both sides

With a hybrid, you've got two systems that can go wrong, and mine has had trips to the garage for both, the engine and the hybrid side.

Right now it has a problem that nobody seems able to diagnose. I'm fairly sure it's down to the hybrid system. A lot of garages here just don't have much experience with hybrids, so they struggle to find the fault.

## Barely worth charging

My hybrid only does around 40 km on electric. A lot of the time, that's five or ten minutes of driving, so you go through the hassle of charging for very little.

On top of that, you've got the same problem every EV driver has here: [there isn't enough charging infrastructure](/articles/thailand-still-lacks-ev-charging-infrastructure).

These days, I don't even charge it. It's just not worth the hassle for what I get back. So most of the time I'm driving a petrol car carrying a battery around.

## One or the other

I'd only ever buy one or the other now. Pulling into a petrol station and filling the tank, or getting a full charge on a proper EV, beats a half-and-half.

## Why my next car will be electric

It's not really about the environment for me. I care about it, but it wouldn't be the main reason. I've driven fully electric cars, and I just prefer them. I prefer the drive and the acceleration, and I think they're simply better cars all round.

## The other side

Plenty of people are happy with their hybrids, especially self-charging ones that never need plugging in, and for someone who does a lot of short trips and can charge at home every night, a plug-in hybrid can make sense. It just hasn't worked out that way for me.

## My take

If you're buying a car in Thailand, pick a side. Go petrol, or go fully electric. Don't buy two sets of problems.
