## Why it happens so fast here

The barrier to opening a business here is fairly low. Lots of people want to stay in Pattaya and struggle to find a job working for someone else, so the natural move is to make their own job by starting a business. More in [finding a job in Pattaya](/articles/finding-a-job-in-pattaya).

In the West, you rarely see a competitor open right next door. In Thailand it's very common: massage shop next to massage shop, coffee shop next to coffee shop, salon next to salon.

## Padel: from one venue to five

The first padel venue in Pattaya was Play Padel, with a couple of courts. It was busy enough, but not especially busy. Then high season came, and it got busy enough that players struggled to get a court.

That's the trap. Outsiders only see a snapshot at the very peak of the year. They don't see the full-year figures, so they think there's huge demand and open their own courts. Now there are five, and the same customers are split between them. More in [Pattaya's high season is getting shorter](/articles/pattayas-high-season-is-getting-shorter) and [staying fit in Pattaya](/articles/staying-fit-in-pattaya).

## How market dilution plays out

It usually happens in stages. The first mover makes money. A second business arrives and splits the market, and for a while both can do fine, because the market is still growing. But more people keep coming in, and eventually you reach a point where nobody is making much money at all. It ends up being a race to the bottom.

You can see it around Mabprachan Lake. People realised the area was growing, and there's so much land there that it's easy to open a restaurant. A lot of restaurants went up very quickly. As far as I know, none of them are doing as well as they used to when customers had fewer choices.

## Cajun Life Cafe and the regulars who opened rivals

I've seen it with Cajun Life Cafe too. On the same street, Pratumnak Soi 5, there are now two other healthy-eating restaurants. Both owners used to be regulars at Cajun. It's no coincidence. They saw Cajun was busy and thought there was something there. More in [eating healthily in Pattaya](/articles/eating-healthily-in-pattaya).

Honestly, that's perfectly normal here. It's dog eat dog, and there are no real friends when it comes to making money. People justify it by saying they have to make a living and support their family. I understand that, and I don't think there's anything wrong with it.

I welcome competition, and I don't much mind how close it is. As long as people stick to the unwritten rules and don't copy you outright, it's fair game. Nobody owns an area or a concept.

## My advice to the first mover

- **Move quickly and keep innovating** to stay ahead. Easier said than done, but it's the only real defence.
- **Make the most of a good patch.** If you're having a great run, there's a good chance it won't last.
- **Consider buying the competition.** Sometimes it's better to buy a competitor than to compete with it.

## Buy the competition

My old boss, Dave, runs the Sportsman on Soi 13. He had the chance to buy the restaurant right opposite, which was really his only competition on that street. I convinced him to do it. My pitch was simple: if you don't buy it and someone else does, and they do a better job than you, they'll take some of your market share. Even if it's a bit too much work right now, it's the wise move.

He bought it, and I know he doesn't regret it. More in [from bar manager to 7 businesses](/articles/bar-manager-to-7-businesses) and [valuing a business in Pattaya](/articles/valuing-a-business-in-pattaya).
