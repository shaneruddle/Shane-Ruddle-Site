Thailand has embraced electric cars, but the charging network hasn't kept up. If you can charge at home, an EV is great. If you can't, and a lot of people in Pattaya can't, be ready for some hassle.

## Booking a slot, then waiting anyway

My partner has a fully electric BYD. I've never owned a full EV myself, so I hadn't really experienced charging on the road until we drove back from Bangkok recently and needed to top up.

At a PTT station, or most places with chargers, there are usually only two or three. You have to book a slot in an app and be there at your time. But if another car is still plugged in, you wait. Sometimes the driver doesn't come back on time, even when they get a notification, because they're off food shopping or doing something else. So there's a lot of standing around.

With a petrol car, you turn up at the petrol station. You might queue behind a car or two, but that's as restrictive as it gets.

## Too many apps

The other headache is apps. Different hotels, shopping centres and petrol stations use different charging networks, so you end up needing half a dozen apps just to be sure you can charge and pay wherever you stop. It's hassle most drivers simply aren't used to.

## The condo problem

If you could just charge at home, most of this wouldn't matter. But a lot of us in Pattaya live in condos. Plenty of condos now have charging points, but not all, so many EV owners have to rely on shopping centres and petrol stations.

## What would fix it

- **Every condo should have charging points.** Buildings could easily track how much they're used and add more when they're needed.
- **More public chargers.** Thailand simply needs more of them, so two or three per station stops being the norm.
- **Fewer apps.** One payment system that works across networks would remove a lot of the friction.

## The other side

To be fair, the network is growing fast, and for anyone who can charge at home overnight, day-to-day driving is cheap and easy. Most of the pain is on longer trips and for people without a home charger.

## My take

I still think EVs are better cars, and my next one will be electric. (More on that in [why I'd never buy a hybrid again](/articles/why-id-never-buy-a-hybrid-again).) But until charging is as easy as filling up, think about where you'll charge before you buy.
