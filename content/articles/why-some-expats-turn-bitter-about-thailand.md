Some expats turn bitter about Thailand after a few years. I know, because I was one of them. What turned it around was realising the problem wasn't Thailand. It was how I was looking at it. More in [what I'd tell my younger self](/articles/what-id-tell-my-younger-self).

## How it happened to me

I came here as a young man, eager to get things done. I was impatient and probably stressed, and I got irritated with the way things work here. Things weren't going the way I'd hoped.

My first reaction, like a lot of people's, was to look outward and blame Thailand, rather than look at myself and how I could approach things differently. More in [what I wish I'd known before moving to Thailand](/articles/what-i-wish-id-known-before-moving-to-thailand).

## What changed

Two things.

First, I started taking responsibility. I couldn't keep blaming other people for the position I was in. It's what I tell my children, my staff and my friends now: you'll get help along the way, but in the end the buck stops with you.

Second, I realised how lucky I was to live here, and that I shouldn't jeopardise it. This was going to be home for a lot longer than I'd first thought, so I had to get my head around how things work here.

My saying is: if you don't like something, change it. If you can't change it, change the way you think about it. More in [the best business advice I've been given](/articles/best-business-advice-ive-been-given).

## Warning signs

- **Moaning all the time.** If you're negative about everything, notice it.
- **Outbursts at home.** Snapping at your partner or children is a sign the pressure is getting to you.
- **Outbursts in public.** This is the real red flag.

We had one just recently at one of my venues. A group of four came in, and one of them left early without paying. When the other three settled up, the staff mentioned the extra ฿155 still owed. One of them completely kicked off, shouting and swearing. Looking at someone like that, I'd say he's let this place get to him far too much. More in [the customer isn't always right](/articles/the-customer-isnt-always-right) and [things expats do that drive me mad](/articles/things-expats-do-that-drive-me-mad).

## Don't let it get to you

If you recognise yourself in any of this, stop looking outward. Take responsibility, remember why you came, and change the way you think about the things you can't change.
