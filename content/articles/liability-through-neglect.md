Many staff don't realise that their own actions at work can make them personally liable. Not following procedure isn't just a reason to lose your job. Depending on the industry and the circumstances, it can land you in a legal dispute, and shrugging your shoulders isn't a defence.

## Where it goes wrong

It's usually the simple things. Not checking a contract properly in the property business. Not checking the inventory. At Pattaya Rent a Car, if a car gets stolen because someone went completely against our company procedure, I have the right as the owner to pursue them for negligence.

Under Thai law, the business is generally responsible to the customer for what its staff do at work. But if the business has to pay out because of an employee's wrongful act, it can then seek to recover that money from the employee.

## Why staff don't see it coming

I think there are two reasons.

First, employment here is often quite informal, so a lot of people would never think that working for someone carries any personal risk.

Second, when there's a problem at work, a fee or a fine to pay, or a customer to reimburse, it's almost always the business owner who pays. Usually that's simply because the employee doesn't have the money to cover it. Some staff may also assume a foreign owner is far wealthier than they really are. Because the owner normally pays, I think some staff see that as their protection. It isn't.

## Follow procedure, and ask if you're not sure

My message to staff is simple. Follow procedure. If you're not sure, ask a superior.

If you act on your own assumptions and get it wrong, being fired could be the least of your problems.

## The other side

Employers have a responsibility too. Procedures only protect anyone if they're clear, written down and properly taught. It's hard to hold someone to a rule nobody explained to them. And because the business answers to the customer first, the owner can't simply pass the blame on. There are also strict limits on deducting money from someone's wages, so it isn't as simple as docking their pay.

*I'm not a lawyer. This is my experience as an employer, not legal advice.*
