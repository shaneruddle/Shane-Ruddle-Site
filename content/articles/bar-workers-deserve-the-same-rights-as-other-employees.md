Women who work in Pattaya's bar scene don't have the rights an ordinary employee has. In my view, that should change. More in [why some women work in Pattaya's bar scene](/articles/why-some-women-work-in-pattayas-bar-scene).

## Outside the system

Most aren't officially employed. They work in what's called the informal sector, so they don't pay into social security as employees and don't get the welfare, insurance or protections that come with it. Sex work itself is still illegal in Thailand under the Prevention and Suppression of Prostitution Act of 1996, so the women doing it fall outside labour law altogether.

## Recognise it for what it is

I struggle to say it, but sex tourism is a real thing. It's one reason many people, especially men, come to Thailand, and the industry brings a lot of money into the country. So it should be recognised for what it is, and the people working in it should have rights. Some kind of union or collective voice would let them talk openly about the industry and what they do.

Groups such as Empower Foundation have campaigned for this for years. In May 2026 they submitted a draft bill to Parliament that would decriminalise sex work and give the people doing it standard workplace protections. As far as I know, it hasn't been voted on yet.

## Safety comes first

Of all the protections, safety at work matters most to me. Some customers aren't kind to the women, and many will simply put up with it. There's basically no reporting.

If a customer is abusive or violent, where does a woman go? When the whole industry is swept under the carpet, that aggressive customer is free to carry on, and eventually he may do something really bad. That could be prevented if the women could share information with each other and talk to the authorities without fear.

## The other side

Not everyone agrees. Some argue that recognising the industry would legitimise exploitation or encourage trafficking, and that the focus should be on helping women leave it. That debate isn't settled anywhere in the world. My view is simply that the people already doing this work deserve to be safe. More in [is Pattaya safe?](/articles/is-pattaya-safe)
