There's a lot of talk at the moment about letting foreigners own more property in Thailand, through 99-year leases or raising the foreign quota in condos from 49% to 75%. Neither has become law yet: the quota is still 49%, and the longest registered lease is still 30 years. I own two property agencies, so this is close to home. My view might surprise people: no to the leases, yes to the condos.

## Why I'm against 99-year leases

I understand why Thailand protects its land. Even though a foreigner on a 99-year lease doesn't technically own the land, for 99 years they effectively do. It's about protecting the country's sovereignty, and honestly, I agree with it.

There have also been ideas in the past about letting foreigners own a small plot, say one rai, enough for a house. I can see that getting messy fast: someone with no intention of ever living here buys a rai because a friend wants two. It would be fantastic for the property market in the short term, but I think it would cause real long-term problems. More in [buying a house in Pattaya](/articles/buying-a-house-in-pattaya).

## Why I'd open up the condo quota

The condo quota is different, and I've never really understood it. Foreigners can only own 49% of the floor space in a building. But a condo goes up into the sky. Why protect a piece of land that already has a huge building on it?

Thai law already lets someone own a building separately from the land it sits on. So the land under a condo could stay Thai-owned, while the units in the sky could be 100% foreign-owned. More in [buying a condo in Pattaya](/articles/buying-a-condo-in-pattaya).

## How I'd do it

Only on new buildings. Changing the quota on existing condos would cause problems. But it's simple to say that any new condo can be 100% foreign-owned.

## What it would do to Pattaya

There'd be a lot more foreign investment, and I think developers would start building bigger units. Families of three or four are often pushed into houses because they need the space. A bigger foreign quota could change the design, away from the small hotel-room-style condos we see so many of now.

## "Thai buyers would be priced out"

That's the main argument against, and I don't really buy it. In a 100% foreign building, foreigners are competing with each other. If Thai buyers can't compete there, developers build other condos aimed at Thai buyers, and there are already plenty of those around the city.

One expensive building doesn't push up prices everywhere else. There are condos at the very top end in Pattaya selling for several times the price per square metre of an ordinary building, and that hasn't dragged up the price of every other building. The economics just don't work that way.

Others would argue that more foreign money would still push up land and building costs across the board, and that housing affordability for locals should come first. It's a fair concern, even if I think the condo market works differently. More in [the Pattaya property market](/articles/pattaya-property-market).
