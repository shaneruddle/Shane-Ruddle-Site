I've lived in Thailand for over 20 years, and I love it here. But there are things about the UK I still miss. Leaving family aside, which is a different article altogether, it comes down to three: the seasons, the convenience food, and the golf.

## The seasons

This one surprises people. I enjoy the weather in Thailand, and it's hot nearly all the time. But I actually miss the seasons.

I don't miss the harsh winters. What I miss is spring and autumn: the colours, the fresh air, the smell of freshly cut grass. Even a bit of cold, now and again. Not for months on end, but a crisp, cold morning is something you just don't get here.

## The food, or rather, the convenience

You can get almost any British food you want in Pattaya. Two friends of mine run expat shops here: Lane's Expat Shop, and the bigger, busier one, The Pantry. You'll find your Cadbury's, your Pot Noodles and everything else.

The catch is the price. It's fairly priced for imported food, but it's a lot more expensive than buying local, so I don't go often.

If I'm honest, though, it's not really British food I miss. It's convenience. I'm a bit of a packet man. I can't cook, and the UK is built for people like me. Walk around London and there's a Pret on every corner, with fresh soups, sandwiches and salads ready to go. Go into a supermarket and the ready meals are endless. And the quality is excellent: a Marks & Spencer microwave meal is in a different league.

It's a bit sad to admit, but I miss packaged food.

## Golf

I came to Thailand as a very keen golfer, and I hardly ever play now.

What I miss is links and heathland courses, and those long summer evenings when you could play late into the night.

Most of all, I miss the club atmosphere. In England, everyone hangs around after the round, chatting at the bar. Here, people shower and go their separate ways. That sense of community just isn't there.

The heat, the caddies, the cost and why I stopped playing are all in [golf in Thailand vs the UK](/articles/golf-in-thailand-vs-the-uk).

## What I don't miss

None of this means I want to go back. I don't miss grey skies for months at a time, or the cost of living. For me, the things I miss are small things, and the life I have here outweighs them.

## My take

If you're thinking of moving to Thailand, expect to miss some odd things. For me, it's not the big things. It's cold mornings, a decent ready meal, and a pint at the club after a round.
