When I first came to Thailand, I wasn't particularly responsible. Everything felt throwaway, and I didn't think much about the consequences of my decisions. Looking back, I took Thailand for granted. Now I'm far more careful, because I know how much I have to lose. ## Taking it for granted

There were a couple of occasions, not many, when I lost my temper or got frustrated with the way things work here. In a slightly different situation, I could have caused myself real problems.

Back then, the authorities weren't doing much about badly behaved foreigners. Now, as I see it, there's a clear push to deport or blacklist people who cause trouble, even when they haven't committed a serious crime. The message is basically: we don't want your kind here. On a bad day, I might once have fallen into that camp.

## The day I lost my temper at immigration

The clearest example was at immigration. I'd given my visa agent my passport well within the time frame, but the visa wasn't processed in time.

When I got to immigration, an officer sent me to a different section. It was the overstay section. I looked at my agent blankly. At the front, I was fined ฿2,000 for overstaying.

She admitted it was her mistake and offered to pay the fine. But I told her she was missing the point. I didn't care about the money. I now had an overstay mark in my passport, after about 15 years of being very careful to make sure that never happened. It was her mistake, and I was the one who looked like I'd done something wrong.

So I had a go at her. I didn't go over the top, because I knew where I was, but I was visibly upset. If an immigration officer had been having a bad day, it could easily have looked like a foreigner, a guest in their country, shouting at a Thai woman in a government office. ## I made her lose face

Looking back, I was lucky. Losing face is a big deal here, and I made her lose face in front of immigration officers. I was within my rights to be upset, but she would have been within hers to fight back, and it could have got out of control very quickly. More in [is Pattaya safe? Yes, unless you poke the bear](/articles/is-pattaya-safe-unless-you-poke-the-bear).

She didn't react. She's still my visa agent and still a friend. I'm sure I lost some of her respect that day, and I think I've earned it back over the years. But that's the point: if you lose control of your emotions here, you lose respect.

## What changed

**Money.** Once you have a bit of money, a fine or an unexpected cost doesn't feel like the end of the world, so there's less to get worked up about.

**Family.** I can't risk anything that might jeopardise my stay, because it isn't just my stay. If I got into trouble and was deported, it would affect my whole family.

**Maturity, and no alcohol.** I'm older, and I haven't had a drink in about 12 years, so I'm more level-headed than I used to be.

**Time.** The longer you live here, the more you shrug things off. Sabai sabai: it's just how it is, and it's not the end of the world. Whether that's maturity or experience, you learn that getting angry or aggressive doesn't get you anywhere in Thailand.

## My advice

If you're new here, learn this early. Being right isn't enough. Keep your cool, especially in a government office, because the consequences of losing it are far bigger than whatever you're upset about.
