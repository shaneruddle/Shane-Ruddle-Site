After 20 years in Pattaya, my honest view is that most of the "scams" people talk about aren't really scams. They're overcharging, misunderstandings or people who weren't well informed. The real ones do exist, though, and they're not always the ones you'd expect.

## Overcharging

The thing that catches most tourists out is being overcharged. It's not nice, but if you pay ฿200 for something that should be ฿100, I'm not sure that's a scam. It's more people taking advantage. If you're not sure what something should cost, check my [fair price checker](/pattaya-prices).

It happens most with taxis. Baht buses on their normal routes have set fares, now ฿15 or ฿20. Go off the route and negotiate with the driver, and you'll usually get a high price to start with that comes down. Markets without price tags work the same way. Haggling is part of the experience of being in Thailand. More in [getting around Pattaya](/articles/getting-around-pattaya).

## The jet ski scam: a rental owner's view

The famous one is the jet ski scam, where the operator claims you damaged it. I've never seen it happen, and I'm actually a bit sympathetic to the operators.

I run a car and bike rental business. We photograph everything, and customers still argue they didn't do the damage when they bring a car or motorbike back. A lot of damage is done without people knowing. You can clip a wall or a kerb and never notice. My staff end up in arguments with customers who think we're scamming them when we have all the evidence, and some of them leave a one-star review saying we did.

It's the same with jet skis. Tourists are excited, they've never ridden one, and they don't check it before they go. They could genuinely have damaged it, then assume they're being scammed because that's what they've read online.

The fix is simple: take photos and a video before you set off, whether it's a jet ski, a bike or a car. It's very hard to scam someone who has evidence. More in [renting a car in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-car-in-pattaya).

## Property: use an agent with an office

Most property problems come from buyers being uninformed and using a freelance agent with no accountability. Often the agent doesn't know what they're doing either. They give advice they believe is right, it turns out not to be, and you're left holding the bag.

I own two agencies, so I'm biased, but my number one tip is to make sure the agent has an office. If they don't, stay well clear. You wouldn't see a dentist in a coffee shop, or buy insurance from someone at their home. Even though walk-in customers are rarer these days, a proper business still has a real presence. Then check them online: a website, a Facebook page, how many followers they have. Look for social proof. More in [buying a condo in Pattaya](/articles/buying-a-condo-in-pattaya).

## Romance and the sick buffalo

Romance scams happen here too, including the famous "sick buffalo" story. But when an older man is with a younger woman and she starts asking for money, I'm not sure it's always a scam. Often she's leaning on the relationship: she's in it for security, and now she's asking for it. More in [relationships in Pattaya](/articles/relationships-in-pattaya).

## Business deals that go wrong

I see people lose money in businesses here all the time. But again, it's rarely a scam. Most people go into business with good intentions and are simply unaware of the downside. Later it can look like a scam, but usually it's people being misinformed. More in [starting or buying a business in Pattaya](/articles/starting-or-buying-a-business-in-pattaya).

## The real scam: borrowing money

The genuine scam I see most is foreigners who want to stay here but don't have the money or the ability to earn it. They borrow from everyone they can with no intention of paying it back. When it finally comes undone, or they leave the country, it turns out they've borrowed from half the town. It happens more often than you'd think.

I've been caught out myself a few times, including by [people who worked for me](/articles/hiring-and-firing-in-pattaya), which is why I was more sympathetic. I've also had customers at Pattaya Rent a Bike get into financial trouble and have the bike they rented from me taken off them, then be unable to pay for it. These days I take it on the chin as a cost of doing business. That's true anywhere, but the difference here is that people leave the country and you never hear from them again.

So now I don't lend money. I'm honest about it. I tell people I've been asked before, I've lent before, and because of how that went, I'm not going to lend to them.

## My advice: don't believe everything you hear

People come and go in Pattaya, and a lot of stories get told. Many people arrive with a past nobody knows about, so it's easy to reinvent yourself. I think it starts in the bars, telling stories to Thais, who tend to be trusting, and it carries over when they meet other foreigners. Most of the stories are harmless, but some aren't. Just don't believe everything you hear.
