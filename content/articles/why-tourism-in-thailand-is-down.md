We're heading into the middle of November, and it still feels like low season. Tourism in Thailand is down, and you can see it on the ground. My businesses are holding up because they're well established, but I wouldn't want to be a new business right now.

The numbers back it up. Foreign arrivals this year are down around 4% so far, after 2025 was already down about 7% on 2024. So we're roughly 10% below where we were two years ago.

## Why it's down

I think it's a mix of things:

- **Flights.** Some airlines still haven't brought back their routes to Thailand since the pandemic. I think some are wary because the government keeps changing its mind on visas.
- **Cost.** Fuel prices have pushed up long-haul fares, and the baht is strong against many currencies, or other currencies are weak. Either way, Thailand is a bit more expensive than it used to be.
- **Uncertainty.** Above all, there's a lot of uncertainty in the world right now, and it's not a time when people rush to book a long-haul holiday.

## The government doesn't help itself

The visa confusion is a big part of it. The rules on how long you can stay and how often you can visit seem to change all the time. People plan trips six months or even a year ahead, especially families booking around the school holidays. They want clarity, and Thailand keeps flip-flopping.

I know how small things can tip a decision. I had tickets to four World Cup games in America this year, but I didn't go. I was already 50-50, and I didn't like the idea of having my social media checked at the border. Much of what was said about it was probably overblown, but it was enough to push me over the edge.

Now picture a family of four round the dinner table with half a dozen holiday options. One of them is Thailand, and someone says, "We don't know how long they'll let us stay, and it doesn't feel like we'd be very welcome." They'll just go somewhere else. The timing, execution and messaging are often terrible. I don't say that lightly. Sometimes it feels like a bad joke. Right now there's talk of a ฿300 fee on arrival, and of a charge on departure too. For me the amount is small in the grand scheme of things, but think about the timing. Tourism is down, Thailand is falling behind its neighbours, the world is in turmoil, and this is when they choose to talk about extra charges. It doesn't look good when you're trying to attract people.

The messages are mixed, too. One minute the government says it doesn't want mass tourism, it wants "quality" tourists who spend more. A few months later it's celebrating headline arrival numbers. It feels like the left hand doesn't know what the right hand is doing. There doesn't seem to be a long-term strategy, and if there is one, it keeps changing.

## The neighbours have caught up

For about 20 years Thailand had it easy. Vietnam was very underdeveloped and wasn't on most people's radar. In the last ten years it has developed a lot, and now it is. I've been half a dozen times, and in my opinion it doesn't compare to Thailand, but that's just me.

Japan has become cheap because of its economy, and it has a huge amount of culture and things to do, plus things Thailand can't offer, like skiing. I've skied there a couple of times and it's exceptional.

People also forget China. I've been a few times this year, and it's fantastic: very safe and relatively inexpensive. I suspect many Chinese tourists are now travelling within China instead of abroad, and I don't blame them.

## What I'd do if I ran tourism

Play to Thailand's strengths: the culture, the beaches, Muay Thai, the food and wellness. Double down on what Thailand uniquely offers.

I'd also run campaigns on safety, because Thailand is extremely safe, certainly compared with many countries. It gets bad press that I don't think it deserves. My personal view is that some of it comes from other governments that would rather their citizens holidayed at home. During the border clashes with Cambodia, the fighting was in a very small area right on the border, yet some news outlets made it sound as if the whole country had become dangerous. ## My advice to business owners: use the quiet time

Ups and downs are normal, even in a good year. Ride out the lows, aim to break even, make the most of the high season, and save some of that money for the next quiet spell.

Years ago I bumped into another business owner who was also very quiet. He told me he quite enjoyed it, because it gave him time to do all the jobs in his business he never usually had time for.
