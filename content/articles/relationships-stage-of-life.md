I'm in a fairly unusual position. I've had a long-term relationship with a partner who was educated in the West, and I've also settled down with Thai partners, before and since. So I've seen both cultures up close.

I'll be honest: I can't always tell how much of the difference was the person and how much was the culture. Probably a bit of both.

## The difference I noticed

With my Thai partner, I have a lot more fun. We laugh a lot more, and day to day things are more light-hearted. We do have deeper conversations, but most of the time it's relaxed.

In the Western relationship, things were a bit more serious. The conversations tended to be more in-depth, about business, money and plans.

## Neither is better

I don't think anyone can say one suits them better than the other. It comes down to your stage of life.

When you're young, you might want fun and nothing too serious. When you start getting your life together, thinking about a mortgage or children, you probably want someone who'll have those more serious conversations with you. Later in life, perhaps when you've done all that and come here to retire, you may not want that any more.

So it isn't really about preferring one culture. It's about where you are in your life.

## The other side

People are individuals first. Plenty of Thai couples talk business and money every day, and plenty of Western relationships are full of laughter. Don't choose a partner based on a stereotype. Choose based on the person, and on what you need at this point in your life.

## My take

Before you ask which culture suits you, ask what stage of life you're in and what you actually want from a partner right now.
