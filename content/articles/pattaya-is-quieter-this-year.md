Pattaya is quieter this year, and the numbers in my own businesses show it. The city's answer is more events. They're great for a few days, but I don't think they're the long-term fix. More in [why tourism in Thailand is down](/articles/why-tourism-in-thailand-is-down).

## What the mayor said

Pattaya's mayor has acknowledged that tourist numbers are slightly down, and put it mainly down to global economic conditions. Operators in the nightlife areas say it's quieter and people are spending less. The city's response is a run of high-season events, including the Pattaya International Fireworks Festival at the end of November.

## What my numbers say

We went through our figures last week. In central Pattaya, we're down about 10% on last year. In Jomtien it's better, slightly up on last year. But overall, this is the quietest it's felt in the four or five years since the pandemic. The numbers don't lie, and on the street it definitely feels quieter too.

## Events are a short-term fix

Festivals bring a big crowd for a few days, and I'm all for them. But they don't change the bigger picture.

Pattaya follows the trend for Thailand, and Thailand's tourism is down. A lot of that is beyond the city's control. People around the world are struggling, disposable income is falling, and a holiday in Pattaya is something people can do without. When money is tight, they travel locally instead of long haul.

Pattaya has survived plenty of downturns before, mainly because its visitors come from so many different countries and backgrounds. I expect it to survive this one too.

## Thailand isn't helping itself

Where I think Thailand could do better is in how it delivers its news. Over the last year there's been a run of headlines aimed mainly at foreigners:

- Visa-free stays cut from 60 days to 30 for most nationalities, from September
- A planned tourist entry fee for foreign visitors
- The [nominee company crackdown](/articles/nominee-companies-in-thailand)
- New deportation rules for foreigners who cause trouble, in force since August

Individually, most of these make sense, and some are long overdue. Take the tourist fee. It isn't being collected yet, but if you read past the headline, the money is meant to pay for insurance for visitors and for looking after tourist sites. That's reasonable.

But most people don't read the article. They see the headline. Collectively, and with poor timing, it can feel as though Thailand is trying to deter tourists rather than attract them. ## The other side

The city can fairly argue that events do work. They bring in big crowds and spending, and they keep Pattaya in the news for the right reasons. And the government would say these policies protect Thailand's long-term interests, even if the short-term headlines aren't pretty.

I'd just like to see the good reasons explained as loudly as the rules themselves. More in [where Pattaya is heading](/articles/where-pattaya-is-heading) and [Pattaya's high season is getting shorter](/articles/pattayas-high-season-is-getting-shorter).
