Over the years, I've heard it a hundred times. I'll get talking to another business owner, often someone retired who did well back home, and ask why they bought their restaurant or bar. The answer is usually the same: "It was just something to do."

Maybe it's genuine. Maybe it's a bit of a defence in case it doesn't work out. Either way, it's the wrong mindset for going into business.

## Why "something to do" doesn't work

From what I've seen, these businesses rarely work. Even when they have a real chance, going in with the mindset that it's just something to do is not a good start. Running a business here is hard work, and it needs someone who really needs it to succeed.

## What I'd do instead

This might sound biased, but I'd back somebody younger who actually wants to make a living and make it work.

You can still get a sense of ownership, a sense of purpose, and even something to do, without all of the doing. There are people here who want to build something and don't have the money. Capable ones aren't easy to find, but they're out there.

Find someone you trust who's capable, and invest in them. That way you're dipping your toe in rather than going all the way. You still get what you were after: an interest here, somewhere to go, being able to call yourself the boss, and a chance to add ideas and keep your brain going.

## The other side

Backing someone else has its own risks. You're trusting another person with your money, so get the agreement in writing, understand the company structure, and make sure it complies with Thai foreign ownership rules. Never rely on a nominee arrangement. I've written more about that in [nominee companies in Thailand](/articles/nominee-companies-in-thailand).

*This is my own experience, not legal or financial advice.*

## My take

If you want something to do, take up a hobby. If you want to invest in a business, back someone who needs it to work.
