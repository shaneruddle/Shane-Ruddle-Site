I charge a credit card surcharge at my car rental business, but not in my restaurants. A friend who owns a restaurant asked me about it recently, and my advice was to drop his. Here's why the same fee works in one business and backfires in another.

## My friend's surcharge

My friend had just started adding a surcharge for customers paying by card. In the first few days, one customer refused to pay it. Another paid, but said he'd never come back.

He asked what we do at Hemingways and Cajun Life Cafe. The answer is: we don't charge.

## Why we do charge at Pattaya Rent a Car

At Pattaya Rent a Car we do add a card fee, and I decided that from the start. The difference is that nobody is surprised by it:

- **They have options.** Customers can pay by cash, QR scan or bank transfer with no charge.
- **It's planned in advance.** Nobody walks in unaware. It's on our website, in our terms and conditions, and in the email confirmation.
- **The amounts are big.** A monthly rental on a Toyota Fortuner can be around ฿50,000. I always felt it was unfair for us to pay around 3% in card fees for the customer's convenience, when PromptPay, QR scan and bank transfer are just as convenient these days.

So at the car rental, the surcharge is really a gentle deterrent that nudges people towards other ways of paying. More in [renting a car in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-car-in-pattaya).

## Why restaurants are different

In a restaurant, the transactions are much smaller. More importantly, the surcharge usually comes as a surprise when the bill arrives. Most diners won't know about it until that moment.

It leaves a slightly different taste in the mouth, if you'll excuse the pun, than it does at a car rental.

## Weigh it against your customers

When my friend went through his numbers, the surcharge only brought in about ฿5,000 to ฿6,000 a month. That's small. He'd already upset two customers in a few days. If that grew to 15 or 20 customers, it wouldn't be worth it.

He told me why he'd done it: margins are tight, and he's being squeezed by rising costs for raw materials and staff. He was looking at every cost he could. I understand that completely.

## What I told him

My advice was to drop the surcharge and look at it a different way. Creep some prices up slightly to offset the costs and claw back that ฿5,000 in a way customers are unlikely to notice, rather than something that's obvious on every bill.

The lesson for any business: a fee isn't just about the money. It's about whether the customer expects it. If they do, it's fine. If it surprises them, it can cost you far more than it brings in.
