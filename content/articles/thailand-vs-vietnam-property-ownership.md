Most people assume Vietnam is more open to foreigners than Thailand. When it comes to owning a business, that's often true. But when it comes to property, the picture is more mixed than you'd think. Thailand is better for condos. Vietnam is better for houses, at least for now.

## Condos

**Thailand:** foreigners can own up to 49% of the floor space in a condo building. It's freehold, in your own name, with no time limit.

**Vietnam:** foreigners can own up to 30% of the apartments in a building. Ownership lasts 50 years from the date the ownership certificate (the "Pink Book") is issued, and can be extended once for up to another 50 years. So that's effectively 100 years, not forever.

**Winner: Thailand.** A bigger quota, and the unit is yours outright.

## Houses

**Thailand:** foreigners can't own land. You can own the house itself, but the land underneath is usually leased, typically for 30 years.

**Vietnam:** foreigners can own a house in an approved housing project for the same 50 years, plus one 50-year extension. The catch is a cap: no more than 250 foreign-owned houses in each ward, which is a small local district.

In both countries you own the home, not the land itself.

**Winner: Vietnam.** It has a clear, legal route to owning a house in your own name. One caveat: in September 2026 Vietnam published a draft housing law that would stop foreigners buying houses in future, leaving only apartments. Existing owners would keep theirs. It's only a draft, but it's worth knowing.

## At a glance

| | Thailand | Vietnam |
|---|---|---|
| Condo quota | 49% of floor space | 30% of units |
| Condo term | Freehold, no limit | 50 years + one 50-year extension |
| Houses | Lease the land, usually 30 years | 50 years + one extension (draft law would end this) |
| House cap | Not applicable | 250 per ward |
| Land | Can't own | Can't own |

## What Thailand could learn

What I like about Vietnam's approach is that it gives foreigners a proper way to own a house while still controlling how many they can own. Thailand already does something similar with condos through the 49% quota.

Why not apply the same idea to housing developments here? I've written about that in [a 49% quota for villas](/articles/a-49-percent-quota-for-villas).

*I'm not a lawyer. This is a general comparison, not legal advice. Rules in both countries change, so check the current position before you buy.*

## My take

If you want a condo, Thailand is the better bet. If you want a house in your own name, Vietnam has the better system. Neither country lets you own the land, but Vietnam has found a way to make house ownership work for foreigners, and Thailand should take note.
