I've hired a lot of people over 20 years in Pattaya. Some have been brilliant, and some I'd rather forget. My rule now is simple: hire slow, fire fast.

## One of my best hires

One of the best hires I've made was an early hire at Pattaya Rent a Car. When she joined, she didn't speak much English and had no experience in car rental or much experience dealing with foreigners. Her background was as a restaurant supervisor.

What she did have was an open mind. She was adaptable and very willing to learn. It didn't always go smoothly, and she didn't always understand my way of doing things, but she learned. She joined as an employee, and for the last four or five years she's run Pattaya Rent a Car pretty much on her own.

The lesson: attitude beats experience. More in [managing Thai staff](/articles/managing-thai-staff).

## The chef I gave everything to

I regret plenty of hires, but two stand out. The first was a chef. We gave him and another candidate a cooking test. He could clearly cook, and the other one was clearly winging it, so we hired him.

In the interview he told us, through a translator, that he'd spent the last of his money the night before on a room by the day. He had nowhere to stay, no money for food and no motorbike, and he desperately needed the job. So I gave him the job and a room above the business. One of my staff gave him ฿500 for food. We gave him a motorbike, and I even gave him my spare phone.

He took advantage of all of it. He lasted a couple of months. He moved his girlfriend in, who also became a problem, and in the end we had to get the police involved over drugs on the premises. The only thing he was still good at was cooking.

## The manager who talked a good game

The second was a foreigner I hired as a restaurant and bar manager. He interviewed brilliantly and told me everything I wanted to hear. He had some experience, but it was more as a social front-of-house type, chatting with customers, and the role needed much more than that.

He was OK at the job, but I soon found out he had a gambling problem, and it overshadowed everything. He ended up stealing from me and borrowing from other people without paying them back. Given what I'd expected from him, he was the worst hire I could have made. More on that kind of borrowing in [scams in Pattaya](/articles/scams-in-pattaya).

## Firing

Firing has never been a problem for me, because everyone I've fired deserved it. I'm understanding and I have a lot of patience, so if it gets to that point, I don't hesitate.

Most of the time I stay friendly with the people I let go. I've even rehired a few once they'd matured, and given them a second chance. Not the manager, though. He was the exception.

## My rule: hire slow, fire fast

Take your time hiring, and don't be won over by a good interview. When it's clearly not working, act quickly. And don't hire someone hoping they'll change; it's [one mistake I'd never make again](/articles/things-id-never-do-again-in-business).
