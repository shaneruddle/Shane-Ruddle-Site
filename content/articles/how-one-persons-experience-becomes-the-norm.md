When someone has an experience in Thailand, they often assume it's normal. Then they tell everyone it's normal. Before long, one person's story has turned into "everybody knows". My advice is simple: take what you hear with a pinch of salt, and check who you're hearing it from.

## "Everybody pays the police"

Not long ago, someone on holiday was asking a man who lives here about paying the police. He told them it was perfectly normal, and that every business pays the police.

That's simply not true. He was speaking from his own experience, and that's fine as far as it goes. But the holidaymaker went home believing every business in Thailand pays the police, and they'll probably repeat it to everyone they know.

That's just one example. The point is how quickly one person's experience gets passed on as fact.

## "You can't make money in Thailand"

This is one I hear all the time. Someone tried to make money here, it didn't work out, and their conclusion is that nobody can. So whenever anyone asks about buying or starting a business, the answer comes back: don't do it, you can't make money here, you're a fool.

I've been running businesses here for over 20 years. It's not easy, and I've had plenty of failures (I wrote about them in [my business failures](/articles/my-business-failures)). But you can make money in Thailand.

## The woman who ignored the comments

Recently I saw a Facebook post from a woman who, a few months ago, had asked about job opportunities here. Back then, the comments told her she was wasting her time.

She took it to heart, but she kept trying anyway. Now she's found a job, her work permit has come through, and so has her visa. Her post was basically telling everyone they'd been wrong.

To be fair, sometimes it is genuinely hard, and people do need to hear that. Getting a job here isn't easy. But "it was hard for me" isn't the same as "it's impossible for you".

## Length of time isn't the same as experience

You often hear "listen to him, he's lived here 20 years". I've written before about [who you should take Thailand advice from](/articles/who-should-you-take-thailand-advice-from).

Plenty of people have been here a long time but have never worked alongside Thais, never started a business, never been through the things they're giving advice on. That doesn't stop them giving it.

If someone asked me about their swimming technique, I'd tell them I know nothing about swimming and to go and ask a professional. The same should apply here.

## How to tell good advice from bad

- **Look at the person's credentials.** Have they actually done the thing they're advising you on?
- **Push back.** Don't be shy about asking, "Have you had that experience yourself? Can you tell me about it?"
- **Verify.** Check what you're told with other people and other sources.

You'll often find the answer is, "Well, no, I haven't done it myself, but I've heard..."

## My take

Whether it's starting a business, looking for a job, or starting a relationship with a Thai partner, don't let other people's experiences put you off. Listen, by all means. Just make sure the person you're listening to has actually been there.
