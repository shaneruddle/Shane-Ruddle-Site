For as long as I've lived here, Thailand has been the obvious choice for a holiday in Southeast Asia. That's changing. Vietnam is catching up fast, and I think for the first time in a long time, Thailand has some real competition.

## The numbers

Thailand is still ahead overall. It had about 26.8 million foreign arrivals between January and mid-November 2026, while Vietnam had about 19.5 million in the first ten months. But the gap is closing, and Vietnam's numbers are rising while Thailand's have dipped.

The Russian market shows it best. In the first nine months of 2026, Vietnam welcomed around 1.13 million Russians, more than two and a half times as many as the year before. Thailand had about 1.5 million Russians up to mid-November, so the two are now in the same league. Reports suggest that in some months this summer, Vietnam actually received more Russian visitors than Thailand.

Vietnam is also developing fast, and it comes across as very welcoming to foreigners.

## Visas that change like the wind

Thailand hasn't helped itself. Since September, visa-free stays for many countries have been cut from 60 days to 30. And the rules are always confusing, and they seem to change constantly. (More in [Thai visas in Pattaya](/articles/thai-visas-in-pattaya).)

Remember, a lot of people plan their holidays six months in advance. Imagine booking your trip to Thailand, then finding out the visa rules have changed by the time you arrive.

Vietnam, meanwhile, offers a 45-day visa-free stay to many nationalities and a 90-day e-visa.

## But really, it's money

Put the visas and the negative press to one side. I think the real reason is simply cost. Thailand is now more expensive than Vietnam, and for a lot of holidaymakers it comes down to that.

## What Thailand should do

Thailand can't control its currency, so it can't just make itself cheaper. What it can do is raise its standards, so that if it's more expensive, visitors clearly get more for their money.

The thing is, you already do get more for your money here. Thailand just doesn't do a good job of promoting it.

And it needs to be a lot more welcoming to foreigners. Not just to tourists, but to the people who come back year after year, and the ones who end up living and investing here.

## The other side

Thailand still attracts millions more visitors overall. Some of Vietnam's growth comes from new charter flights and from starting at a lower base. And plenty of tourists who try Vietnam will still come back to Thailand.

## My take

Thailand has had it easy for a long time. Vietnam is cheaper, easier to get into and keen to welcome visitors. Thailand needs to compete, and that starts with making people feel welcome.
