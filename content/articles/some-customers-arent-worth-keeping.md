Recently, my manager at Cajun Life Cafe had to ask a customer to leave, and warned me we might get a bad review for it. My answer was simple: I back her completely. Some behaviour crosses a line, and no review is worth putting up with it.

## What happened

The customer had been in a couple of times before, and the staff had already noticed his behaviour. On his third visit, my manager was there. He was spitting into a handkerchief, and, she told me, a bit on the floor and the table as well.

In a restaurant, that's a massive no-no. So she politely asked him to leave. Unsurprisingly, he didn't like it.

By coincidence, another of my managers had been in earlier that day and had noticed him too. His view was simple: you just don't need people like that in your business.

We don't know what was going on with him, and it's not our place to guess. But whatever the reason, a restaurant has to put its other customers, its staff and basic hygiene first.

## Always back your manager

When a manager makes a call like this, the worst thing an owner can do is second-guess them. She was there, I wasn't. She saw what was happening, handled it politely, and told me straight away. That's exactly what I want.

If staff think you won't support them, they'll start putting up with things they shouldn't, and your other customers will pay the price.

## Don't fear the bad review

Yes, he might leave a one-star review. That's the risk. But one bad review from someone asked to leave for spitting in a restaurant isn't going to sink a business. If you reply calmly and factually, most readers will see it for what it is. (More in [how I handle an unfair one-star review](/articles/how-i-handle-an-unfair-one-star-review).)

What would hurt far more is other customers watching someone spit at the next table and seeing us do nothing.

## Where to draw the line

Most customers are lovely, and most problems are small: food that isn't hot enough, a wrong order. Those need patience. But anything that affects hygiene, safety or other customers' comfort is different. Deal with it politely, deal with it quickly, and don't apologise for it.

For me, the real line is other customers. A difficult customer who makes my staff's job harder is one thing, and we can usually manage that. But when someone starts spoiling the meal for everyone else in the room, they've crossed it. Our other customers came to relax and enjoy themselves, and protecting that matters more than keeping one person happy.
