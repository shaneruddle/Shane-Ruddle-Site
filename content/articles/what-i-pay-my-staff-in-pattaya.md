When people find out I own businesses here, one question comes up again and again: what do you pay your staff? When I tell them, some are shocked. "How can they live on that? How is that right?" My answer is simple. That's the going rate here. You can't pay staff based on what they'd earn in another country.

## What staff actually earn

These are rough figures across my businesses.

- **Starting out, little responsibility:** around ฿12,000 to ฿15,000 a month. For a new waiter or waitress, that's close to the minimum wage.
- **More responsibility, or long service:** around ฿25,000 a month.
- **Management roles:** ฿30,000 to ฿40,000 a month, sometimes more.

We don't set out to pay the minimum. It's simply where inexperienced staff start in this kind of work. We raise it after probation, and again with years of service, so the starting rate only really applies to people who are new.

## Tips and meals

In our restaurants, staff also share the tips. At Hemingways, that comes to around ฿150 a day each. It's paid out daily, so most staff live on their tips day to day and use their salary for fixed costs: rent, a motorbike, and often money they send home to family. (More on how that works in [tipping in Pattaya](/articles/tipping-in-pattaya).)

Most of our staff also get free meals, even at Pattaya Rent a Car, which isn't a restaurant. Food here is inexpensive, so it's an easy way to help.

## Why you can't compare it to the West

A monthly wage of ฿15,000 sounds tiny if you convert it into pounds or dollars. But it isn't being spent in London or New York. Rent, food and transport here cost a fraction of what they do back home.

Businesses here also charge local prices. A meal, a car rental or a beer costs what the local market will bear. If I paid Western wages, my prices would have to be Western too, and I wouldn't have any customers, or any staff, for long.

Wages are set by the local market, like anywhere else. Paying what's fair here, treating people well, and rewarding loyalty and responsibility is what matters.

## My take

I understand why the numbers surprise people. But the right question isn't "would I live on that back home?" It's "is this fair for here, and is it a good job by local standards?" That's the standard I try to hold myself to.
