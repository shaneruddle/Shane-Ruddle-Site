When someone asks me whether to buy off-plan or resale, my first answer is a question: what's it for? Living in it and investing in it are very different paths. But if it's purely an investment, I'd lean towards resale. More in [buying a condo in Pattaya](/articles/buying-a-condo-in-pattaya).

## Why resale for a pure investment

With a resale condo, everything is in front of you. You can see what it really costs to own, not just the asking price, and what it rents for. There's no speculation. You know exactly what your return will be. More in [renting out your condo in Pattaya](/articles/renting-out-your-condo-in-pattaya).

## How people make money off-plan

The big attraction of off-plan is that you don't need all the money up front. Here's how investors play it:

- **Get in early.** Every building has good units and bad units. Higher floors beat lower ones, and sea views, mountain views and spots near the pool or gym all matter. Early buyers get the pick.
- **Put down around 20%.** Developers usually raise prices as the early demand gets used up, so the earliest buyers pay least.
- **Sell the contract before completion.** You often have three or four years while it's built. The developer typically charges a fee of around ฿50,000 to ฿100,000 to transfer the contract to a new buyer.

With a good developer, you could in theory make a few million baht having put down only a few million. That's doubling your money.

## The risks

It's speculative. You're buying something you can't see yet, and you're betting that demand rises, both for that building and for condos in general over three or four years. Even with a great developer, if the overall market drops, you won't make money. More in [the Pattaya property market](/articles/pattaya-property-market).

In my view, as long as the building actually gets built, most people at least get out flat. But you may have had a few million baht tied up for years, and there's commission to pay on both sides when you sell.

## Delays and projects that never happen

Some buildings never get built at all. More commonly, they're delayed. That's not always bad: it gives you more time to find a buyer or raise the money for the final balloon payment.

But projects do fail. The best-known example here is the Waterfront tower on Beach Road, which has stood unfinished for years, tied up in legal disputes, while buyers fought in court to get their money back. There are others.

## Pick a developer with a track record

Go with a reputable developer that has several finished projects behind it, such as the Riviera Group, Global Top Group or Raimon Land. A developer with no history is more likely to run late or not finish at all.
