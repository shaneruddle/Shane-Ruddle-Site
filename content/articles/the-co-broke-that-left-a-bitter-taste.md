Pattaya's property agencies work together all the time, and it usually works well. But once in a while it goes wrong. In five or six years of owning my property agencies, this is the only time another agency has cut us out of a deal, and it still leaves a bitter taste. ## How a co-broke works

If a buyer wants something we don't have, we contact other agencies. If one of them has the right property, we show it to the buyer together. That's a co-broke. One agency brings the buyer, the other brings the property, and the standard arrangement is to split the commission 50/50. It isn't written down anywhere, but it's an unwritten rule among agencies and agents here.

## The buyer who knew exactly what he wanted

A buyer came into one of our offices knowing exactly which development he wanted, and exactly what type of unit. We didn't have one available, so we contacted other agencies. One did, so we viewed it together with the buyer.

He liked it, but he wasn't ready. His circumstances meant he needed to wait, so the deal stalled.

## A few months later

A few months later, we found out he'd bought that exact unit, through the other agency. Their story was that he'd walked in to ask about a completely different property, and after viewing it, decided to go back to the original one.

Our view was simple. He was our client. He wouldn't have known about that unit without us, and he bought the very one we'd shown him. We felt we were entitled to our share.

There was some back and forth. In the end, the other agency agreed to pay us a small token commission, nowhere near 50%.

## Why it stung

I know the owner of that agency, and his girlfriend too, so I was surprised. I think in his head he didn't feel he was doing anything wrong. I'm not so sure. But when hundreds of thousands of baht are at stake, people can talk themselves into almost anything to justify what they're doing.

## The lesson

We don't put co-brokes in writing, because it almost never goes wrong, and most of us know each other. Generally, business owners here still help each other out. But when a significant amount of money is involved, it can be a different story.
