After more than 20 years here, my family, my friends and I have used Thai private hospitals a lot. Overall, I can't fault the care. But what really stands out isn't just the medical side. It's the customer service.

## Walk in and be seen

The thing I still find incredible is that you can walk into a hospital at most times of the day and be seen. Whether it's the dentist, an eye test like the one I had recently, or a general check-up, nine times out of ten I've gone in to book an appointment and been told, "We can see you right now."

I'm not talking about anything serious here, just routine check-ups. Yes, you pay, but it's relatively inexpensive. In my experience, a typical visit costs around 2,000 to 3,000 baht, which I think is very reasonable for that level of service.

## It's not all sunshine and roses

I've had a couple of experiences that left a bitter taste.

Once, I went in just to have my ears cleaned out, because I was struggling to hear. I'd had it done before and knew exactly what was involved. They still wanted to check my blood pressure, my weight and everything else, and charge me for it. I argued that it was unnecessary. These days I just accept that sort of thing as par for the course, and if it's 800 baht for my vitals, so be it. But years ago, when 800 baht was a lot of money to me, it felt like a waste.

Another time, I had food poisoning and called an ambulance because I was in a bad way. Then I hit one of those windows where you feel better for a while, so we cancelled it quickly and drove to the hospital ourselves. They still charged me for the ambulance call-out. I stayed overnight and was charged for that too. That one felt a bit like being exploited.

## Where it really counts

But when I think about the birth of my children, the minor surgery my kids have had, and stitches after a fall, I can't fault the care. When it's really mattered, the hospitals here have been excellent.

## Excellent doctors and specialists

I've had full health check-ups here, where you move smoothly from blood tests to scans to the doctor, and I've had routine screening done too. The experience has always been friendly and efficient. I've had plenty of dental work done, including veneers, which I'm very happy with.

People who don't live here often assume the best doctors are in the West. That definitely hasn't been my experience, or the experience of people I know. There are some really good doctors and specialists here.

## Consider coming here for treatment

If you live in the West and face months on a waiting list, or you're paying privately anyway, consider coming to Thailand. Plenty of people do. Medical tourism is big here.

It might not be quite as cheap as somewhere like Turkey, but in my experience it's cheaper than the West. And if you think lower prices mean lower quality, that hasn't been the case for me.

## My advice

Expect to pay, and expect the odd extra charge you might not think is necessary. If something seems unnecessary, ask before they do it. And make sure you have good health insurance.

*This is my own experience, not medical advice.*

## My take

The occasional over-the-top charge is a small price for being seen the same day by well-qualified doctors. I'd choose it every time.
