Over the years, I've heard it many times, and not just from people in business: in Thailand, if you're the nice guy, or you're kind to people, especially your staff, it'll be taken as weakness.

## There's some truth in it

I do think there's something in it. You can't be overly kind and overly friendly, because that isn't what business needs. As a leader, there will be times when you have to make hard decisions, and there won't always be room to be friendly.

## But kindness isn't weakness

Where people go wrong is mixing up kindness with weakness. I think it's the opposite. You can absolutely be kind and strong at the same time. They aren't opposites.

## What that looks like in practice

Say you have a member of staff who can't do the job. Someone who's being "kind" might just keep them on, because they don't want the difficult conversation or to disappoint them.

In reality, that isn't doing them any favours. You're almost enabling them to stay in a role that doesn't suit them.

The kind thing is to be compassionate when you let them go, and strong enough to actually do it. More on that in [how I let staff go in Thailand](/articles/how-i-let-staff-go-in-thailand).

## It comes down to respect

There's a related myth: that to earn your staff's respect, you have to be tough. I don't believe that either. There are plenty of business people around the world who are genuinely kind and still very successful, and I think it's because their staff respect them, not because they fear them.

If you believe kindness is weakness, then by that logic being strong means being unkind. And if you're unkind, you'll slowly lose your staff's respect. That's not a good place to be.

Be kind, earn respect, and you'll be fine.

## My take

Kindness doesn't mean avoiding hard decisions. It means making them, and treating people well while you do.
