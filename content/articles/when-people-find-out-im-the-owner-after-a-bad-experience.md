One of the more awkward parts of owning several businesses is the moment someone realises I own the place where they had a bad experience. It's a reflection on me, so it stings a little. But with around 120 staff, things are going to go wrong, and I've learned to shrug most of it off. ## The polite silence

It happened again recently. After padel, a few of the guys arranged to meet at one of my bars. They were all singing its praises, saying they go there often and really like it. One of them asked if I was the owner. I said yes, and he said well done.

Then he asked if I also owned one of my other venues. I said yes, and he went quiet. I could tell from his reaction that he hadn't had a good time there. I asked if he'd been, and he politely said he had, but he preferred the first one. That was his polite way of telling me.

I left it there. Other people were around, I don't know him that well, and I didn't want to put him on the spot. So I smiled and changed the subject.

## It happens most weeks

That wasn't a one-off. In the space of two days:

- A friend emailed one of my property agencies, upset about the service he'd received. He didn't know I was now the owner. I reached out to him privately and apologised.
- Someone else phoned me about a bad experience at one of my businesses.
- Another business owner had asked my staff to list his property, and nobody got back to him. I ended up getting involved. He thanked me for listing it, and made it clear my staff hadn't done a very good job.

That's just two days. It happens on a weekly basis.

## Restaurants can't get it right every time

Especially in the restaurant game, nobody gets it right all the time. It's impossible. So meeting people and finding out they had a bad night at one of my places is just part of being in business. And at least we business owners are having a go.

## Smile, don't apologise, gather the facts

I almost always smile and change the subject. I don't usually apologise, unless the person is clearly looking for an apology, because I don't know all the facts. If something needs chasing up, I gather the facts first.

A lot of the time, people don't actually want anything to change. They're telling me to help me out, as constructive feedback. That's appreciated, even if it's still a little embarrassing.

## My advice to other owners: take it with a pinch of salt

If you have multiple businesses or a lot of staff, you have to shrug a lot of it off. You can't react to every bit of feedback, and you certainly can't overreact.

Food in particular is very individual. Ten people can order exactly the same meal, and two of them might not like it. Maybe they chose the wrong dish, didn't realise what they were ordering, or something else was affecting how it tasted. So take a lot of feedback, not all of it, with a pinch of salt.

I'd like to think people who've been here a while understand that I can't possibly know everything that goes on in my businesses, or be responsible for everyone's actions.
