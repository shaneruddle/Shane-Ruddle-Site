I've been to China twice, to different parts of the country, and I really enjoyed it. It's far more organised and much cleaner than Thailand. But every time, I'm grateful to get back to Thailand. For me it comes down to the people and the food. More in [living in Pattaya](/articles/living-in-pattaya).

## Where China wins

China is more organised and a lot cleaner. And because it's such a huge country, with very different climates, you get a lot of variety in what you can see and do.

## Where Thailand wins

**Communication.** The Chinese people I met were very nice, but most don't speak English, so communicating is hard. In Thailand, I find most people speak some English and you can get by.

**Food.** In a Chinese shopping centre, as a Westerner, I'd struggle to find somewhere to eat. In Thailand that's never a problem. More in [Thai food for newcomers](/articles/thai-food-for-newcomers).

## Where to go

- **Fenghuang.** A beautiful old town in western Hunan, full of history. I'd definitely recommend it, and I'd go back.
- **Zhangjiajie.** Also in Hunan. The stone pillars rising out of the ground look like something from the film Avatar. It's spectacular. We walked across the glass bridge at what they call the Grand Canyon of China.

## Practical tips

- **Visas.** British passport holders can now visit China visa-free for up to 30 days, which is one reason I went. Check the rules for your own nationality before you travel.
- **Alipay is a must.** Everything runs on it: the metro, taxis, 7-Eleven. You barely need cash. Set it up before you go if you can, and link a couple of cards in case one fails. Each person needs their own account, because you tap your phone to get through the metro gates.
- **Internet.** We had problems until we bought an eSIM through Trip.com, which worked well and was fast. Sites like YouTube, Facebook and Instagram are blocked in China, so to use them you'll need a VPN or a roaming package from home, which is what I used.
