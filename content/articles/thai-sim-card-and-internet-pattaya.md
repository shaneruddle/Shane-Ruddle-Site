The first thing to sort out when you move to Pattaya is a Thai SIM and phone number, registered in your own name with your passport. A tourist SIM from the airport is fine for a holiday, but if you're staying, you'll want a proper number, because your bank, your visa agent, Grab and half the apps you use here will be tied to it. Home internet comes next, and in most condos it's quick and easy.

People who are moving here ask me about this a lot, so here's how it works.

## Tourist SIM or a Thai number in your name?

On a holiday, the tourist SIM sold at the airport arrivals hall, or an eSIM bought before you fly, is the easy option. It gives you data and a number for a fixed number of days, and you don't have to think about it again.

If you're living here, or staying for a few months, get a SIM registered to you with your passport in a phone shop. The reason is everything that gets linked to it. When you open a Thai bank account, the bank sends codes to your Thai number and the banking app is tied to it. Lose the number and you can lose access to your banking until you sort it out in a branch. So treat it like your number at home: keep it topped up, and don't let it lapse while you're away.

Thailand has tightened SIM registration in recent years to fight phone scams. Expect to show your passport, and if the network ever asks you to confirm your identity again, do it rather than ignore the message, or the number can be suspended.

## Which network should you choose?

There are two big networks in Thailand: AIS, and True, which merged with dtac. Both cover Pattaya well. Coverage in the city is good on either, so choose by the deal on offer and which shop is closest to you. Both have branches in the big malls, including Central and Terminal 21.

Most people here use a monthly package on a postpaid plan, or a prepaid SIM topped up through the app or at any 7-Eleven. Packages with a lot of data cost a few hundred baht a month, which is a fraction of what most of us paid back home. If you're not sure, start prepaid and switch once you know how much data you use.

## Can you keep your number from home?

Yes, and most people do. Keep your home SIM in a second slot, or move it to an eSIM, and keep it on a cheap plan so you can still receive codes from your bank at home. Just turn off data roaming on it, or you'll get an expensive surprise. Your Thai SIM handles data and local calls.

## How do you get internet at home?

Fibre broadband is widespread in Pattaya, and the internet is very good. Even in storms these days it very rarely goes down. The main providers are AIS Fibre, True Online and 3BB, and most condo buildings are already wired for at least one of them.

Ask your landlord or the condo office before you sign up for anything. Some condos include internet in the rent or the building fee, and some landlords already have a contract on the unit that you can take over. If you sign up yourself, you'll usually need your passport and a 12-month contract, and an installer comes out within a few days.

If you work online, it's worth asking about upload speed as well as download, and keeping a phone hotspot as a backup for the rare day the fibre drops. Most remote workers here simply work from their condo, as I wrote in [Pattaya for digital nomads](/articles/pattaya-for-digital-nomads).

## What to sort out in your first week

1. Buy a tourist SIM or eSIM for the first few days, so you have maps and Grab from the airport.
2. Get a SIM in your own name with your passport at an AIS or True shop.
3. Keep your home number active on a cheap plan, with roaming data off.
4. Ask your landlord or condo office about internet before signing a contract.
5. Use your Thai number when you open your bank account, and keep it.
