Massage chairs are popping up all over Pattaya, and I'll admit I've become a convert. I don't think they'll replace Thai masseuses any time soon. But I can see a lot of people, like me, visiting a masseuse less often.

## They're everywhere now

When I first noticed them at Central, there were maybe two or three. Now there are around 30, and sometimes you can't even get one.

Royal Garden now has whole shops filled with nothing but massage chairs. And they're turning up anywhere people wait around: water parks, attractions, anywhere you might be sitting for a while.

## The price is hard to argue with

I used one recently. It was ฿60 for 30 minutes, or ฿100 for an hour. A traditional massage is around ฿250 an hour.

## Not as good, but more convenient

To be fair, a chair isn't a real massage. It mostly works your back and the backs of your legs. A masseuse works your whole body, feet included, and no chair comes close to that yet.

But for me, convenience wins. If I'm out shopping with someone for an hour, I can sit in a chair and relax while I wait. After that, I don't feel the need to go for a proper massage later. These days, I hardly use masseuses at all.

## What it could mean

I don't know how big a risk this really is. I doubt chairs will ever fully replace a human masseuse. Massage is part of Thai culture and a big draw for tourists, and a chair can't do what a good masseuse does.

What I think is more likely is that people simply go less often. A quick top-up in a chair while you wait, and the regular visit to the massage shop gets pushed back a week or two. Multiply that across a lot of people, and it adds up for the shops.

## My take

Only time will tell. But if the chairs keep spreading at this rate, I wouldn't be surprised if massage shops start to feel it, especially the ones relying on regulars rather than tourists.
