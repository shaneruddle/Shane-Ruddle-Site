I haven't had a drink in about 12 years, and I live in one of the biggest party cities in the world. People assume that must be hard. Honestly, for me, it wasn't.

## The secret: change the situations, not the friends

I didn't cut my drinking friends out of my life. I just made sure I was never in situations where drinking was going on.

That meant I became a bit more boring. A typical Saturday night turned into a cup of tea and a film at home with the family. I didn't want to go out and not drink, because I didn't think I'd enjoy it. I don't particularly like being around drunk people when I'm sober, and I didn't want the temptation.

After a while, it just became easy, especially with a young family. The last thing you want with young kids is to be hungover.

## What I've gained

I've gained so much that I'd never drink again. I hope my sons won't drink either, and I think drinking isn't seen the same way now as it was when I was growing up.

**Health.** My health wasn't bad back then, but I was younger, and you don't notice drinking affecting your health until you're older. I don't feel any different now, but I'm sure I would if I'd carried on for the last decade or so. And no more hangovers, which I always struggled with.

**A clear head.** Business can be mentally exhausting. Adding alcohol on top of that is never a good idea. Decisions are being made all the time, and you need to be level-headed and clear-minded to make them well.

**Money.** In Thailand, it isn't the drinking that costs you. You can get very cheap alcohol at 7-Eleven. It's everything else you do when you've been drinking: the extra entertainment, the nights out on Walking Street, and the poor decisions that end up costing you a lot of money.

## My take

You don't have to drink to live in Pattaya, and you don't have to lose your friends to stop. Change where you spend your evenings, give it time, and it gets easy.
