I came to Thailand hoping to try for the Asian Tour. I never teed it up at qualifying. Instead, the refund on my entry fee paid for me to stay a little longer, and I'm still here. Looking back, it's one of the best decisions I never really meant to make. (How I ended up in Pattaya in the first place is in [how an internet café search brought me to Pattaya](/articles/how-an-internet-cafe-search-brought-me-to-pattaya).)

## Spotted at the driving range

It started at Pattaya City Golf Driving Range, which is still there today, right next to the Pattaya Sports Club, a long-running club that raises money for charity through sport, mostly golf.

I was hitting balls and striping them towards the back fence when a guy called Mick came over. Mick, who I'm still friendly with, asked how long I was here for, where I was from and what my handicap was.

After we'd chatted, he rang a friend of his, Paul, a good golfer off six or seven at the time, and told him he'd just met a young pro from the UK. Paul was playing in a team tournament: you played individually, but your score counted towards the team. So, ironically, Paul subbed me in, in place of Mick. Mick wasn't a particularly good golfer, and still isn't, and he didn't seem that bothered.

## My first round: Mountain Shadow

That first round was at Mountain Shadow. I played reasonably well, but I struggled with the layout and with the heat, and I didn't shoot the lights out. To be fair, nobody should expect to on their first round in a new country.

Looking back, my first round in Pattaya probably shouldn't have been a competition. Something more relaxed would have been better. But I enjoyed it, and I went on to play a lot more with those guys. Once word got round that I was shooting decent scores, I started getting invited to play with some of the local pros.

## The round that changed my plans

The one that stands out was with Mike, a Scottish pro. I'd never seen anyone hit the ball as far as he did.

When I got chatting to him, I found out he'd tried to qualify for the Asian Tour around half a dozen times and hadn't made it. I was there to try for the Asian Tour myself. I'd assumed it would be a bit easier to get onto than the tours in Europe. After playing with Mike, and a few more rounds with him after that, I quickly realised it wasn't.

## The refund that kept me in Thailand

The entry fee was a lot of money for me at the time, and I didn't feel ready. So I asked for a refund and used the money to stay in Thailand a bit longer. I never went back for it in later years either. If anything, I got worse at golf the longer I stayed.

## Why it worked out for the best

I always wanted to be a pro because I loved playing. But if someone had sat me down back then and shown me the numbers, I think I'd have looked at it very differently.

It's not like football, where you can make a living in the lower leagues. In golf, once you add in the travel, I'd guess only around 500 golfers in the world genuinely make a living from playing, maybe a thousand at a push. And there are hundreds of thousands, if not millions, of people chasing those spots.

I'm more analytical now than I was then. If I'd thought it through properly at the time, I don't think I'd have chased it at the expense of every other dream or goal. And I wouldn't have the life I have in Thailand now.
