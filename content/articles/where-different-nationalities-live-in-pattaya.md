One of the curious things about Pattaya is how different nationalities seem to gather in their own pockets of the city. Maybe it's human nature to gravitate towards people from a similar background. This isn't a guide to where you should go. Pattaya is friendly and welcoming to every nationality and walk of life, and I'd always encourage people to mix. But if you want to find people from home, talk your own language or argue about your own country's politics, here's where to look.

## British

The Soi Buakhao area is very British. Prices are good, there are lots of sports bars for the football, plenty of pubs and the kind of food Brits like. There are also expat pubs all over the city. I'm biased, but our three Hemingways restaurants are mostly British customers too. More in [watching football in Pattaya](/articles/watching-football-in-pattaya).

## Russian

There are two main Russian areas. One is on the Jomtien side of Pratumnak, around the road down to Pattaya Park, with a big Russian market at the bottom and mostly Russian-run businesses along it. The more affluent Russians tend to be in [Wongamat](/areas/wongamat-naklua). Cozy Beach used to be popular too, but the community seems to have moved more towards Pratumnak.

That said, many Russians I know have deliberately rented elsewhere. They came here to meet new people and learn a different way of life, not to be surrounded by other Russians.

## Indian

Second Road, from the flyover at the bottom of Pratumnak Hill up to South Pattaya Road, is the Indian area. There are at least a dozen Indian restaurants, plus tailors and some hotels that cater mainly to Indian guests. There's also an Arab area just off that stretch of Second Road.

## Chinese, Japanese and Korean

Chinese residents are spread across the city, but Siam Royal View has a large Chinese community. For going out, Third Road between Central Pattaya Road and North Pattaya Road is the place, with Chinese restaurants and karaoke bars. It's really a mix of affluent Chinese, Japanese and South Korean visitors.

## Scandinavian

Scandinavians are spread out, with quite a few around Soi Buakhao. Honestly, a lot of Scandinavians in Thailand live in Hua Hin (see [Pattaya vs Hua Hin](/articles/pattaya-vs-hua-hin)). What they do have in Pattaya is golf. They're very keen golfers, and many run or join golf societies. If you want to speak Swedish or Finnish, a golf society is a good place to start. More in [golf in Pattaya](/articles/golf-in-pattaya).

## German

Naklua, from Terminal 21 up to around Soi 18, has long been the German area, with at least half a dozen German restaurants. (If German is your first language, some of my guides are [in German too](/de).)

## American

Americans don't have one area. Many live in East Pattaya, and like the Scandinavians, plenty play golf, so a golf society is a good way to meet fellow Americans.

Cajun Life Cafe has a lot of American customers. Its original owner was American, and many of his regulars stayed. I also used to own a bar called the Golf Club on Soi Buakhao that was very popular with Americans, though I sold it years ago and it's no longer there.

## Don't stay in your bubble

These pockets are useful to know about, but don't limit yourself to them. You don't need to live in Wongamat because you're Russian, or on Soi Buakhao because you're British. Pattaya is at its best when you mix. More in [making friends in Pattaya](/articles/making-friends-in-pattaya) and [the best area to live in Pattaya](/articles/best-area-to-live-in-pattaya).
