Someone I know was arrested for drink driving in Pattaya about 15 years ago. It was a long, uncomfortable process, cheaper than the alternative but a lot more hassle. The first lesson is obvious: don't drink and drive. The rest is what the process actually looks like. More in [driving in Pattaya](/articles/driving-in-pattaya) and [alcohol rules in Thailand](/articles/alcohol-rules-in-thailand).

*This is one person's experience from years ago. Laws and procedures change, so if you're arrested, contact your embassy and a lawyer.*

## The night it happened

Back then, drink driving was illegal, but riding a motorbike from bar to bar after a few too many was almost the done thing. People told themselves that on a bike you'd only hurt yourself. That's no excuse at all.

One night there was a police checkpoint. He was stopped and arrested along with half a dozen others, put in the back of a pickup truck, and driven to the police station on Soi 9.

## Pay now, or stay the night

At the station, everyone was given a choice. Pay around ฿10,000 on the spot and go home. Or spend the night in the cells, go to court the next day, and pay whatever the court decided.

It was late, he'd been drinking and he was tired, so he stayed.

## The cells

The cells above the station were pretty horrific. You share with a lot of other people, some of whom have been there a very long time, not just that night's arrests. You're asleep for most of it, so one night isn't the end of the world. But it's not somewhere anyone would want to go back to.

## The passport and the petrol stop

The next morning, before releasing you, the police want your passport so they know you'll turn up to court. Most people don't carry it, so they drive you to wherever it is, home or work.

On the way, they stopped at a petrol station, and he ended up filling up the police car. There wasn't much he could say about it. At that point you're in their pocket.

Once they had the passport, he was let go. Court is usually set for the next working day. This was a Sunday, so it was the Monday.

## Court day

He was told to be there at 8am. The hearing didn't happen until around midday, so he waited half the day.

In the waiting area, there was a choice of rooms. For a few hundred baht you could sit in the air-conditioned room. Otherwise you sat in a sweat pit with everyone else. The courtroom itself was air-conditioned.

The fine the court gave him was about ฿1,200, compared with around ฿10,000 he'd been offered at the station. As far as he knows, there were no lasting consequences for a minor offence like that: no effect on his visa or work permit. A more serious offence would be very different.

## Getting the passport back

The last hurdle was getting his passport back. The police said immigration had it. Immigration said the police had it. It felt like he was being given the runaround until he paid something extra. Luckily he knew someone at immigration and got it back without paying. But once you're in that situation, expect people to try to take advantage.

## The rules are tougher now

This was around 15 years ago, and penalties have been increased since. Today, drink driving in Thailand can mean up to a year in prison, a fine of ฿5,000 to ฿20,000, or both, plus a licence suspension of at least six months. Refusing a breath test is treated the same as being over the limit. If you injure or kill someone, the penalties rise to years in prison and fines of up to ฿200,000. Don't assume a story like this one is what you'd face today.

## My advice

First, don't drink and drive. Get a taxi or a Bolt.

If you are arrested, stay calm and polite, and contact your embassy if you need help. Looking back, he says that if given the choice again, he'd probably have settled at the station, even though it cost a lot more, just to avoid the night in the cells, the half-day at court and the passport chase.
