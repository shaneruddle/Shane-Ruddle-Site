If you own property in Thailand through a company with Thai nominee shareholders, the panic is justified. But from what I'm hearing, the headlines may not be telling the whole story about who's actually ending up in prison, and why.

*I'm not a lawyer, and this isn't legal advice. What follows includes unconfirmed accounts I've heard secondhand.*

## The phone calls have started

I was talking recently to someone who's been getting phone calls from friends back in the UK, panicking about the nominee crackdown. I understand why. (More background in [is the nominee crackdown really about the OECD?](/articles/is-the-nominee-crackdown-really-about-the-oecd) and [nominee companies in Thailand](/articles/nominee-companies-in-thailand).)

## What was reported

On 9 November, a court in Hua Hin heard four nominee-shareholding cases. In three of them, defendants were given six-month sentences without suspension: a married couple were allowed to apply for bail, while two others were taken to prison. In the fourth case, heard in a different courtroom, a married couple received suspended three-month sentences. Companies and foreign shareholders were fined in every case. Many people, including a lawyer involved, were left asking why the outcomes were so different.

## What people on the ground are saying

The person I spoke to has friends living in Hua Hin. What they're hearing locally, and I want to stress that this is unconfirmed, is that the nominee issue may have been the reason to investigate, but not the whole picture. The talk is that other matters, such as renting out property without paying tax, may have come up once the authorities started looking.

I can't verify that, and the court reports describe these as nominee-shareholding cases. But if there's any truth in it, the lesson is important.

## Why it matters

A nominee investigation can open the door to everything else. Once the authorities are looking at your company and your property, they may also look at how you've used it: whether you're renting it out, whether you're paying tax, whether everything else is in order.

And whatever the full story, the publicity is doing its job. It's scaring people away from nominee structures, which is exactly what the authorities want.

## My advice

- **Take it seriously.** The panic is justified, whatever lies behind individual cases.
- **Get independent legal advice now**, from a lawyer who isn't connected to whoever set up your company.
- **Get everything else in order too.** If you rent out your property, make sure the income and the tax are properly declared.
- **Don't rely on rumours**, including this one. Rely on proper advice about your own situation.
