When Thailand legalised cannabis in 2022, Pattaya changed almost overnight. Now it has swung back the other way: since 2025 you need a prescription to buy it, and smoking in public is banned, with fines of up to ฿25,000. Here's what I saw from the ground.

## Shops everywhere

The first thing you noticed was the shops. Within about six months Pattaya must have had 30 to 40 cannabis shops.

## A younger crowd

In my 22 years here I'd never seen so many young people in the city. I can't prove it was the cannabis. Thailand is attracting a younger crowd for other reasons too, like big events such as Tomorrowland and the DTV visa. But it felt too much of a coincidence that the crowd changed as soon as the rules were relaxed. More in [working remotely in Pattaya](/articles/working-remotely-in-pattaya).

## Smoke outside our restaurants

I never had staff using it, not to my knowledge. But we did have problems with customers, especially at Cajun Life Cafe. We allow cigarettes outside, but not cannabis, because the smell is so much stronger. People still lit up outside, signs or no signs.

Smoking cannabis in public was actually illegal, but there was so much confusion about what was and wasn't allowed. That comes back to how badly the government handled it. More in [why tourism in Thailand is down](/articles/why-tourism-in-thailand-is-down).

## The crackdown was the original plan

What makes me laugh is that the tighter rules are what was meant all along. Cannabis was decriminalised for medical use, because research suggested it helped people with certain conditions. Everyone took that to mean they could freely smoke joints in public, which was never the intention.

How do you decriminalise something for medical purposes and then let 30 to 40 shops open in one city? It's beyond ridiculous. All the government has done recently is enforce the law, which is what it's always so bad at doing.

Today most of those shops have gone. I'd say we're down from 30 or 40 to fewer than half a dozen.

## If you're visiting

Know the rules before you do anything, because they've changed and they're now enforced. Recreational use isn't permitted: licensed shops can only sell with a prescription from a practitioner, and smoking in public can get you a heavy fine. Don't bring any into the country either, even from somewhere it's legal.

And remember the smell travels. In one condo I stayed in, a neighbour was told not to smoke in his room, so he moved to his balcony, and with the wind blowing the wrong way you could smell it in the rooms above. The building now has signs up everywhere, as many do.
