When I first came to Pattaya, high season ran from October to the end of April, about seven months. Today it feels like it runs from mid-December to the end of March. That's roughly half what it was. More in [why tourism in Thailand is down](/articles/why-tourism-in-thailand-is-down).

## How it used to be

Those seven months were all busy, with a super-peak from around 15 December to the end of January. For about ten years the gap between high and low season even narrowed, because low season was still fairly busy.

Then the pandemic wiped out a year or so. When it ended, Pattaya was busy almost all year round. It was crazy how busy it was. After that it settled back to the usual six or seven months of high season, and now it has shrunk again. More in [how my businesses got through COVID](/articles/how-my-businesses-got-through-covid).

## Why it's shrinking

- **The rain seems to last longer.** The rainy season traditionally ended around October. In my experience it now often runs to the end of November, an extra six to eight weeks, so people who used to come in autumn now come later. More in [the rainy season in Pattaya](/articles/rainy-season-in-pattaya).
- **Thailand costs more.** People still come, but for shorter stays. More in [is Pattaya expensive?](/articles/is-pattaya-expensive)
- **Visas.** Plenty of people used to spend the whole Western winter here, six months or more. Many would plan a few months, then extend or do a visa run and stay longer. That's much harder now, and since September 2026 the visa-free stay for most Western nationalities has been cut from 60 days to 30. More in [Thai visas in Pattaya](/articles/thai-visas-in-pattaya).

## What it means for car rental

At Pattaya Rent a Car, the demand now piles into those few peak weeks from mid-December to the end of January. If you don't book in advance, you won't get a car.

Prices rise with demand, and regular customers complain about the difference. People used to accept paying about 20% more in high season. Now, over the peak, it can sometimes be double. We're not trying to rip anyone off. Demand is just that high. More in [renting a car in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-car-in-pattaya).

## What it means for restaurants

In restaurants, it's a staffing problem. When a lot of people arrive in a short time, you need more staff so service doesn't slip, but you only need them for a few weeks. More in [running a restaurant and bar in Pattaya](/articles/running-a-restaurant-and-bar-in-pattaya).

## Why it matters

Any business wants a fairly steady cycle, a bit up and a bit down. Huge swings make it very hard to manage. My advice: book early if you're visiting over the peak, and if you run a business here, plan for a short, intense high season and a long quiet one.
