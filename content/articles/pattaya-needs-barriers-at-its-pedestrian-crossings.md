I was in Dubai recently, and one thing stood out: railings and barriers along the roads that only let you cross where there's a proper crossing and traffic lights. It got me thinking again about how much Pattaya needs the same. I think it would stop a lot of accidents, and make the city look better too.

## The problem with crossings here

I've written before about [how dangerous crossing the road can be in Pattaya](/articles/expect-the-unexpected-crossing-the-road-in-pattaya). A big part of the problem is where the crossings are.

The authorities tend to put crossings where pedestrians naturally cross, which often means right on a junction. So drivers are trying to watch traffic and negotiate a junction while, at the same time, someone steps onto the crossing. In most other countries, crossings aren't put right next to junctions for exactly that reason.

On top of that, there are often obstacles in the way, so it isn't easy for drivers to see people waiting to cross.

## Why barriers would help

The reason crossings end up on junctions is simple. If the city moved them further down the road, people would just carry on crossing wherever they liked.

Barriers would fix that. With railings along the road and a gap only at the crossing, everyone would cross in the same place. Pedestrians would gather at one point, drivers would know where to expect them, and crossings could be put where they're safest, not just where people happen to walk.

## Start with Thepprasit Road

If I had to pick one road to start with, it would be Thepprasit Road. It's fast, people jaywalk across it all the time, and there are plenty of near misses. I'd put barriers all the way along it, with openings only at the pedestrian crossings.

It matters even more here because Pattaya has hardly any pedestrian bridges. In Bangkok, you can often go over the traffic. Here, you nearly always have to cross at road level, among the cars and motorbikes. There's one on Sukhumvit Road, but hardly anyone seems to use it.

## The other side

Barriers aren't free. They cost money to install and maintain, and some people will climb over them anyway. They also only work if there are enough crossings, close enough together, that people aren't tempted to take a shortcut.

## My take

Even so, I think it would be a worthy investment for the city. Barriers wherever there's a pedestrian crossing would save lives, make the roads calmer, and make Pattaya look a lot more cared for.
