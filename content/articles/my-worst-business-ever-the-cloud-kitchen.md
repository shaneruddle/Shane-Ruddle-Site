My worst business wasn't the one that cost me the most money. That was the driving range, which I wrote about in [my biggest business failure](/articles/my-biggest-business-failure). This one sounded perfect on paper and never really got going: a delivery-only cloud kitchen I started in Pattaya during COVID.

## Looking for an opportunity in COVID

When COVID hit, I did what most people with an entrepreneurial streak did: I looked for an opportunity. The only obvious one was food delivery, which went through the roof for obvious reasons.

While I was researching it, I came across cloud kitchens. They weren't new, and they'd been around well before COVID, but they were new to me, and I loved the idea. A delivery-only kitchen can be anywhere. You can run several cuisines out of one kitchen, drop the ones that don't work, double down on the ones that do, and keep trying new ideas.

It made even more sense for me because I already had a fully working kitchen and food production facility, licensed with the FDA, for my pie business, London Pie.

## The commission problem

The delivery apps take around 30% in commission. I knew that going in.

When delivery is just a sideline for a restaurant, that commission feels like a cost on extra sales. When delivery is your whole business, you have to be extremely tight on the numbers. That sounds easy enough, but when you're new, you also have to spend on advertising to get known. Long story short, we were getting orders, but the margins were razor thin.

## The wrong location

Once we had enough data, we found the bigger problem: the location.

The kitchen was where it was because that's where our pie kitchen and FDA licence were. But it was too far from central Pattaya. What we found out later is that Grab mostly shows people restaurants that are closest to them, apart from their favourites and places they've ordered from before. Because we weren't in the centre, we didn't even show up in a lot of people's searches.

For a pie factory, the location was fine. For a cloud kitchen, it was wrong.

## How it ended

It didn't end in one go. When COVID ended, people went back to restaurants and deliveries dropped off naturally. Then we sold the pie production and moved it out, and the facility didn't make sense to pay for with just the cloud kitchen.

I'd bought The Golf Club bar by then, so I moved the cloud kitchen there. When I eventually [sold The Golf Club](/articles/selling-the-golf-club), that was the end of it.

## Why it was my worst business

Not because of the money. Because it sounded so good. Everyone I told said Pattaya definitely needed one. I loved how flexible it could be. On paper, it was a great idea.

And I wasn't the only one. Around the world, cloud kitchens were meant to be the future of food, and a lot of them have struggled or closed since.

## What I'd tell anyone thinking about one

- **Work out your numbers after commission and advertising**, not before.
- **Put it where the customers are**, not where your existing kitchen happens to be. On the delivery apps, distance decides who sees you.
- **Don't build a business on a temporary boom.** Delivery during COVID wasn't normal, and it didn't last.

The idea still appeals to me. But a great idea and a great business aren't the same thing.
