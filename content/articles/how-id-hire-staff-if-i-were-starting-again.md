If I were starting my businesses again, I'd take hiring far more seriously. To be honest, I still don't take it as seriously as I should, especially for key roles. But I'm a lot better than when I started.

## Throwing everyone at the wall

In the early days, my strategy was basically to throw people at the wall and see who stuck. I didn't really think about the type of person I was looking for, what I could offer them, or things like contracts.

## What I'd do now

**Write a proper job description.** Set out the role and responsibilities, then split what you need into must-haves, non-negotiables and nice-to-haves.

**Have a real interview process.** At least two people should interview each candidate, and there may need to be a second interview.

**Train them properly.** This is where a lot of businesses fall down, mine included. People get stuck straight into the role and are expected to know how everything works, just because their CV says they've worked in a kitchen. "There's the kitchen, crack on." There's no real training. Even now, onboarding is something we still need to get better at.

**Know where to find people.** I use Pattaya Job Centre, a business I set up over 20 years ago, along with local Facebook groups. For more senior positions I use JobsDB, a Bangkok-based job platform, and I've had some success with it.

**Use the probation period.** Make sure new staff are onboarded correctly, and use that time to check they're the right fit.

## Why it matters

In Thailand there's no severance pay if you let someone go before they've worked 120 days. I always thought it was 90, but it's 120, which is why many probation periods here are set at 119 days. You still need to give proper notice or pay in lieu, and I'm not a lawyer, so check the details. But even when letting someone go is cheap, hiring the wrong person still wastes a lot of time, and a fair bit of money too.

## Never stop recruiting

This is something I'm still telling my managers today: take recruitment more seriously, and never stop doing it. Even when you're fully staffed, keep recruiting, because people come and go all the time.

## My take

Hiring is one of the most important things you do in a business, and one of the easiest to rush. Slow down, have a process, and train people properly. It's far cheaper than hiring the wrong person.
