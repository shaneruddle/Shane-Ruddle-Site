I was in Bangkok recently and spent time in Chatuchak Park. I've also enjoyed Benjakitti Park and Lumpini Park. They have a great atmosphere: people running, walking and taking their dogs out, surrounded by trees and plants. There's wildlife too, monitor lizards and fish in the ponds. You look up and see skyscrapers, and hear the traffic in the distance.

Pattaya doesn't really have anything like that, and I think it's missing.

## What we have

Mabprachan Lake has a lot of what I'd want: space to run, walk and enjoy nature. But it's quite a long way out of town.

A couple of newer parks have opened in the Jomtien area, but hardly anyone uses them.

## Good idea, poor execution

I went to one of those parks, which opened three or four years ago. Unless you knew it was there, you'd never stumble across it. The trees aren't mature yet, so there's no shade and no escape from the sun during the day. At night, there isn't enough lighting for it to feel safe. So nobody uses it, day or night.

That seems to be a bit of a theme in Pattaya: the idea is there, but it isn't thought through.

## The real difference: mature trees

The biggest difference between the Bangkok parks and ours is the trees. Many of Bangkok's parks have been there for decades, so the trees are grand and give plenty of shade.

Here, it's mostly palm trees, and palms give almost no shade. If there were a way to bring mature trees into our parks, we could start to recreate what Bangkok has. I think that would make all the difference.

## Parks as a priority

In a big, busy city like Bangkok, the parks feel like a real priority. They're well looked after and an important part of city life.

In Pattaya, parks don't feel like a focal point yet. I think the city would say it has the beach, and that's what it takes care of. That's fair, and the beach is looking better than ever. But why not do both?

## What I'd do if I were mayor

This is a suggestion, not a criticism. I'd create one proper, well-kept city park closer to the centre. Jomtien would be a great spot, and there's plenty of land.

It would need:

- **Shade from day one**, with mature trees or shade structures, so people can actually use it in the heat.
- **Good lighting**, so it feels safe in the evening when it's cooler.
- **A central, visible location**, easy to reach, that people pass by and know about.
- **Space to run, walk and bring the dog**, with ponds and greenery that attract wildlife.

## My take

Bangkok shows how much a great park adds to city life. Pattaya has the land and the people who'd use it. It just needs to be done properly.
