The thing nobody tells you before you open a restaurant in Pattaya is how hard it's going to be. For the basics, read [running a restaurant and bar in Pattaya](/articles/running-a-restaurant-and-bar-in-pattaya).

## Doing it better isn't enough

You might see a busy restaurant and think you could do it better. Maybe you can. But it takes a long time for people to find that out. Pattaya is very competitive, and building a customer base takes much longer than people expect. I reckon about three years, as I explain in [why I'd buy a business rather than start one](/articles/why-buy-a-business-in-pattaya).

## Your friends won't save you

People tell me, "I've got lots of friends, they'll come." They will, at first. But if the food and service aren't good, they'll quietly stop. And because they're your friends, they won't tell you why. They'll say it's not on their route, or they're not eating out much at the moment. You'll just see less of them and never quite know why.

## Keep a year of money back

The other mistake I see all the time is spending the whole budget before opening. Say someone has ฿4 million. They spend all of it on the fit-out and open with nothing left, assuming they'll make money from day one.

Keep enough to cover your costs for at least a year. My [business reality check](/business-in-pattaya) gives you a rough budget for your idea. You'll take some money, so you won't need to cover every fixed cost in full, but you won't take enough to turn a profit. If you're in profit within a year, you're doing really well. After a year is a good goal.

Be wary of other owners' success stories, too. People like to sound smarter than they are. "I got my money back in 18 months" might really have been two and a half years. Believe it, and you go in with false expectations.

I recently turned down someone who wanted to turn one of my venues into a sauna. He had the money, but he expected a return straight away. He had no business experience, and that one expectation told me everything I needed to know.

## Staff come and go

What surprised me most when I opened my first restaurant, at Fairways, was staff turnover. People leave for reasons that seemed odd to me: they've run out of money and are going back home to their province, they've argued with a colleague, a customer shouted at them and they don't turn up the next day, or they've split up with a boyfriend.

To be fair, a lot of the staff we employ in Pattaya are young, and young people everywhere are still finding their feet and tend to move around. I didn't realise that at first, because I never did it myself. More in [managing foreign staff vs Thai staff](/articles/managing-foreign-staff-vs-thai-staff).

## My one tip: location

Location, location, location. It matters less than it used to, because it's so easy to get around Pattaya on a motorbike, but it still makes a difference. If your location isn't great, make sure you have parking.
