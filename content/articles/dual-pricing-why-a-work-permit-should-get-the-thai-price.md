I've written before that [dual pricing in Thailand](/articles/dual-pricing-in-thailand) is the right idea with the wrong execution. Everyone talks about the national parks, where the two prices are carved into the sign at the gate. But dual pricing goes a lot further than the parks, and I think there's a simple, fair fix.

## It's not just the national parks

Over the years, I've seen two-tier pricing in all sorts of places:

- **Attractions.** There's one in Pattaya, which I won't name, with a board out front showing one price for Thais and another for foreigners.
- **Promotions for Thais only.** An aquarium in Bangkok ran a promotion that was reserved only for Thais.
- **Golf courses.** Many have done it over the years.
- **Public hospitals.** Since 2019 they've been allowed to charge foreigners more. We use private hospitals, but most foreigners at a public hospital probably wouldn't even know.

That's part of the problem. Sometimes it's on a board out front. Often it isn't. At an amusement park, unless it's advertised, you'd never know you were paying more.

## The golf courses found the answer

The golf courses are an interesting example. After a major backlash, many changed from a Thai price to a resident price. To get it, you had to prove you lived here, with a residence certificate or a work permit. A condo lease or a non-immigrant visa wasn't enough.

I think that's the fairest approach, and it could work everywhere.

## Why the driving licence didn't work

Some places have tried to extend the Thai price to residents before. Some attractions used to give you the Thai price if you showed a Thai driving licence.

The problem is that almost anyone can get a Thai driving licence. A tourist could get one if they wanted to, and so could someone who comes for three or four months every year to escape the winter. A driving licence doesn't really say anything about whether you live or work here. I think that's why many places no longer recognise it. It didn't go far enough.

A work permit does. So in my opinion the idea was right, but the document was wrong. It shouldn't be the driving licence. It should be the work permit.

## What a work permit actually says about you

The usual argument for dual pricing is that places like national parks and public hospitals are subsidised by the government, and Thais fund the government through their taxes. So Thais get the concession.

But look at what a work permit means:

- **You're employing Thais.** A company generally needs four Thai employees for every foreigner it hires on a work permit. And for many of us, it's a business we've set up, employing far more than four.
- **You're paying social security** for those staff.
- **You're paying personal income tax** on your own salary.
- **You're paying to hold the permit** and renewing it every year.

A work permit is proof that you're here to stay and that you're paying into the system. It's also easy to carry and show, just like a passport.

So if the concession is for the people who fund the government, how does that argument hold up for someone with a work permit?

## The wrong message

I'm not talking about myself here. Plenty of foreigners come to Thailand, get a job and genuinely do a lot of good for the country.

I was chatting recently with the headmaster of an international school. His school teaches hundreds of Thai students to a very high standard. And yet when he visits a national park, he's openly charged the foreigner price.

That just feels wrong. It sends completely the wrong message to the people who are contributing the most.

## A simple suggestion

This isn't about attacking anyone. A lot of these rules were set up years ago and simply haven't been revisited. My suggestion is straightforward: anyone who can show a valid work permit should pay the Thai price. It's mostly a matter of telling the people who run these places that a work permit counts.

The golf courses have shown it can be done. It's time for the national parks, attractions and public services to do the same.

## The other side

Supporters of dual pricing argue that even working foreigners haven't contributed over a lifetime the way Thai citizens have, and that lower prices for Thais are about making national heritage affordable for local people on local wages. Others say that checking documents at every gate would be impractical.

## My take

Dual pricing for tourists I can understand. Charging the same foreigner price to someone who has lived here for 20 years, employs Thai staff and pays Thai taxes is harder to justify. A resident price, based on a work permit, would fix that.
