I tip around 10% for service. Restaurants, petrol stations, my hairdresser. I don't tip professional services, and I certainly don't tip officials. If you're new to Pattaya, that's a good rule of thumb.

## Why Pattaya has a tipping culture

When I first came here, everyone I knew who'd been here longer tipped, so I did too. It's simply the culture, and it's stayed that way.

As I understand it, it goes back to the American servicemen who came here on leave during the Vietnam War, when Pattaya was still a small fishing village. America has a strong tipping culture, the locals got used to it, and over the years it became expected, to a degree.

## Who I tip, and who I don't

- **Yes:** restaurants and bars, petrol station attendants, hairdressers, and other service jobs.
- **No:** professional services, like lawyers or accountants.
- **Never:** officials, such as immigration officers. That isn't a tip.

Around 10% is a good guide in restaurants. Elsewhere, leaving the change or rounding up is normal.

## Where the tips go in my restaurants

In our restaurants, tips go straight to the staff. The owners and management don't keep any of it, and we don't get involved in sharing it out. The staff handle that themselves.

The shared pot is split between everyone involved in service, front of house and the kitchen alike. It's divided by how many hours each person worked that day.

There's one exception, and it's common sense. If a customer clearly hands a tip to a particular waiter or waitress, they keep it. Anything else goes into the shared pot. Not every restaurant works this way, but ours do.

Here's a bit of inside information. We don't get involved in sharing tips out, but we do record them, just for the record. Across a month, or even a year, tips come out at around 6.5% to 7% of our total takings.

That's lower than the 10% many people aim for, and I think it's down to convenience. If your bill is ฿950 and you pay with a ฿1,000 note, most people just leave the change rather than adding more on top. Spread across thousands of bills, that works out at just under 7%. So if you're wondering what's normal, that's a good real-world benchmark.

## My advice

Tipping isn't compulsory here, but it's appreciated, and it's part of how things work in Pattaya. If the service is good, around 10% in a restaurant is about right. If you want a particular person to get it, hand it to them directly.
