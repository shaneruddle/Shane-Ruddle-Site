At Alan Bolton and East Coast Real Estate, we don't do short-term rentals. By short term I mean anything from one month up to six months. Six months is normally our minimum. The simple reason is that it isn't worth the hassle, and most landlords don't want it anyway. More in [renting a condo in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-condo-in-pattaya).

## The work doesn't match the commission

On a short rental, the commission is usually around 10%. For that, we still have to do everything we'd do for a long let. We're not a hotel with staff on hand, so an agent has to meet the tenant at the property, do the inventory, check the electricity and water readings, and then come back for the checkout. Even for a one-month rental, it just doesn't add up.

Some people offer to pay more, even 20% extra. That isn't really the point. It still wouldn't make it worthwhile, and the landlord still wouldn't agree. More in [working as a property agent in Pattaya](/articles/working-as-a-property-agent-in-pattaya).

## Landlords don't want it either

This is the other side of the coin. Most landlords simply won't consider short lets. More turnover means more chances of a bad tenant, and one bad tenant can be catastrophic for a property. So even if we wanted to do it, we'd struggle to find owners willing. More in [renting out your condo in Pattaya](/articles/renting-out-your-condo-in-pattaya).

## It's not just us

I'd say 90%, maybe even 95%, of agencies here don't do short-term rentals either. A lot of people who ask already half know the answer and are just chancing it. In October and November, at least half our enquiries are for short stays, ahead of the high season. We reply with a standard answer and don't usually go into the reasons. More in [Pattaya's high season is getting shorter](/articles/pattayas-high-season-is-getting-shorter).

## Where to look instead

If you want somewhere for one to five months, try Booking.com, Agoda or Airbnb. Just be aware that, under Thailand's Hotel Act, renting out a condo for less than 30 days without a hotel licence generally isn't legal, so look for monthly stays or proper serviced apartments. More in [where to stay in Pattaya](/articles/where-to-stay-in-pattaya).
