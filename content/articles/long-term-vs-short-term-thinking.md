One of the biggest struggles I have with my staff, especially in my property businesses, isn't about effort or ability. It's about time. I'm thinking in five- and ten-year cycles. My staff are thinking in days, maybe weeks, a month at most. That creates a constant tug of war.

## Owners think long term

Most owners naturally think long term. The business is yours, you plan to be around, and you're building something for years ahead.

Staff generally don't think that way. Most people simply aren't thinking about where a company will be in five years, and many aren't sure they'll still be in the company, or even the industry, by then.

## Why strategies don't land

This is where it becomes a real problem. Say I'm trying to put a new strategy in place. I explain it to the team, and it feels like nobody is listening. For a long time I wondered why.

The reason is that if someone doesn't see themselves in the role in five years, a five-year plan doesn't mean much to them. And even if they do expect to be around, they're probably just not thinking that far ahead. It's very hard to get people to carry out a plan on a timescale that doesn't feel real to them.

## Why breaking it down isn't the full answer

The obvious fix is to break the strategy into smaller, short-term steps. That is more manageable, and it's probably a better way to get staff to act on it.

But there's a catch. A five-year strategy is really a broad direction for where the company is going. When you first put it together, you don't know all the details. You work a lot of it out as you go.

Once you start breaking it down into detailed sections, you're committing to more detail than you actually have, or want, at that stage. I'm not sure that suits a broad strategy at all.
