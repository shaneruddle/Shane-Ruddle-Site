Thailand wants to join the OECD, and that has a lot of foreigners hoping the rules on owning a business here will finally be relaxed. You might expect a foreign business owner like me to be cheering that on. I'm not, or at least not entirely. I actually don't mind the restrictions.

## What's happening

Thailand is aiming to join the OECD, the club of mostly wealthy economies, with 2028 as its target. The OECD has encouraged Thailand to reduce its barriers to foreign investment, especially in services. But joining doesn't automatically mean foreigners will be allowed to own any business outright.

I've written before about [whether the nominee crackdown is really about the OECD](/articles/is-the-nominee-crackdown-really-about-the-oecd). This piece is about what should come next.

## Why I don't mind the restrictions

The honest answer is competition. The current rules limit how many foreigners can set up and run businesses here, and that protects those of us who already have.

Of course, I'd like to own my companies outright. But if I can, so can everybody else. A complete, blanket 100% foreign ownership rule would open the floodgates.

## What I'd suggest instead

I don't think the answer is all or nothing. There are a couple of middle grounds that would make more sense to me.

**A 75/25 split.** Foreigners usually put up most of the money, so let them own 75%, with Thai shareholders holding the other 25%.

**Earn 100% ownership.** Or let foreigners own their business outright if they can show they've made a meaningful contribution to Thailand. That could mean:

- employing a certain number of Thai staff
- having been here a certain number of years
- paying taxes and running a legitimate business

I'll be upfront: it's easy for me to recommend that, because it would apply to me and not to a lot of other people. But I think that's the point. Ownership should go to people who are clearly here to stay and are contributing, not to anyone who turns up with a bit of money.

## The other side

Many would argue the opposite. Supporters of full liberalisation say open ownership would bring more investment, more jobs and better service, and that protecting existing owners isn't a good reason to keep foreigners out. Others worry that any foreign ownership above 49% would squeeze out Thai businesses altogether. And a points-style system would need to be run fairly and consistently, which isn't always easy.

*I'm not a lawyer. This is my opinion as a business owner, not legal advice.*

## My take

The OECD bid is a chance to make the rules clearer and fairer. I'd like to see that happen, but not through a blanket free-for-all. Reward the foreigners who commit to Thailand, and keep some of the protection that's served the country well.
