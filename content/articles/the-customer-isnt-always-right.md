The old saying is that the customer is always right. I completely disagree. It took me years in business to really accept that, but some customers are wrong, and when they are, your staff come first. More in [customer service and online reviews](/articles/customer-service-and-online-reviews).

## The regular nobody wanted to serve

We have a regular at one of my venues whom the staff dread serving. Nothing is ever good enough. At first his feedback was genuine and constructive, but over time it turned into constant complaining, with the truth stretched along the way.

The final straw came this week. He said he'd had a bad beer, so the staff replaced it, and he then claimed the second one was bad too. I later found out he never touched the second beer. He just left.

I think he actually likes the place, but it's often quiet when he's in. So he's decided we don't know what we're doing and he does, and that it's his job to tell us, and everyone else, how badly we and the staff are doing. More in [my worst customers](/articles/my-worst-customers).

## How I handled it

He always complained through our social media, so I sent him a private message. I told him he was now speaking to the owner, not the manager. I said I appreciated his feedback, but at some point I have to protect my staff. They need to be comfortable at work, and everything they'd tried to help him hadn't worked.

Then I said that maybe the venue just isn't for him, since we're clearly failing to meet his expectations. It was a polite way of saying it would be better if he didn't come back.

## Why the customer isn't always right

I don't think the saying was ever meant literally, but plenty of business owners take it that way. Some customers attack you for their own reasons, not yours. We've had people take it out on us because they're in a bad mood, maybe after a row with a partner, and later come back to apologise.

## Protect your staff

Your staff run the business. A company is just a collection of people, and they're the most important ones in it. If you always side with the customer over your staff, especially when the customer is wrong, you won't have staff for long.
