Pattaya's expat world is small. You never know when you'll meet someone again, who you both know, or whether one day the shoe will be on the other foot. That's a good reason to be courteous to everyone.

## Ten years later

I met a former customer recently whom I'd looked after more than 10 years ago at Pattaya Rent a Car. It got me thinking: if he'd been a nightmare customer back then, or if we'd given him poor service, that meeting would have been a lot more awkward. (I wrote about that meeting in [If I don't remember you, it doesn't mean you didn't matter](/articles/if-i-dont-remember-you).)

I think that's one reason expats tend to behave a bit better in foreign-owned businesses here. They never know when they'll run into the owner again, or whether you know the same people. It's different from walking into a coffee shop in a province where nobody knows you.

## The pro who wasn't helpful

When I first arrived in Pattaya, I went to the golf driving range where I was practising and asked to speak to the resident pro. I asked if there were any positions going, who I should speak to, and for a bit of advice.

He was friendly enough, but cold, and not very helpful at all.

About five years later, I had my own driving range, Fairways. And who came to me looking for a job? The same pro.

## It works the other way too

Good turns come back as well. A man walked into Alan Bolton looking for a property one day, while I happened to be in the office. He recognised me and reminded me of the first time we'd met, back in my Sportsman days. He still remembered that I'd been very forthcoming with advice, and he was really appreciative of it. He rented a condo through us, and said once he knew I was the owner, he wouldn't go anywhere else.

It happened again when I bought East Coast Real Estate. The previous owners took me to meet the accounting firm that handled the accounts, and the owner of that firm turned out to be someone I'd played golf with years before. He remembered me being helpful on the course, and apparently I'd bought him a beer after the round. That made the conversation, and the whole handover, much easier.

## Be helpful anyway

I'm usually on good behaviour anyway, because I know what it's like to run a business, and I have more patience than most. But the lesson applies to everyone: be courteous, professional and helpful to fellow expats.

You might know the same people. You might meet again. And one day, you might be the one asking for help.
