Young people have been protesting in Bangkok. I'm not going to wade into Thai politics, but I think the frustration behind it is part of something global. Around the world, young people feel their parents and grandparents had an easier, richer life than the one they've been handed. And now AI is taking away the one thing they could still count on: a job.

## What happened

On 20 November, around 200 young people, many still in school uniform, gathered at Bangkok's Democracy Monument, calling for educational, political and social reforms.

## A global story

This isn't just a Thai thing. For decades, governments in many countries have kicked the can down the road on debt and hard decisions, and it's the younger generation who are now paying for it.

Buying a home is a good example. It's far harder for young people now than it was for their parents or grandparents.

## The deal that's breaking down

Gen Z were told that if they went to university and got an education, a job would be waiting for them at the end, or at least they'd have a much better chance of getting one.

That's not what's happening. AI is already taking a lot of entry-level jobs, and it will take more. The cat's out of the bag, and there's no stopping it. For all the other struggles young people have had, at least they could get a job. That's no longer a given.

## What I'd say to a young person

Honestly, it's a tough one.

The easy answer is to learn something AI won't take over, like being an electrician or a plumber. But that isn't the world Gen Z grew up in. They grew up with computers and content creation. Asking them to put the laptop down and work with their hands is a huge reskill, and I think there'd be a lot of reluctance.

The other answer is to lean into AI. It isn't going anywhere, so you may as well learn how to use it and build skills around it. (More on how it's changed my own work in [AI took the boring work, and my brain lost its rest](/articles/ai-took-the-boring-work-and-my-brain-lost-its-rest).)

## What I'd say to the government

I don't claim to have the answers. Thailand has tried to encourage the use of AI, but in my opinion it was poorly executed.

What I do know is that the government needs to get in front of this and take it seriously. Most big changes in society have played out over decades. This one is happening very quickly. Some kind of support for young people through the transition seems essential.

It's all connected, too. As I wrote in [Thailand's population is shrinking](/articles/thailand-should-be-welcoming-families), the country needs more young families. But if young people feel hard done by, can't find work and can't get on the property ladder, having children is the last thing on their minds.

## The other side

Not everyone agrees that young people have it worse. Many older people point out that they also faced hard times, high interest rates and fewer opportunities, and that today's young people have technology and choices earlier generations never had. And some economists argue that AI will create new kinds of jobs, just as past technologies did, even if the transition is painful.

## My take

Whatever you think of the protests, the frustration behind them is real, and it isn't going away. A generation that can't find work or afford a home won't build the future any country needs.
