Many tenants rent direct from the landlord because they think they're saving money. That works until something goes wrong. Then there's no third party to step in, and for a small dispute, a lawyer isn't worth it. A recent case shows exactly why. More in [renting a condo in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-condo-in-pattaya).

## The one-star review

The tenant first rented a condo through one of my agencies. He slipped and fell in the condo, and the landlord agreed to cover some of his medical costs. Later the landlord changed his mind and said it was the tenant's own responsibility.

The tenant was unhappy, understandably, and gave our agency a one-star review, even though the landlord's decision had nothing to do with us.

It was early on after I took over the agency, and the amount was small, around ฿2,400. I don't normally get involved, but I called him, explained our side, and said I'd cover the medical costs, because one of my staff had technically done something slightly wrong. I paid it, and he removed the review. More in [how I handle an unfair one-star review](/articles/how-i-handle-an-unfair-one-star-review).

## Going direct

For his next apartment, he rented direct from a landlady, with no agency involved. Now she has sold the apartment and wants him out, even though he has prepaid some rent.

From what he's told me, he's well within his rights. It should be a clear-cut case. But he has nowhere to go.

## Why a lawyer won't help

He asked me for advice. The honest answer is that a lawyer isn't worth it. The amount is too small, so the fees would cost more than he'd get back. And most lawyers wouldn't give a dispute this size much attention anyway.

## What an agency would have done

If he'd rented through an agency, we would have mediated. To a landlady, a single tenant has little leverage. But an agency is a business she may want to work with again. Behaving badly could cost her future business with us, with our sister agency, and with other agencies we talk to.

With that at stake, I'm fairly sure she'd do the right thing, and probably wouldn't try it on in the first place. Dealing with one individual tenant, some landlords feel they can act as they like.

## My advice

If you're renting in Pattaya, insist the agreement goes through a third party. You may think you're saving money by going direct, but with our agencies the landlord always pays the fee, so for a tenant it costs no more to go through an agency. I'd go further: even if you find a property direct with a landlord, still insist it goes through an agency. That way you have someone in your corner if things go wrong.
