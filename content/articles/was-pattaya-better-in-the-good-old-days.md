Ask long-term expats and many will tell you Pattaya was better 20 years ago. I've been here that long, and honestly, I agree, though it's not as simple as rose-tinted nostalgia. (For the other side of the argument, read [why Pattaya's old reputation is out of date](/articles/pattaya-old-reputation-out-of-date) and [where Pattaya is heading](/articles/where-pattaya-is-heading).)

## What was better

Pattaya was much quieter. Jomtien was small, and roads like Jomtien Second Road and Pattaya's Third Road were nothing like they are now. Everything was smaller, there were fewer tourists, and most people were older.

That meant less chaos and less rowdiness. People just went about their business. Today there are many more younger visitors. Nothing wrong with that, but it's a completely different crowd, with a lot more energy than the old expats had.

Back then hardly any foreigners worked here, either. The work was left to Thais, and the foreigners were nearly all retirees. Now plenty of people are trying to make a living here, and that changes the feel of the city. More in [finding a job in Pattaya](/articles/finding-a-job-in-pattaya).

## What was worse

It was still chaotic, just in a different way. It wasn't crowds, it was rules being broken everywhere. Drink-driving, for example, was illegal but almost everyone did it. It really was the Wild West. Today it can land you in a cell: [what happens if you get arrested in Pattaya](/articles/what-happens-if-you-get-arrested-in-pattaya).

To be honest, that was part of the appeal for many of us. Life in the West had become all paperwork and bureaucracy, and here you could get things done without the red tape. A lot of people miss that. But it came with real risks. More in [my tips for the road in Pattaya](/articles/my-tips-for-the-road-in-pattaya).

## Would I go back?

It's a tough one. Back then I felt a bit like a big fish in a small pond. Now I'm just another person in a much bigger city. That has its benefits: more business opportunities, more people to meet and far more to do. There really wasn't much to do back then. But a big city brings its own problems too.

If I had to choose, yes, I'd go back 20 years to the good old days. Then again, I'd also be 20 years younger, so who wouldn't?
