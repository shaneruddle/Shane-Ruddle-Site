The honest answer is no, I never fully switch off. But the same thing that stops me switching off is what lets me get away at all. It's a double-edged sword. More in [a day in my life](/articles/a-day-in-my-life).

## Why I can't switch off

My phone is always on. I have children and family, so I can't turn it off in case there's an emergency. And with it on, notifications come through all the time, even on holiday.

I think it's the same for most business owners now. Before mobile phones and the internet, you could go abroad and nobody could reach you. You'd set an auto-reply saying you were away for two weeks and would reply when you got back. That isn't good enough any more. People expect instant replies, and if you made customers wait a week or two, I don't think you'd stay in business long. More in [customer service and online reviews](/articles/customer-service-and-online-reviews).

## A change is as good as a rest

I can still switch off partly, and travel is how I do it. They say a change is as good as a rest, and I believe it. I've been to China a couple of times this year. I was still doing some work, but being somewhere completely different put me in a different frame of mind. The new environment keeps you occupied.

## The double-edged sword

The phone, the notifications and the internet are the reason I can never fully switch off. They're also the reason I can travel at all. Because I can work from anywhere, I can go away or change location far more often than I could if I had to be in Pattaya to keep things running.
