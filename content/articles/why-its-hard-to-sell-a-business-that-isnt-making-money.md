Everyone wants to buy a business that's making money. Nobody wants to sell one. So most businesses for sale are the ones that aren't doing well, and selling one of those is hard before you've even found a buyer, because first you have to tell the world.

## Everyone wants the profitable one

There's a funny paradox in buying and selling businesses. Buyers want something that's already making money, but those are almost never for sale. Occasionally someone is retiring or relocating, but even then people are sceptical. They automatically assume there's another reason. More in [what selling 12 businesses in Pattaya taught me](/articles/what-selling-12-businesses-in-pattaya-taught-me).

## Advertising your failure

For me, the hardest part is the advertising itself. It's an ego thing. Maybe it isn't for everyone, but when you put a struggling business up for sale, you're effectively advertising your failure.

And it invites criticism. If someone once had a bad experience at a restaurant and then sees it's for sale, it's their chance to say: I told you so, I gave you that feedback. People don't see that there's usually a lot more to it than that.

Facebook is the best place to sell a business in Pattaya. It's also where all the negative comments come in. I think a lot of owners have seen that happen to others, and it makes them reluctant to advertise at all. More in [why Pattaya's expat Facebook groups are so toxic](/articles/why-pattaya-expat-facebook-groups-are-so-toxic).

## How I go about it

My process is to widen the circle slowly:

1. **People I know.** I start with friends and contacts who might be interested.
2. **People known to me.** Then other business owners in my wider network.
3. **Lower the price.** If there are no bites, I look again at what I'm asking.
4. **The open market, anonymously.** Only as a last resort, and without naming the business. Having several businesses helps here, because people can't tell which one it is.

## Offer a partnership instead

Another approach is to offer a partnership rather than a sale. It's a lot less scary for buyers. Because I'd be staying in, I have every reason to be completely transparent: we're going to be partners. More in [business partners in Pattaya](/articles/business-partners-in-pattaya).

It isn't deceitful. If I offer a partnership, I genuinely mean it. But once you're talking, it sometimes becomes clear that the best fit is for them to take it on alone, or that a partnership wouldn't work. Then a sale is on the table. It's a way of starting the conversation.

## The lesson

If you're selling a business that's struggling, expect it to be hard, and expect some of the hardest part to be in your own head. Start quietly, with people you trust, and only go public when you have to. More in [why the restaurant business is the toughest I've been in](/articles/why-the-restaurant-business-is-the-toughest).
