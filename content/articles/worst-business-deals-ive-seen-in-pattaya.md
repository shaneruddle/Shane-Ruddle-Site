In more than 20 years here I've seen plenty of people lose money on bad deals. I'm keeping these anonymous, because the point isn't who, it's why. The common thread surprised me. (I've made my own mistakes too: read [my biggest business failure](/articles/my-biggest-business-failure).)

## The ten-million-baht upstairs bar

A guy was persuaded by his girlfriend to rent the empty upstairs of a busy bar. The space had never been used. The idea was a separate, more VIP-style venue with its own brand.

Anyone who's been in business knows that getting people to go upstairs is extremely difficult. He had no real experience, and it didn't even last six months.

He also lost far more than he needed to. People could see he was inexperienced and too trusting, so he was overcharged by almost everyone, from the electrician to the sound engineer to the stock supplier. He spent close to ฿10 million on a fit-out that should probably have cost half that. More in [opening a restaurant in Pattaya](/articles/opening-a-restaurant-in-pattaya).

## The crypto "opportunity"

Another man had just sold his business. He then handed about ฿4 million to someone promising returns way above anything normal, in a crypto-type investment. Maybe it was wishful thinking, believing he was getting in on the ground floor. The returns never came, and he lost everything.

This happens more than you'd think. Pattaya has a lot of people trying to reinvent themselves, and it's often hard to know someone's history. It also has a lot of people who are a bit too trusting. More in [scams in Pattaya](/articles/scams-in-pattaya).

## The common thread

It looks like poor judgement of character, but really it's not knowing people well enough. Pattaya is transient. I have friends I've known for 20 years, but only because I've been here that long. If you're going into business or an investment here, there's a good chance you've known the other person for a few years at most. It's very easy to get your head turned. More in [business partners in Pattaya](/articles/business-partners-in-pattaya).

## My advice

Take it slow and don't rush into anything. Ask around. Work out who the person is before you worry about the deal they're offering.

I'd go further: back the person, not the idea. If you find someone with a reputation for being trustworthy, that's who you should invest or start a business with, even if their idea isn't brilliant. A trustworthy person with an average idea is a far better bet than someone with a fantastic idea and no track record of being trusted. A good idea can be fixed. A bad partner can't.
