I'll be honest. Some days I feel like jacking it all in and calling it a day. Most of the time the feeling passes before I could ever act on it. And I've learned that the trick isn't to never feel it. It's to know what it is when it turns up.

## Where it comes from

I don't think there's one cause. It's a mixture of everything.

When a problem lands, business owners deal with it, because it has to be dealt with. There's no time to feel stressed about it in the moment. But the stress doesn't disappear. It gets deferred. It turns up later.

Then another problem comes along, and that stress gets deferred too. If they come in quick succession, it stacks up. Add a few nights of bad sleep, often for the same reasons, and it all builds until you find yourself in one of those moods. You look at everything you've built and think: why am I doing this? (I've written before about [when being the owner doesn't feel worth it](/articles/when-being-the-owner-doesnt-feel-worth-it).)

## I have walked away before

This isn't just talk. I have sold up before, because I decided enough was enough. Looking back, it was the right call, because the feeling wasn't a one-off. It kept coming back. (More on that in [what selling 12 businesses in Pattaya taught me](/articles/what-selling-12-businesses-in-pattaya-taught-me).)

It's more complicated now. I'm more invested than I used to be, and you can't just walk away from that on a bad Tuesday.

Right now I've got one business where I'm not even really looking at the numbers anymore. I'm looking at how it makes me feel. Any mention of the place winds me up. When it gets to that point, it's not really about spreadsheets. It's about whether something is worth what it costs you.

## A bad week or a real "enough is enough"

So how do you tell the difference? For me, it comes down to two things.

First, does the feeling keep coming back? A bad week passes. If the same business, the same problem, keeps dragging you back to the same place month after month, that's telling you something.

Second, am I overreacting? I know myself well enough to notice when I start letting things get to me more than they should. When small things set me off, that's a sign it's gone past a normal rough patch.

If it's both, it might be time to make a real decision. If it's neither, it's probably just a bad week.

## Don't take it out on the people around you

This is the part I find hardest, and I'm saying it to myself as much as anyone.

When you're in that mood, it's very easy to take it out on whoever is nearest. Staff, family, your partner. It isn't their fault. They didn't cause the problems, and most of the time they're just trying to help.

I'm not perfect at this. On the day I talked this article through, someone close to me mentioned that business, and I snapped. Not because of anything they did, but because that one word was a trigger. I knew it straight away, and that's the point. You won't always catch it before it happens, but you can catch it after, own it, and say sorry.

## Wait it out

My advice, if you're in that spot right now, is simple. Wait it out.

Recognise that you're in a bad place, and that the bad place is talking. Things usually aren't as bad as they feel at 2am after a week of problems. The mood swings back. It nearly always does.

Don't sell, sack someone or make a big decision in the middle of it. If the feeling is still there in a few months, and it keeps coming back, then make the call with a clear head. I've done that before, and I don't regret it.

But most days, the answer is to get some sleep, be decent to the people around you, and let it pass.
