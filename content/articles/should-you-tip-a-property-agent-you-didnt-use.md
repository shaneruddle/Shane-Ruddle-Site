A good friend of mine, Andy, asked me a question recently that I think a lot of people looking for a property in Pattaya should ask themselves. If an agent works hard for you and you end up renting through someone else, should you give them something for their trouble?

My answer was yes, absolutely.

## What happened

Andy was looking for a place through Alan Bolton. One of our agents showed him round four or five properties. She organised the viewings, made sure the places were clean and ready, and went out of her way for him.

In the end he didn't settle on any of them. He went with an agent from another company, on basically the first property that agent showed him.

That doesn't mean our agent did anything wrong, or didn't understand what Andy wanted. Sometimes it just works out that way.

## The work you don't see

A lot of people assume viewings are easy: the agent has the keys, unlimited access, and everything's ready to go. I can assure you it isn't like that. There's a lot of organising behind every viewing.

So the agent who came in at the end got all the commission, and ours got nothing. She was actually out of pocket, because she drives her own car and pays for her own fuel.

That's unfair. It works the other way too. Sometimes another agent does all the work and the client ends up renting through us. There's no exclusivity here. Nobody signs a contract saying they'll only use one agent, so it can be a bit dog-eat-dog.

## A fair tip

Andy felt bad about it, which is why he asked me. I told him that if someone has done a good job for you and put themselves out, and you didn't use them through no fault of theirs, a tip is not unreasonable.

At the very least, it should cover their fuel. It shouldn't be so much that you set a precedent for yourself every time you deal with someone. I suggested 1,000 baht. It's a round number, it isn't a lot of money to most Westerners, and it covers some fuel and a bit of their time.

Yes, agents are on a salary, and it's part of their job. But if you come back to Pattaya and need a property again, that agent will keep working hard for you, rather than remembering you as the client who wasted their time.

## It isn't really about the money

To be clear, I'm not touting for tips here, and I'm not saying everyone should give one. What I'm saying is: think about what someone has done for you, the time they've put in, and everything that goes on behind the scenes just to organise a viewing. Don't completely discard that just because you went with another agent.

Show a bit of compassion. At the very least, thank them. Tell them you're sorry you didn't go with them, but another agent showed you something ideal. Then ask if there's anything you can do to help them and their career, whether that's a recommendation, a five-star Google review or a tip.

## My take

You're not obliged to tip, and you shouldn't feel you have to every time. But when an agent has genuinely gone out of their way and you go elsewhere, a small thank you goes a long way.
