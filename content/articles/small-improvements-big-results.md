I've always believed in small, steady improvements. On their own they don't look like much, but after months or years they add up to something significant.

## Why Atomic Habits hit home

When I read *Atomic Habits* by James Clear, it resonated with me because it describes how I've always worked. I liked it so much that I tracked down the Thai version and bought about a dozen copies for my staff.

## Where I learned it

I learned it through golf. Anything I've tried to master, I've broken down into very small improvements and worked on one at a time. I've carried that same approach into business.

## The portal: built one day at a time

The best example is the portal I built for Alan Bolton and East Coast Real Estate. I rebuilt the entire back end from scratch, working on it every day and adding new features and improvements almost daily.

After six months or more, we now have a system that I think is better than anything else in the local market. If I'd looked at it as one huge project, it would probably have felt like too much to take on.

## What I want from my staff

I'd like my staff to work the same way. Rather than looking at something that seems big, break it down and work on it one step at a time. Keep improving the way you operate, and keep contributing ideas to the way the business runs.

## Did they read it?

I delivered the books to the office myself and explained what the book was about. Everyone was very thankful. Luckily, one of the team had already read it, so she explained to the others in Thai that it was a really good book and they should all read it.

I was leaving for England for a month the next day, so I told them I'd check whether they'd all read it when I got back. Of course, I never did check. I later found out that none of them had read it.

Which, in a way, proves the point: change doesn't come from one big gesture. It comes from small habits, repeated.

## My take

You don't need a big breakthrough. Just get a little bit better every day, and give it time.
