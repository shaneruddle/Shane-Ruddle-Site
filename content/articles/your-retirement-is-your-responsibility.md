If you come to Thailand as a young man and build your working life here, there's no safety net waiting for you at retirement. Nobody is saving for you. If you don't plan it yourself, you can reach your fifties with almost nothing, and that's a trap I've watched people fall into.

## What you leave behind

Back in the UK, most people get a kind of forced saving without really thinking about it. It comes in two forms.

The first is a pension: the state pension, and usually a workplace pension too. The second is the housing ladder. You pay a mortgage for years, and by retirement you normally own your home outright, with the equity in it.

So by 65, most people have some income from the government and a paid-off house. Come to Thailand young, and you don't get that automatically. You have to build it yourself.

## What happens to the UK state pension

You don't lose it completely. You'll get whatever you built up from the years you paid National Insurance, and you may be able to top it up with voluntary contributions while you're living abroad. The rules on that have tightened, so check what applies to you.

But if you retire in Thailand, the UK state pension is frozen. It stays at the rate it was when you first claimed it, and it doesn't rise with inflation each year. As Thailand gets more expensive, it buys less and less. Don't count on it being enough.

## The trap

The trap is simple. People spend years here living for today, often close to the breadline, and never put anything aside.

Then they hit 50 or 55 and realise they have nothing saved and no safety net for 65. So they go back home to earn some money, to top up their pension or build some savings. Very often they end up working much longer than they ever planned, because they didn't save while they were working here.

## What I'm doing

I'm building my businesses, and I own property here. That's my safety net, and I started on it early.

Your route might be different. What matters is that you have one, and that you start it while you're young.

## My advice

- **Start early.** The earlier you start, the easier it gets later on.
- **Don't rely on the state pension.** If you're retiring in Thailand, inflation will eat away at it.
- **Build your own safety net.** Savings, property, a business, investments. Whatever suits you, but build something.
- **Check your National Insurance record.** See whether voluntary contributions make sense for you.
- **Take advice.** Talk to a qualified financial adviser about your own situation.

The crux of it is this. When you build your life in Thailand, the responsibility for your retirement moves from the government to you.

I'm not a financial adviser, and this isn't financial advice. It's just what I've seen in 20-odd years here.
