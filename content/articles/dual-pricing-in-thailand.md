Foreigners in Thailand often pay far more than Thais to get into national parks and attractions. At Khao Yai, for example, it's ฿40 for a Thai adult and ฿400 for a foreigner. I wouldn't call it fair, but I don't think the idea behind it is wrong. It's the way Thailand does it that's the problem. More in [Khao Yai from Pattaya](/articles/khao-yai-from-pattaya).

## It happens everywhere, just less obviously

All over the world, residents pay less than tourists. Other countries just do it differently, usually with some kind of ID or resident card that gets you a discount. What they don't do is look at you and decide that because you're clearly not local, you pay more.

## The simple fix

Set one price for everyone, say ฿200 for a national park. Then give a discount to anyone who shows a Thai ID card, and to anyone who pays tax here, like foreigners with a work permit. Everybody else pays the full price.

That achieves exactly the same goal, looking after Thai taxpayers and residents. It's far better than a permanent carved wooden sign at the entrance that basically says Thais pay this and foreigners pay that.

## Discounts are normal, charging more by nationality isn't

None of my businesses charge foreigners more, and I would never even consider it. It would be extremely foolish. As far as I know, other businesses here don't either.

What's perfectly normal is giving a discount to a certain group. At Hemingways Lakeside we give teachers 20% off if they can show they're a teacher. Businesses in the UK do the same for nurses and the armed forces. That's very different from charging someone more because of their nationality.

## Does it put tourists off?

I don't think it stops anyone coming. If dual pricing alone stops you visiting a country, maybe you need to take a look at yourself. But it leaves a sour taste, a bit like the long immigration queue when you first arrive. It's not a good first impression, and it's one more small thing on top of everything else. More in [why tourism in Thailand is down](/articles/why-tourism-in-thailand-is-down).

For me it's a PR problem more than anything. Thailand can look after its own taxpayers without it, so it's completely unnecessary. It's on my list in [if I were mayor of Pattaya](/articles/if-i-were-mayor-of-pattaya) too.
