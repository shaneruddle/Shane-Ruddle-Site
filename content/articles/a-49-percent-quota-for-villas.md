Foreigners in Thailand can own a condo outright, but not a house. I think there's a simple fix, and Thailand already uses it: apply the condo quota to housing developments.

## Where it came from

When I compared [buying property in Thailand and Vietnam](/articles/thailand-vs-vietnam-property-ownership), one thing stood out. Vietnam lets foreigners own houses in approved housing projects, but controls how many by capping foreign-owned houses at 250 per local ward.

Thailand already controls foreign ownership in a similar way, with the 49% condo quota. So why not do the same for villas?

## How it would work

Take a housing development with 100 villas. Under my idea, up to 49% of it could be owned by foreigners. That could be measured by the number of villas, so 49 of the 100, or by floor area, the same way condos are.

The structure would mirror a condo:

- **The foreigner owns the house**, freehold and forever, in their own name.
- **The land stays Thai.** It would be held by the development's juristic person, just as the land under a condo building is.
- **The land can't be sold off.** It's part of the development, so it couldn't be separated and used for something else.

Selling and inheriting would work exactly as it does for a condo. You'd effectively own the property, and you could sell it or pass it on under the same rules.

## Going a step further: owning the plot

You could even go further and let foreigners own the land under their villa freehold as well. Because the plot sits inside an approved housing estate, it's still controlled. It can't be bought up, combined with other land or turned into something else.

## It's all about control

That's the real point. Thailand doesn't want a free-for-all, and I understand why. Nobody wants big investors coming in, buying up all the beachfront land and driving prices up.

Keeping foreign ownership inside approved developments solves that. Just as larger condo projects need an environmental impact assessment, every development would be approved in advance. The authorities could make sure it wasn't on beachfront land, or anywhere else sensitive, and that it met all the other rules and regulations.

Thailand wants to offer a path for foreign investment, but it has to be controllable. This would be.

## Why it makes sense

The big objection to foreigners owning houses in Thailand has always been about the land. This gets around that. From a sovereignty point of view, the land is still owned by Thailand. The foreigner just owns the house on it, exactly as they already own a condo unit without owning the land under the tower.

And it keeps control. No development could ever be more than 49% foreign-owned, so there's no risk of whole estates going to foreigners.

It would also give foreigners a secure, legal alternative to the 30-year leases and company structures many rely on now.

## It isn't a new idea

Vietnam already does something very similar. Foreigners can only buy houses there within approved housing projects, and the numbers are kept under control with a cap on foreign-owned houses in each local ward. (Vietnam is now considering scrapping house ownership for foreigners in a draft law, so it may not last there.)

And Thailand already has a version of it. Some villa developments in Phuket sell to foreigners on 30-year leases with renewals offered. The renewals aren't legally guaranteed, but because the villa is part of a development of hundreds of homes, I think they're effectively guaranteed. A developer couldn't just start refusing to renew certain villas in the middle of an estate.

So the path is already there. But owning your own house and plot freehold, rather than relying on a 30-year lease, sits a lot better with foreigners. I'm convinced it would attract far more foreign investment.

## The other side

Critics would say it could push up house prices in popular areas and make homes less affordable for Thai families. Some would see any foreign ownership of houses as a step too far, whoever holds the land. And it would need proper rules for things like inheritance, common fees and what happens if a development is redeveloped.

*I'm not a lawyer. This is my idea and opinion, not legal advice. Under current Thai law, leases are limited to 30 years and renewal promises aren't guaranteed.*

## My take

Thailand has already shown, with condos, that controlled foreign ownership works. Extending the same model to villa developments would bring in investment and give foreigners real security, while Thailand keeps control of the land.
