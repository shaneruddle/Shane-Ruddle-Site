A business broker I've known for close to 20 years told me recently how he got into the trade. It's a good reminder that in Pattaya, if you treat someone badly, the fact that they can't sue you doesn't mean they can't hurt you.

## A bad first deal

Twenty-odd years ago, he wanted to buy a business. He found one through a local broker and paid a 10% deposit.

Then he started to realise the business wasn't what he'd been told. The owner didn't honour the original inventory and began taking things out of the business. Nobody told him. He found out for himself.

Naturally, he wanted to back out and asked for his deposit back. The broker refused.

## No way to fight it

He went to see a lawyer. To the lawyer's credit, he was honest. Yes, there was a case. But the legal fees would probably cost more than the deposit he'd lost. His advice was to forget about it.

So he had no real recourse. The broker kept the money.

## Doing it better

Instead of letting it go, he decided he could do a better job than that broker, and do it with some ethics. He had no experience, but he was looking for something to do. So he opened his own brokerage and went into competition with the man who'd turned him over.

Within a year, the old broker was out of business. My friend has been running his brokerage for around 20 years since.

## The lessons

For anyone in business here: be careful who you turn over. Just because someone has no legal way to come after you doesn't mean they can't come after your customers and your income instead.

And for anyone buying a business:

- **Check the inventory yourself** before you hand over a deposit, and again before completion.
- **Get the deposit terms in writing.** Know exactly when it's refundable, and under what conditions.
- **Choose your broker carefully.** Ask around. In a place this small, people will tell you who to trust.
