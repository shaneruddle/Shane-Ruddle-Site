When a member of staff gets something wrong, it reflects on the business, and because my name is on it, it reflects on me. I understand that. But I think customers need a bit more sympathy for the fact that, if you have a lot of staff, things won't always go to plan.

## What happened

Recently, at one of my property businesses, a member of staff didn't do a checkout properly. The deposit was handed back too quickly, before everything had been checked, and it turned out some items were missing. That's now caused problems for the customer and for the company.

The procedure was there. It just wasn't followed. And yet the flak for being unprofessional lands on me.

## Where my responsibility starts and ends

I do have a responsibility. It's my job to train my staff and to put proper procedures in place, and I take that seriously.

But if a member of staff then doesn't follow those procedures, there's only so much I can do. Staff will never care about a business as much as the owner does. Some will cut corners, some will make honest mistakes, and occasionally someone will go rogue. With enough people, it's going to happen.

## Every business gets it wrong

This isn't unique to small businesses. Some of the biggest companies in the UK are notoriously bad at customer service and get things wrong all the time. Even the most valuable companies in the world, run by some of the most successful people, make mistakes.

And let's be honest: most people who own companies, big or small, don't see what happens day to day. Being the owner doesn't mean you're standing next to every member of staff for every task.

## How I handle it

When something goes wrong, I explain to the customer that these things happen. People who run their own businesses usually understand straight away. Others don't. They think that if something can be done properly, it should be done properly.

And I agree with them. It should. The fact is, it just doesn't always work like that, in any business.

## The other side

From the customer's point of view, it doesn't matter whose fault it was. They dealt with the business, and the business got it wrong. That's why it still falls to the owner to put it right, even if the owner didn't make the mistake.

## My take

I'll always take responsibility for training my staff and fixing what goes wrong. But mistakes by staff aren't the same as an unprofessional owner. A little understanding goes a long way.
