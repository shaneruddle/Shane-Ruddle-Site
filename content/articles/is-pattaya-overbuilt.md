People often ask me if Pattaya has too many condos. My answer is no. If anything holds the city back, it's the roads and infrastructure, not the number of buildings. More in [the Pattaya property market](/articles/pattaya-property-market).

## The real limit is infrastructure

On holiday weekends, when people from neighbouring provinces and Bangkok come down, the city bursts at the seams and getting around is hard. The rest of the time, it's still not bad. As long as infrastructure keeps pace with new developments, I think there's a long way to go yet. More in [Pattaya traffic](/articles/pattaya-traffic).

## Why so many windows are dark

Drive past any condo late at night and you'll see very few lights on. It's easy to think the building must be half empty and unsold. It isn't.

A lot of these units belong to holidaymakers: people who love Thailand, or want somewhere away from the weather back home. They pay something like $50,000, which isn't a huge sum for many people in the West, and use it on their regular trips. That might be two or three weeks a year, or a month. That's enough for them.

In my view it often beats paying for hotels here, which I think are overpriced. Many condos have facilities that compare with a hotel's. If you buy for $50,000 and sell ten years later for the same, you've effectively had free accommodation for ten years. If you'll keep coming back, that's money well spent. More in [off-plan vs resale condos](/articles/off-plan-vs-resale-condos-in-pattaya).

## What would change my mind

Falling prices. If condo prices started coming down, I'd worry. From what I see in our agencies, they're not. To be fair, some recent reports talk about unsold stock in newer projects, especially around Jomtien, and developers offering discounts to shift it, so it's something I keep an eye on.

When one group of buyers fades, another seems to appear. After the war in Ukraine began, many Russians moved money out of Russia and parked it in Pattaya condos. That has eased off over the last couple of years, but new buyers keep arriving from other parts of the world, often people looking for somewhere stable away from conflict at home.

And even when buyers visit less than they planned, they rarely sell. Owning a place in another country sits well with people, even if it stays empty eleven months of the year.
