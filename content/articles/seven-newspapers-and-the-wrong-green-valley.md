Things get lost in translation in Thailand all the time. Usually it's innocent and funny. Occasionally it costs you. Here are two of mine, one of each. (For another, see [the Batman story](/articles/lost-in-translation-batman).)

## Seven copies of the Bangkok Post

At Cajun Life Cafe we decided to put out physical newspapers for customers, the Bangkok Post, like some other restaurants do, including Hemingways.

On the first day we asked our delivery driver to go and buy one and bring it to the shop. He did.

The next day, no newspaper. The manager asked why. He said he didn't realise we wanted it every day. She told him yes, we want it every day, seven days a week.

So the next morning he went out and came back with seven copies of the same newspaper.

## The wrong Green Valley

The second one wasn't funny at the time.

Back when I ran a driving range, I also ran a golf society from it. One day we had about 20 players: eight in one minibus, eight in another, and a few in my car. We all left within about five minutes of each other, heading for Green Valley golf course in Rayong, roughly a 45 to 50-minute drive.

I arrived with one minibus. The other didn't. After 15 minutes or so I rang the driver. He said he was about 25 minutes away. On a 50-minute journey, that didn't add up. The passengers were tourists, so they had no idea where they were meant to be going.

Another 15 minutes later, our tee time was close, so I rang again. He said he was 10 minutes away. It turned out he'd driven to the Green Valley course near Bangkok, not Green Valley in Rayong.

## The cost

That group missed their tee time completely. It was an afternoon tee time, so there was no chance of starting later. Those tourists had come over for a two-week golf holiday, and one of their days was ruined. We had to refund them.

Nobody saw the funny side. I was angry, and the driver lost a lot of face, with our staff and with the customers. It was an innocent mistake, but a costly one.

## The lesson

Most of the time, a misunderstanding here is harmless, and you'll laugh about it later. But when something matters, don't assume your instruction has been understood the way you meant it. Be specific, spell out the details that could be confused, and check before it's too late.
