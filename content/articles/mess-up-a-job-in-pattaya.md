If you're a foreigner working in Pattaya, here's something to understand early. The business community is small, and everyone knows everyone. If you get let go from a job here, you've just made the next one a lot harder to get.

## A recent example

A foreigner worked at a friend's restaurant and was eventually let go. I don't know all the details, and I don't need to. He went back to the UK, realised he missed Thailand, and decided he wanted to come back and work here again.

He applied to one of my businesses. It didn't take long to find out where he'd worked before. So, naturally, we asked his old boss for a reference. The answer was that he'd been difficult to work with.

That was the end of that.

## Why it matters so much here

Back home, you can leave a job badly, move to another town, and start again. Here, the owners and managers in the expat business world all know each other. Many of us are friends. A quick phone call is all it takes, and it's very likely to happen.

So your reputation follows you, whether you mention your last job or not.

## How to leave the right way

Sometimes a job just doesn't work out. Maybe you can't connect with the staff, or you don't like the way the place is run. That's fine. What matters is how you go.

If you plan on staying in Pattaya, leaving on good terms matters far more than it would back home. In a big country with lots of jobs, a bad exit gets lost in the crowd. Here it's cliquey, the pool of jobs is smaller, and everybody knows everybody. If you're moving to another part of Thailand, it may matter less. But if you're staying here, leave on good terms, no matter what.

In practice, that means giving proper notice, handing over properly, and having an honest, calm conversation with your boss about why it isn't working. Don't walk out, and don't badmouth the place afterwards. The person you leave behind could be the reference that gets you your next job.

## My advice

- **Don't mess up in the first place.** Turn up, work hard, and be easy to work with. It sounds obvious, but it's the best protection you have.
- **If it isn't working out, leave on good terms.** Give notice, hand over properly, and don't burn bridges on the way out.
- **Have an honest story ready.** Be able to explain clearly why a previous job didn't work out.
- **Be upfront.** If you left on bad terms, raise it yourself in the interview. Assume the question will come up, and get ahead of it. If you try to hide it, the person interviewing you probably knows your old boss quite well, and will find out anyway.

## The bottom line

In a small place like Pattaya, every job is also a reference for the next one. Treat it that way, and if something does go wrong, be honest about it. Being upfront won't always get you the job, but being caught hiding something almost certainly won't.
