When something goes wrong at one of my businesses, people who know me often want me to sort it out personally, because I'm the owner. I understand why. But in most cases I'm not the best person to fix it. I don't work in my businesses day to day, so I'm usually the least informed person involved. ## A friend, a water bill and four unanswered messages

A friend I've known for about 15 years, who lives overseas, recently found out I own the property agency he'd rented his house out through. The tenancy had run for 18 months.

When the tenant moved out, my staff weren't as thorough as they should have been at checkout. They returned the deposit before the final water bill arrived. This house gets two water bills: one for the house, and one from the village that only comes every three months. Now there's an unpaid bill, and the agency can't reach the tenant.

The bill bothered him a bit. What really bothered him was the lack of communication. He'd sent four messages without a reply. When he found out I was the owner, he called me to let me know.

That's fair. It's a mistake on our side, and it will be put right. But I didn't step in and handle it myself. I told him my manager would call him and get it squared away.

## A birthday party at one of my venues

The week before, another friend wanted to hold a birthday party at one of my venues, and wanted me to organise it because he knows me. I had to explain that it's not something I know about or do. Yes, I'm the owner, but that's what I employ people for.

I put him in touch with the manager. There was a bit of a communication problem, and now he wants to deal with me again. So I keep having to push back, politely.

## Why it rarely ends well when I get involved

Whenever I've stepped in personally in the past, it has rarely ended well. The problem is the follow-up.

When I get involved, I hear one side of the story, but I don't know the ins and outs. It takes time to piece together what actually happened. Some issues run for days, weeks or even months. Because I'm not there day to day, I miss a lot along the way. I start as the least informed person, catch up, and then fall behind again by the time a decision is needed.

People expect the owner to jump in and make a brilliant decision. But I don't have all the pieces.

There's a second problem. If I take over, I undermine the person I've employed to do that job.

## A mistake I've made: reacting to one side of the story

I've also made the mistake of acting on what someone told me without getting the full picture. It might be a customer I've met at padel or on the golf course. Because I know them a little, I take their word for it and react.

Say someone tells me they used to be a regular at one of my bars, but started getting a bad feeling from the staff and the manager, so they stopped going. My first thought is that I need to look into it, because no customer should feel that way. Then I speak to the staff and the manager and hear the other side. Sometimes it turns out that customer caused real problems, and the team were glad to see the back of him. He has no idea.

That's why I've learned not to react to one version of events, however much I like the person telling it.

## What I do instead

I hand it back to the person who should be accountable for it, and I let them know the customer has reached out to me. That usually means it gets handled with extra care and attention, and the right person sees it through from start to finish. ## Go through the proper channels

By all means tell me if something has gone wrong, especially if it's something I can change at a higher level. Friends tell me all the time that their eggs weren't cooked right at one of my restaurants. Often they're just telling me, and that's fine.

But if you want it fixed, speak to the staff first. If that doesn't work, ask for the manager. The manager isn't always on site, but the staff are, and that's what they're there for. If it really needs the owner, the manager will look into it first and then come to me with the full picture. That's how it should work, and it gets you a better result.

There aren't many situations where an owner genuinely needs to step in. Going through the proper channels isn't me passing you off. It's the best way to get your problem solved properly.
