This follows on from my piece on the stereotype that [every Thai woman in Pattaya works in the bars](/articles/not-every-thai-woman-in-pattaya-is-a-bar-girl). Most don't. But some do, and people often ask me why. From what I've seen over 22 years, the main reason is money.

## Money and family

It can be well-paid work. Many of the women I've met come from Isan, a rural and poorer part of Thailand. Earning good money in Pattaya and sending it home is seen as something honourable, a daughter looking after her family.

There's often pressure from home, especially since there's very little help from the state here. But in my experience that pressure is to go out and earn money, not necessarily to do this particular work. More in [relationships in Pattaya](/articles/relationships-in-pattaya).

## A young person's job

I see it as a young person's industry. Unless someone is trapped in it for some reason, I don't think many women do it much past 30.

## The social side

There's another reason people don't like to talk about: many of the women I've seen seem to enjoy it. They're out at night with friends, listening to music, chatting with foreigners and having a drink, the things young people do in other countries, except they're being paid. That's my observation, not everyone's experience. More in [nightlife in Pattaya](/articles/nightlife-in-pattaya).

## What outsiders get wrong

A lot of people assume every woman in the bar scene is being exploited, has no choice and hates it. That's not what I see day to day. Many work by choice and have a good time with their friends.

But it would be wrong to pretend the dark side doesn't exist. Exploitation, debt bondage and trafficking do happen in Thailand, and some people are trapped. If you ever suspect someone is being forced or is underage, report it to the Tourist Police on 1155 or the government's social assistance hotline on 1300.

## Respect

Whatever brought someone into this work, treat them with respect, the same as anyone else.
