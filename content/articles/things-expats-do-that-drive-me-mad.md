I love Pattaya, and most expats here are great. But after 20 years there are a few habits I see over and over that really wind me up. (To be fair, there's plenty I admire too: read [what I admire about some expats](/articles/what-i-admire-about-some-expats).)

## 1. Speakerphones

This is the big one. People taking calls or watching YouTube on loudspeaker in restaurants. I absolutely can't stand it.

It has actually worked in my favour. It's part of the reason Jeff, the original owner of Cajun Life Cafe, sold the business to me. It wound him up so much that he'd get angry, and in the end he couldn't face dealing with the public any more. I understand completely. When I'm in someone else's restaurant and the next table is on speakerphone, it drives me mad. More in [my worst customers](/articles/my-worst-customers).

## 2. Five-minute experts giving bad advice

Watch two expats meet and one of the first questions is always, "How long have you been here?" If the other person says they're on holiday, that's the cue. Someone who's been here a few years, which is five minutes in the grand scheme of things, starts talking as if they know everything about Thailand and Pattaya.

The bad advice is usually about property and business. The classic is the old saying: "If you want to make a million baht in Thailand, bring ten million." In other words, you can't make money here. That's simply untrue. I've made money here, and so have plenty of people I know. If you want advice on doing business or buying property, ask someone who's actually done it. More in [starting or buying a business in Pattaya](/articles/starting-or-buying-a-business-in-pattaya) and [buying a condo in Pattaya](/articles/buying-a-condo-in-pattaya).

## 3. Bar Thai

Fair play to anyone who tries to speak Thai. But it annoys me when someone's Thai is entirely phrases picked up in the bars. They can't really speak it, but they'll throw bar talk around as if they're fluent. If you want to speak Thai, learn it properly. More in [learning Thai](/articles/learning-thai).

## 4. No shirts in restaurants

If you're at a beach bar or on Koh Chang, I get it. But in Pattaya, even close to the beach, it doesn't take much to work out which restaurants are fine with no shirt and which aren't. Most aren't. It's mainly men.

Last week in Terminal 21 I saw a young foreign woman walking round the shopping centre in a bikini. That's a major no-no here, especially with families and children around and people eating. Nobody said anything, because Thais are very tolerant and avoid confrontation, so she probably had no idea. But plenty of people would have been thinking it was inappropriate. More in [cultural mistakes in Thailand](/articles/cultural-mistakes-in-thailand).
