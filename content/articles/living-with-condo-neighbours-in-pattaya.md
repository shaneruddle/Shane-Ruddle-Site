I love my condo. The location is great, and so is almost everything else about it. But living in a condo in Thailand means living very close to other people. The walls are thin, corridors echo, and sooner or later you'll have to deal with your neighbours. Here's what I've run into, and what you can do about it. ## The slamming doors

In my building, the corridors echo, so you hear everything: people walking, people talking. The doors use key cards. From the inside, you can close the door quietly with the handle. From the outside, you need to use the key card so the lock releases and the door closes softly.

A lot of people don't know that, so they just slam it. Late at night, that bang echoes all the way down the corridor. It certainly wakes me up, and I imagine it wakes everyone else too.

The fix was simple. When new tenants move in, I've shown them how the door works and asked them not to slam it.

## The cannabis smoke

When cannabis was first decriminalised, nobody was quite sure what the rules were, and some people started smoking in their rooms. Someone on my floor did, and the smell drifted through the corridor. Every time I stepped out, I could smell it.

Security investigated, but couldn't work out which room it was coming from, and everyone they asked said it wasn't them. It went on for a long time and only stopped when a few tenants moved out.

Then the building put up signs telling people not to smoke in their rooms. So people started smoking on their balconies instead, and depending on the wind, it still came into my room. The juristic person, the building's management, has it under control now. More in [cannabis in Pattaya](/articles/cannabis-in-pattaya).

## A neighbour's violent arguments

The hardest one was a foreign man on my floor who was violent towards his girlfriend. Their arguments spilled into the corridor, and everyone could hear. One time she was screaming so loudly that I came out to try to calm things down.

It kept happening for a couple of months. At one point it was clear she'd been hurt. I wasn't sure whether to go to the police, and I was advised not to get involved, so I reluctantly stayed out of it. The last time, he was throwing her out, it kicked off in the corridor again, and another tenant got involved. It was a mess. Eventually they moved out.

Looking back, it's a reminder of how close you live to other people in a condo. You can end up involved in things you'd never choose to be part of.

If you're experiencing violence at home in Thailand, or are worried about someone who is, you can call the government's 1300 social assistance hotline, or 1155 for the Tourist Police, who speak English. In an emergency, call 191.

## Condo or house?

Don't base your decision on stories like these alone. Each of these problems was solved in the end. And houses have their own problems. When I lived in a house, I had snakes in the garden and mosquitoes to deal with. There are pros and cons either way. ## Your path to peace

If you rent a condo in Thailand, expect thin walls and some noise, and expect to deal with the odd issue. But remember you're not on your own. You have people to turn to:

- **The agency** you rented through
- **The juristic person**, the building's management
- **Security**, which almost every condo has
- **The Tourist Police (1155) or police (191)** if things escalate

There's always a path to peace. Use it.
