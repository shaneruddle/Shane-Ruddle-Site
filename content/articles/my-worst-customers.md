In 20 years running car and bike rental, restaurants and property agencies in Pattaya, I've had some memorable customers. Most people are great. These are the ones that weren't, and what they taught me.

## The car we collected from Phuket

This one sticks in my mind more than any other. A customer asked whether we could collect a car from Phuket. We told them clearly that we're a Pattaya company and we couldn't. They rented from us anyway.

At the end of the rental they asked to extend by two or three days without paying, which is normal. We usually take it from the deposit when the car comes back. But the car never came back. They left it at their hotel in Phuket, about as far from our office as you can get. We had to fly down and drive it 12 hours back.

We never got anything back for the extra days, the flight or the time. The most shocking part is that they weren't young tearaways with no responsibilities. They were a normal family with two kids.

## The customer who kicked off at Hemingways

At Hemingways Jomtien, a customer propositioned one of our staff. She turned him down, and he didn't like it. He kicked off in the restaurant, then left us [bad reviews](/articles/customer-service-and-online-reviews). We threw him out and he's not welcome back. My staff come first.

## Speakerphones at Cajun

At Cajun Life Cafe we've never really had aggressive or disruptive customers. Our biggest problem is people taking calls on speakerphone, which is incredibly annoying for everyone else. When we ask them to stop, they usually don't like being told.

## The bikes that crashed in the yard

In the early days of Pattaya Rent a Bike, we had plenty of inexperienced riders. They'd assure us they could ride, show us a licence, take the keys, open the throttle and crash straight into something before they'd even left the yard. It happened about half a dozen times.

Those customers changed how we work. We're much more careful now, especially with younger renters. We look harder at licences, check the renter's age and how long they've held a licence, and use our experience to judge whether someone is likely to be a responsible renter. More in [renting a car in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-car-in-pattaya).

## The tenant who wanted to sue

We've had some very difficult clients at both East Coast and Alan Bolton. The one that stands out is recent. A tenant broke his lease early after a dispute with the landlady over plumbing in the condo. Whose problem it was wasn't clear-cut. The landlady said it was a building issue, not her condo. You could also argue that choosing a unit in an older building comes with that risk.

I stepped in to mediate. He seemed reasonable at first, and we negotiated something. Then he changed his mind, became aggressive, and ended up harassing our company and our staff because he didn't get his deposit back.

Part of the problem was that he expected things to work the way they do back home, where you get a lawyer involved and sue. It doesn't work like that here, and he got more and more frustrated when he realised.

## My advice: be reasonable

If you end up in a dispute over a deposit, wear and tear or anything else, keep a cool head and work with your agency. We've done this a lot longer than you, and we know how these things play out here. I was trying to find an outcome that was reasonable for everyone. In any conflict in Thailand, being reasonable gets you much further than threatening lawyers. More in [renting a condo in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-condo-in-pattaya).
