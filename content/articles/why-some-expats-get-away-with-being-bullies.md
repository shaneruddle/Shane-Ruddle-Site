## Who they pick on

It's usually a young member of staff, often small in stature, someone who isn't going to argue back. They'll just take the abuse and the complaining, so the person dishing it out gets away with it.

I've seen a lot of these situations, and I'm convinced most of them wouldn't happen if the person on the other side of the counter was a big man.

## What sets it off

Usually it starts with a misunderstanding. Sometimes it's money. If someone is living on a tight budget and something goes wrong that they have to pay for, they kick off because it's eating into that budget.

But a lot of the time, people walk in already expecting to be ripped off. They assume that because they're a foreigner in Thailand, they're being scammed, so the first small misunderstanding confirms what they already believed. ## How this fits with "poke the bear"

In another article I wrote that if you poke the bear here, you should expect an overreaction. That's still true. But it doesn't happen often, and it doesn't happen to everyone.

In my experience, many Thais are very tolerant and will put up with an awful lot before they reach breaking point. This article is about that long stretch before the explosion, the times when someone is clearly poking the bear and nothing happens. ## When it happens to my staff

If I see it happening to my staff, I step in. Usually, as soon as another foreigner gets involved, it calms down. Maybe it's because I'm a foreigner too, maybe it's because I'm the owner. Either way, they rarely carry on in the same way. More in [the customer isn't always right](/articles/the-customer-isnt-always-right).

## Bullies with no consequences

Some of these expats are simply bullies. Because there are no consequences, they keep doing it. They go from restaurant to restaurant, business to business, getting away with it every time, and that becomes who they are.

## The resentment builds up

Most staff laugh it off at the time. But I'm sure some resentment towards foreigners builds up underneath.

The place you see it most, I think, is immigration. Officers at the Jomtien office can come across as short and unfriendly. I strongly believe a lot of that comes from years of foreigners treating them badly. I've seen foreigners screaming at immigration officers, and the officers just taking it. They don't scream back, but you can sense the feeling afterwards: how dare you act like that to me in my own country. Over time, people harden, and everyone who walks through the door gets treated the same way. So one bully makes life harder for every foreigner who comes after them. ## When I ban someone

I've banned people for this many times. I'll usually give someone the benefit of the doubt once. But if my staff tell me they're not comfortable serving someone, that's my cue. I tell the person, politely, that they're no longer welcome.

I know my staff. If they feel threatened, or even just uncomfortable, that customer has crossed the line, and we won't serve them again.

## My advice

Just don't do it. The fact that you can get away with something doesn't give you the right to do it. Treat the young woman behind the counter the way you'd treat anyone who could answer back.
