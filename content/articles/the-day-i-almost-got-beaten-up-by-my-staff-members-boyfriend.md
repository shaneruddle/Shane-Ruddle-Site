I once nearly had my head knocked off by a staff member's boyfriend over a pay rise. It was all a misunderstanding, and it taught me a lesson I've never forgotten: when it comes to staff pay, how you explain a change matters as much as the change itself.

## The problem: staff walking out without notice

This was back in my Fairways days, when I was still fairly inexperienced. We had a high staff turnover, and worse, people were leaving without any warning at all.

I thought about holding back part of everyone's salary until they left, but that's not allowed in Thailand, and it wasn't something I wanted to do anyway.

## My solution: a notice bonus

So instead, I introduced a deferred bonus. On top of everyone's normal salary, which stayed exactly the same, staff built up an extra bonus worth 5% of their pay, paid out when they left, as long as they gave fair notice. Nobody's pay was cut or held back. It was extra money on top, a reward for leaving properly.

*I'm not a lawyer. If you're thinking of doing something similar, check the rules on pay and bonuses with a Thai lawyer first.*

## The one who didn't get it

My manager explained it to all the staff as a group, and everyone seemed to understand, apart from one woman. She just couldn't get her head around it. Even though it was extra money, all she could hear was that money was being held back.

So we sat down with her privately and went through it again. I'll admit I got a bit frustrated, because it seemed simple to me. But she still didn't get it, so we left it there.

## The boyfriend

That evening, she went home and explained it to her boyfriend, completely wrongly. As far as he was concerned, I was taking money out of their salary.

The next day he came in, absolutely furious, and wanted to knock my head off. He went so far over the top that I was more shocked than anything. I didn't bite back. I let him vent, and when he'd finished, I asked if I could say something. Then I just kept repeating that it was a misunderstanding, and that I absolutely wasn't trying to rip anyone off. Eventually, he calmed down.

Because he'd threatened me, I had to let her go.

## The twist

Years later, by pure coincidence, she was working at a company I ended up buying. As soon as I bought it, she quit.

## The lesson

Explain any change to pay clearly, one to one if you need to, and check that every single person has understood it, not just most of them.

And remember that money is a sensitive subject. Staff may not seem especially driven by money day to day, but the moment they think you're taking some away, it becomes the most important thing in the world.
