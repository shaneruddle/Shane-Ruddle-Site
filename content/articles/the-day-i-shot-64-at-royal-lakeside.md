About 10 years ago, I shot 64 at Royal Lakeside, eight under par, with nine birdies and one bogey. It's the best round I've ever played, and it came completely out of nowhere. ## The setting

Royal Lakeside is about an hour from Pattaya, towards Bangkok. It suits my game. I'm a former pro and was playing off scratch at the time, but I've never been a long hitter. My strength was always around the greens.

I was playing with three good golfers: one off one, one off four and one off eight. My manager, who was also a PGA pro and the coach at the golf driving range I owned back then, was playing that day too. There were plenty of birdies in our group.

I was practising a lot at the range back then, but not playing much. I didn't walk onto the first tee thinking I was going to play well.

## Momentum

I birdied the first, and I think the second as well, and it just went from there. My putter was on fire. As I got to three under, four under, five under, I started to realise something special was happening.

Every time I got into trouble and thought, here we go, this is where it ends, I managed to recover. I remember one bunker shot in particular. I thought, okay, this is it. I got it out, chipped onto the green and holed the putt to save par and keep the round alive.

## The unlucky bogey

My only bogey came from a good shot. I hit what I think was a nine iron into the green, and in the air it looked like another birdie chance. But I must have caught a flier, with some grass between the club and the ball. It went an extra 10 to 15 yards, sailed over the flag and over the green, and I didn't get up and down. Looking back, the round could even have been better.

## The caddy

The moment I'll never forget came on the 17th. I hit another iron in close, and as the ball landed, my caddy turned to me. In English, she told me she'd been caddying for 18 years and had never seen anyone play golf like that.

The way she looked at me, she was in complete awe. I think she kept expecting me to falter at some point. I just didn't. I made the birdie, and kept going.

## Afterwards

When I came in, everyone wanted to hear about the round, which is unusual. Most golfers are only interested in their own game. My manager had played well too, a few over par, but I'd beaten him by quite a lot and won the day.

For weeks afterwards, people at the range were talking about it, and a few told me I should try being a pro again. I pushed back. It was one of those days when everything clicked, and you can't count on that.

## What it taught me

The best things often happen when you're not forcing them. I didn't expect anything that day. I just played one shot at a time, and every time it looked like going wrong, I kept going. That's not a bad way to approach business either. More in [do I ever switch off?](/articles/do-i-ever-switch-off)
