I own two real estate agencies in Pattaya, Alan Bolton Property Consultants and East Coast Real Estate, and I'm often asked what it's like to work as an agent here. Here's the honest picture.

## What makes a good property agent?

Passion: for property and for helping people. Real estate is tough everywhere. There's a lot of rejection and a lot of admin. AI makes the admin a bit easier now, but there's far more to the job than people outside realise. Without a passion for it, you won't last.

## What does a typical day look like?

Admin first thing: answering emails and messages, and dealing with any tenant issues. Then marketing, getting properties out there so people know they're still for sale or rent. Then, through the day, following up leads, booking viewings and searching for properties that clients are looking for.

## How are agents paid?

A basic salary of around ฿20,000 to ฿25,000 a month. It's not a lot. On top of that, agents earn 25% commission on the deals they bring in, which seems to be standard across Thailand and certainly in Pattaya.

Realistically, a rental agent earns between ฿60,000 and ฿100,000 a month. A good sales agent can earn upwards of ฿150,000 a month.

## Can foreigners work in property here?

We have both Thai and foreign staff. Any foreigner working in Thailand needs a work permit for a role they're legally allowed to do, and some jobs, including broker and agent work, are reserved for Thais. So if you're a foreigner thinking about a career in property here, make sure the role and the paperwork are set up properly from the start.

## Why do new agents quit?

Some underestimate how hard it is. But most who quit early do so because they can't handle the rejection. They expect that if they show someone a few properties, that person will buy or rent through them. It doesn't work that way. It's a numbers game.

## How long until you earn well?

Give it three or four months to learn the areas, the buildings, what's available, where to look and the systems we use, and to get a feel for how the industry works. Over the following year you'll start earning reasonably.

You start earning well once you can qualify a customer properly: telling whether they're wasting your time or a hot lead. That only comes with experience. By then you may also have found ways to generate better-quality leads yourself.

## What do the successful agents do differently?

They market themselves. We found early on that leads agents generate themselves are much easier to convert than company leads. So the good agents don't just rely on what the company gives them. They build their own personal brand: they're on social media, a few write blogs, and they put their face and their expertise out there.

## My advice if you're thinking of becoming an agent

Be patient, and understand as much as you can about the industry before you decide, because it's very different from what you probably imagine. Talk to other agents, Thai and foreign, and get a real sense of what the business involves.
