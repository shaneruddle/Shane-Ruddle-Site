There's a lazy assumption that if a Western man is with a Thai woman, especially a younger one, they must have met in a bar. Most of the time it's wrong. And even when it's right, so what? More in [no, not every Thai woman in Pattaya is a bar girl](/articles/not-every-thai-woman-in-pattaya-is-a-bar-girl).

## Where the assumption comes from

The picture people have in mind is an older Western man, from Europe, America or Australia, with a younger Thai woman. Years ago, for a lot of those couples, the bar really was where they met. It was often the only place many Westerners and Thais had the chance to meet each other. More in [was Pattaya better in the good old days?](/articles/was-pattaya-better-in-the-good-old-days)

## How couples actually meet now

Today it's normal. People meet on dating apps, in restaurants and hotels, at padel clubs, through friends of friends, the same as anywhere else. A lot of Westerners now know they can meet Thai partners in ordinary settings, and Thai women have far more ways to meet foreigners than working in a bar. More in [relationships in Pattaya](/articles/relationships-in-pattaya) and [making friends in Pattaya](/articles/making-friends-in-pattaya).

## Where the stigma shows up

In Thailand, I've never really seen or heard comments about it. Back in the West, it's different. It's rarely said out loud, but you can get looks and insinuations, a feeling that people have made their minds up.

That's fading. Mixed couples are common everywhere now, right up to the most successful people in the world. Tiger Woods' parents are a well-known example. His father, Earl, was an American soldier, and people have always speculated about how they met. In fact, his mother, Kultida, was working as a secretary in a US Army office in Bangkok.

## So what?

The assumption says a lot more about the person making it than about the couple. Keep an open mind, and take people as you find them.
