With two young boys, we've unfortunately spent a lot of time in hospitals over the years, in Thailand, the UK, Cyprus and France. One day in the UK has stayed with me more than any other.

## A cut that wouldn't heal

On a visit back to the UK, my eldest son got rope burn on his finger from a climbing frame. It wasn't clearing up, so we took him to the nearest hospital to get it checked. We didn't think much of it.

We got there at about two in the afternoon, and because he was a child, we were seen quite quickly. I remember thinking, "This isn't too bad."

Then we were told he needed minor surgery, and transferred to a specialist hospital on the other side of the city. We didn't have a car, so we went by public transport.

## Waiting, and not knowing

At the second hospital, we waited close to eight hours before anyone saw him. My partner at the time stayed with him, and I wasn't allowed to wait in that area, so I sat elsewhere with our youngest. I had no idea what was happening or how long it would take. My youngest was hungry, tired and restless.

We finally left at 11pm, nine hours after we'd first arrived.

By the end, I'm not proud to say, my frustration got the better of me. The staff overheard and stepped in. They didn't apologise. They told me they were understaffed, and that this was what you should expect.

I had a quiet word with the doctor and asked whether this really was what everyone goes through, a young boy and his little brother waiting the best part of nine hours. He said yes, that's how it is.

I left with a bitter taste in my mouth, not just because of the wait, but because of the acceptance that it was normal.

## The same kind of emergency in Pattaya

Compare that with when my youngest slipped in the bathroom and cut the top of his eye. He needed stitches. At the hospital in Pattaya, we were in and out, treatment included, in less than two hours. We had co-pay insurance at the time, so it cost us a few thousand baht.

Of everywhere we've had to take the kids, the experience here has been second to none.

## Free vs fast

Yes, in the UK it was free, and here you pay. But when it's your children, you don't think about the money. You just want it sorted quickly, so a stressful situation doesn't become even more stressful.

I hope the staff that day were wrong and that it isn't the normal experience in the UK. If it is, that's a real worry.

## A friend's story

A very good friend of mine had an even harder experience during COVID. His wife was very sick, with something unrelated to COVID, and needed emergency care. Because of the precautions at the time, she ended up being treated in the hospital car park. It shows how stretched things were, even for people in urgent need.

Those were unprecedented times, and I understand that. But the NHS was already under huge pressure before COVID, and since then the system seems to be under even more strain.

That makes the Thai setup look even better to me. The hospitals here are privately owned businesses, and they're in it to make money, which has its downsides. But when it comes to emergency care, I think most people would rather pay and be seen than get it free and wait weeks or months.

## The other side

This was one day at one hospital. The NHS treats millions of people for free, and many staff work under huge pressure. Private hospitals here are fast partly because you pay, and without insurance a serious illness can cost a fortune. That's why I always tell people moving here to get proper health insurance. *This is my own experience, not medical advice.*

## My take

The price tag in Thailand puts some people off. After that day in the UK, I'd pay every time.
