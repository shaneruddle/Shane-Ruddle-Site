I'll be honest: I'm not really a money-saving kind of guy. But plenty of people retire to Pattaya on a fixed budget, and after more than 20 years here, I've seen what works. These are the tips I'd give anyone who wants their money to go further.

## 1. Eat where the budget-savvy eat

Places like Cheap Charlie's and Kiss Food are canteen-style, open-air restaurants serving very cheap food. They run on slim margins and high volume. It's a very different business model from mine, but if you're on a budget, they're a great option.

## 2. Eat Thai food

Thai food is cheap. In most Thai restaurants, you can get rice with chicken and vegetables for 40 or 50 baht. A foreign meal like fish and chips will usually cost north of 300 baht. Eating Thai most of the time will save you a lot.

## 3. Buy Thai brands

It's tempting to grab Kellogg's Cornflakes, but most Thai alternatives aren't bad at all. Shop at the big supermarkets like Lotus's and Makro, and look at the Thai brands first.

## 4. Play golf on sports days

Weekday golf is cheaper than weekend golf, which is fairly obvious. The real savings come on sports days. Courses around Pattaya, and I believe in other parts of Thailand too, each have a sports day, always midweek, often a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday, with a very reasonable green fee or package. Seek them out. More in [golf in Pattaya](/articles/golf-in-pattaya).

## 5. Rent a motorbike

For getting around, a rented motorbike is the cheapest and most convenient option. You can find bikes for around 2,000 baht a month, which is about 67 baht a day, and fuel is relatively cheap.

I should declare an interest here: I own Pattaya Rent a Bike. To be fair, we don't usually rent bikes that cheaply, unless we have older models and it's low season.

The downsides are comfort and safety. A bike isn't as comfortable as a car, and it can be more dangerous. A bicycle is cheaper still, but has obvious drawbacks. For value for money, though, you can't beat a motorbike.

## 6. Rent, don't buy

This might go against what a lot of readers think, because many see rent as wasted money. But renting here is cheap by comparison.

If you rent smart, you're often paying around 2.5 to 3% of the property's value a year. The owner, meanwhile, pays the maintenance fees and the repairs. A landlord might aim to make 6 or 7% on their money, but smart renters can sometimes get places far cheaper than that. Add the flexibility of being able to move, and renting often beats buying. More in [renting a condo in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-condo-in-pattaya).

## 7. Be disciplined with nightlife

This is where I think a lot of people go wrong with their budgets. Once you start down the road of nightlife and entertainment, it's very easy to get carried away. Set yourself limits and stick to them.

## The other side: don't skimp on health insurance

There are some things in Thailand you shouldn't skimp on, and health insurance is one of them. Healthcare here can be expensive, and many people choose to go without cover. Saving money is great, but not if one hospital bill wipes out years of savings.

*This is my own experience, not financial advice.*

## My take

You can live very well in Pattaya on a budget. Eat Thai, rent smart, play golf midweek, and keep a lid on the nights out.
