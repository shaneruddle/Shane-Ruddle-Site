I've made plenty of mistakes in 20 years of business in Pattaya. Some I've made more than once. These are the ones I'd never repeat.

## 1. Going in with my heart over my head

I've made this mistake twice.

The first was Fairways Golf Range. I was a keen golfer, and when it came up, it felt as though everything I'd ever learned and done had led to that moment. Nobody could have talked me out of it. Even if the numbers hadn't looked good, I'd probably still have done it. I've written more about it in [my biggest business failure](/articles/my-biggest-business-failure).

The second was Hemingways Lakeside. I took it on during a period of personal problems that had real consequences, and I wasn't in the right frame of mind. I decided too quickly. Looking back, I think I wanted something to focus on to take my mind off everything else.

## 2. Ignoring my gut

When I took over Hemingways Lakeside, my plan was to spend a few million baht, put the kitchen at the front and do it very differently from how it ended up. Instead, I asked a friend in construction whose opinion I respect, and I gave his advice too much weight. That's what really cost me.

It was easy for him to say, because he wasn't involved and there were no consequences for him. The lesson: listen to advice, but don't take it too literally, and trust your own instincts. It's your money on the line.

## 3. A partnership without a clear agreement

I've never gone into a partnership with no agreement at all, but I have done it without a clear one. An agreement should be looked over by a third party, ideally a lawyer. It's worth the extra time and money to get it right, because if you don't, problems come up later. More in [business partners in Pattaya](/articles/business-partners-in-pattaya).

## 4. Going into business with someone I didn't know

I invested in BizPay, a barter system a bit like Bartercard: businesses charge for goods and services in barter credit, then spend it on other members' goods and services. We were franchisees of a bigger nationwide network. I was a silent investor, and the day-to-day running was done by a partner I didn't know very well.

The idea definitely caught on. The partnership was what didn't work, along with some politics with the wider network. Don't go into business with someone you don't really know.

## 5. Thinking people will change

I've hired people believing they were where they were through bad luck, or because previous employers never let them show what they could do. I hoped working for me would be a fresh start. It almost never works out. People do change, but usually only for a while before old habits come back. Nine times out of ten, people are who they are. More in [hiring and firing in Pattaya](/articles/hiring-and-firing-in-pattaya).

## One I break: stick to what you know

I'd tell most people without business experience to stick to what they know. Funnily enough, I've gone into businesses I knew nothing about several times and done well. I feel I've got enough experience now to learn the ropes of almost anything quickly. But if you're starting out, stick to what you know.
