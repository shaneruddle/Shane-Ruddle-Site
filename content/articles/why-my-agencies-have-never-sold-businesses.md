My property agencies, East Coast Real Estate and Alan Bolton Property Consultants, sell and rent condos and houses. We've never listed businesses for sale, and people sometimes ask why. A recent chat with a business broker I've known for 20 years confirmed I made the right call.

## It's too easy to cut the agent out

With a condo or a house, buyers and sellers can go direct, but it takes some effort. With a business, it's easy.

You can advertise a business without naming it at first. But as soon as someone is interested, you have to tell them what it is and where it is. From there, they can simply walk in, find the owner and do the deal without you.

The broker told me it happens to him often.

## Deals take a long time

Businesses can sit on the market for months. A buyer looks once, walks away, then comes back six months or a year later when the price has dropped, and goes straight to the owner.

There are fewer businesses for sale than condos, so it's a little easier to keep track. But with timelines that long, it's still very hard to prove who introduced who.

## It's more complicated

Selling a business is also far more involved than selling a condo. There's much more due diligence: the accounts, the lease, staff, licences, stock. There are simply more ways for a deal to go wrong.

## The twist: I cut him out too

As it turns out, I've done exactly this to him, without knowing it.

Years ago, I bought a business from someone I knew. I went to the owner directly. What I didn't know was that the business was listed exclusively with this broker.

When he went to the seller for his commission, the seller used me as the excuse for not paying. I had no idea until he told me recently.

## My take

There's good money in business broking, but it takes a particular kind of operation to make it work. For my agencies, sticking to residential property has been the right decision.

If you're a seller with an exclusive agreement and a buyer you know turns up, remember the broker may still be owed a commission.
