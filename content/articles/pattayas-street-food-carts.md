Pattaya's street food carts are part of the chaos, and part of the culture. Ten years ago I'd probably have said get rid of them. Now I'd say let them be. More in [Thai food for newcomers](/articles/thai-food-for-newcomers).

## Something real about it

There's something quite nice about a cheap meal at the side of the road, even in a dusty, busy city, cooked and served by someone working hard for a living. It feels real. It's part of what Thailand is.

## And part of the chaos

The other side of the coin is that there's no real plan for where they can set up. From what I understand, a lot of them have licences now, but they go wherever they want. Sometimes they cause traffic problems, and sometimes even accidents because of where they've stopped.

Often someone flags one down, and the vendor's focus is on serving that customer quickly, not on whether they've stopped somewhere safe or convenient for everyone else. It's a lot like the baht buses. More in [Pattaya's baht buses](/articles/pattayas-baht-buses).

## Not my competition

As a restaurant owner, I don't see them as competition at all. Most of my staff go out and buy food from them on their breaks. I'd guess around 90% of their customers are Thai, while around 90% of ours are foreigners.

I wouldn't even mind if one set up outside one of my restaurants, even selling something we sell. And at the back of your mind, you know it's someone trying to earn a living in a hard job that probably doesn't pay much. Moving someone on in their own country doesn't feel right.

## Would zones work?

I don't think set zones would work. Even if there were dedicated areas, I doubt many would use them. The whole idea is to move around and go where the people are. When there's a festival on, you'll see hundreds of them turn up, because it's an opportunity.

Licensing is a good start. It stops the streets being overrun by anyone with a sidecar deciding to have a go, and you'd hope it covers food safety and some basic experience. Maybe a simple uniform or badge would help, so customers can see a vendor is licensed and regulated.

## Let it be

Beyond that, I'd leave them alone. The chaos is part of it. Some things in Pattaya need fixing, but the food carts aren't top of my list. More in [where a restaurant owner eats in Pattaya](/articles/where-a-restaurant-owner-eats-in-pattaya).
