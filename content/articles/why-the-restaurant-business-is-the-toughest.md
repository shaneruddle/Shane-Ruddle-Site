Of all the businesses I've run, restaurants are the toughest. The reason is simple: in a restaurant, your success and your decline happen in public. More in [running a restaurant and bar in Pattaya](/articles/running-a-restaurant-and-bar-in-pattaya).

## Everyone can see how you're doing

In car rental, if we have a quiet month, our customers don't know. Nobody renting a car can see how many other cars went out that week. Property is the same.

In a restaurant, it's obvious. Anyone walking past can see whether the tables are full or empty.

## People attract people

Unlike car rental or property, in a restaurant people attract people. A busy room creates atmosphere, and customers assume, even subconsciously, that the food will be better, quicker and fresher.

Believe it or not, the service usually is better when it's busy. When it's quiet, staff end up sitting around on their phones and not paying attention.

The opposite is also true. When a restaurant is empty, anyone who walks in is taking a risk: that the food won't be good, that the service won't be good. Most people would rather not take that risk, so they walk on. It becomes a spiral.

## Easy to keep busy, hard to get busy

The flip side is that once a restaurant is busy, it's fairly easy to keep it that way. The hard part is when you're quiet.

Take Hemingways Lakeside. To get it busy again, I'd need to do one of two things: a major marketing drive, or slowly grow it organically, which takes a long time.

One thing that helps is posting on social media every time you are busy. People see a full room online, so when they decide where to go, the risk feels lower, even if it's a bit quieter when they arrive. More in [marketing a small business in Pattaya](/articles/marketing-a-small-business-in-pattaya).

## The point of no return

There comes a point where you have so few customers that it's very hard to drag yourself back. At that stage, a lot of owners look to sell, because it seems to be the only way out. Someone else comes in with fresh ideas, maybe a fresh brand or concept, and certainly fresh energy.

## Usually I'm the fresh energy

The irony is that I'm usually the person who comes in and turns a place around. I did it at the Outback, at Retox, at the Golf Club and at Paddy's Palms. A tired restaurant with a new owner and a new idea can come back to life.

So if you're stuck in that spiral, selling isn't necessarily failure. Sometimes the best thing for a restaurant is a new owner who sees it with fresh eyes.
