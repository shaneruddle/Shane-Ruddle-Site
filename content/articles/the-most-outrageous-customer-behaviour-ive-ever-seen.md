In over 20 years of dealing with the public in Pattaya, I can only really come up with about a dozen truly outrageous customers. They're rare. But when they happen, they stay with you, and they've become some of my favourite stories to tell.

## My days at the Sportsman

When I worked at the Sportsman, three customers stand out.

**The smashed watch.** One customer got so angry with a member of staff that he grabbed her watch and smashed it on the bar.

**The salad.** Another wasn't happy with his salad and went absolutely ballistic, to the point where another customer stood up and physically threw him out of the restaurant.

**The hangover.** The worst was a man who came in clearly hungover one morning and threw up all over the restaurant floor. That was bad enough. But while the staff were cleaning it up, he showed no sympathy for them and didn't apologise once. So I had a word with him, and he got really shirty with me for pointing it out.

All three were banned, and all three left.

## The kiss on the cheek

This one happened when I was a customer myself, years later, at the same restaurant. I was sat outside with friends, next to a table of very drunk men. One of them got up to go to the toilet, and as he walked past the back of my chair, he leaned round and kissed me on the cheek.

I followed him to the toilet to have a word. I'll leave that story there.

## Stories from Hemingways

At Hemingways I'm the owner, not the manager, so I don't see as much myself. But I've heard plenty.

- A customer went absolutely ballistic at his girlfriend in the middle of the restaurant.
- A customer touched one of our staff inappropriately.
- Someone who wasn't even a customer came in to use our toilet and left it in such a state that we checked the CCTV to see who'd done it.

We've also had customers pass out in their chairs. A couple of times, slumped over at their table, they looked so still that we feared the worst. Thankfully, nobody has died in the restaurant. Sadly, though, we have had customers pass away not long after being taken ill with us. Those are the moments that put everything else in perspective.

## What it's taught me

Expect the unexpected. Patience is what you need for the everyday problems, like food that isn't hot enough. Incidents like these are a different thing. They're so rare you can't really prepare for them.

So treat them as part of the job. Deal with them, ban who needs banning, look after your staff, and move on. And at the end of it, you'll have a story to tell. I've told these ones more times than I can count.
