I've lived in Thailand for over 20 years. I run businesses, employ Thai staff, and pay every tax going: personal income tax, corporate tax, VAT, social security, withholding tax, signboard tax, property tax and more. I've written about [the taxes we pay](/articles/tax-in-thailand-why-you-need-a-thai-accountant).

And yet, in the eyes of the system, I'm treated no differently from someone on a two-week holiday.

## The paperwork never stops

- **90-day reporting.** Every 90 days I have to tell immigration where I'm living, as if I'm some kind of criminal. More on that in [TM30 and the 90-day report](/articles/tm30-and-90-day-report-pattaya).
- **An annual work permit.** I have to renew it every year.
- **Re-entry permits.** If I leave the country, I need one, or my visa ends when I go.
- **Reporting where I stay.** If I move or travel to another province, the authorities are supposed to be told where I'm staying.

None of this changes, however long you've been here or however much you've contributed.

## Paying more, getting less

As a company director, I don't get the health cover that my staff get through social security. I pay the foreigner price at national parks, often several times what a Thai visitor pays. I've written about that in [dual pricing in Thailand](/articles/dual-pricing-in-thailand).

And my children were born here, but they're foreigners. Being born in Thailand doesn't give them any path to the benefits that come with it.

## Not asking for special treatment

I'm not asking to be treated as a Thai citizen. But after more than 20 years of living here, running businesses and paying tax, it would be nice to be treated differently from a tourist.

A lot of these rules were written a long time ago and simply haven't been revisited. Something as simple as dropping the 90-day reports for long-term residents with a clean record, or a longer work permit for people who've been here for decades, would make a real difference. That's a suggestion, not a complaint.

## The other side

There are routes for long-term foreigners, including permanent residency and the Long-Term Resident visa, and some would say the answer is to apply for one of those. Others point out that every country has rules for foreign residents, and that Thailand has every right to keep track of who's living here.

## My take

Thailand says it wants to attract long-term investors and residents. The best way to do that is to treat the ones who are already here, and already paying their way, a little better than a two-week tourist.
