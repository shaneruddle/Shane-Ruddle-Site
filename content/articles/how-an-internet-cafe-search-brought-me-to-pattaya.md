## The first trip: the chaperone

Between my first and second years at university, a couple of girls in my group of friends decided they wanted to go to Thailand. Their parents wouldn't let them go on their own, so they asked around to see if anyone would join them. I was one of the only ones with the money, so I said yes.

I spent about two months backpacking around Thailand. Back then you didn't plan trips on the internet. You used the Lonely Planet guide, and Pattaya either wasn't in it or wasn't well covered. It wasn't on my radar at all.

When I left, I promised myself that as soon as I finished university, I'd come back. Not to work or live, just for another holiday.

## The internet café

When I finished my degree, I flew back to Bangkok and headed straight to Khao San Road, because I knew it from the first trip. This time I'd brought my golf clubs. I knew Thailand had golf courses, but I had no idea how many or how good they were.

So I went into an internet café and searched for golf in Thailand. Pattaya came up. I'd never heard of it. I kept reading and found out it had 20-odd golf courses and wasn't far from Bangkok. That settled it. More in [golf in Pattaya](/articles/golf-in-pattaya).

## The minibus

I took a minibus from Khao San Road down to Pattaya with some other young travellers. It's about two hours, so we got chatting. An Irish guy asked me what I knew about Pattaya. I said I'd never been and didn't know anything about it. He put me straight on a few things. Back then, a lot of people went mainly for the nightlife. ## Up Soi 6 with my golf clubs

The minibus dropped me on Beach Road, right at the bottom of Soi 6. I had a big rucksack, my golf clubs and no accommodation booked. In those days you didn't book everything online. You just turned up and worked it out.

Just as I got there, a guy walked out of the soi carrying a tennis racket. He spotted my golf clubs and asked what I was looking for. I said somewhere to stay. He told me to walk up the soi about 100 metres, look for a dive shop on the left, and ask for Dave, who had rooms upstairs.

So there I was, fresh off the plane, pale as anything, walking up Soi 6 with a rucksack and a set of golf clubs, with what felt like every bar on the street calling out to me. I never made it to the dive shop. I walked straight to the end of the soi, jumped on the first baht bus on Second Road and got out of there. More in [nightlife in Pattaya](/articles/nightlife-in-pattaya).

## Heaven next to a driving range

The baht bus took me towards Naklua, and I checked into a short-stay room for the night. The next day I rented a motorbike and went looking for a golf driving range.

I found one with rooms right next door. With the exchange rate back then, it was £80 a month. It was a very basic fan room, but it was next to the driving range. I spent all day hitting balls, played rounds with people I met there, then walked across to my room for a shower. It was absolute heaven.

## Deciding not to go home

I was enjoying myself far too much to go home. I had family in the UK, but I hadn't started a career. I knew that if I went back, I'd end up with a career, a mortgage and eventually kids, and I'd never do it. I was lucky to have no ties, excuse the pun, so I stayed as long as I could.

Then I got a job, and it went from there. That first job was at 15 Palms on Koh Chang, but that's a story for another day.
