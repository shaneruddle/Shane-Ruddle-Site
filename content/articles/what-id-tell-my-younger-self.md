I came to Pattaya for a couple of months to [play golf](/articles/golf-in-pattaya). I didn't leave Thailand for the next six years. Here's what I'd tell my younger self if I could go back.

## 1. Enjoy yourself without the alcohol

In Pattaya every night feels like Saturday night, and it's very easy to get carried away. I was definitely guilty of that. I wasn't an alcoholic, but I partied far too much.

I haven't had a drink in over 10 years, and life got easier when I stopped. Looking back, a lot of the problems I had in the early days were down to alcohol: things that happened when I'd been drinking, or bad moods a day or two later from poor sleep and hangovers. That's advice I'd give my younger self wherever he lived, but Pattaya makes it especially easy to overdo it.

## 2. Look after your health

I've only really eaten well for the last five or six years. Before that it was sandwiches, pizza, takeaways, fast food and snacks. I'm reasonably healthy, but I wish I'd started sooner. Twenty years ago healthy food was harder to find here. Now there are so many options that there's no excuse. More in [eating healthily in Pattaya](/articles/eating-healthily-in-pattaya) and [staying fit in Pattaya](/articles/staying-fit-in-pattaya).

## 3. Take money seriously earlier

The earlier you start saving and investing, the easier life is for your future self, because compounding does the work. Invest young, leave it alone, and financial freedom comes much sooner. I'm not a financial adviser, but that's the one thing I'd hammer home.

## 4. There's no safety net

The one thing I'd tell myself to keep doing is putting money aside, and it matters even more in Thailand. If you make this your home, there's no safety net. Retirement is on you. The government won't help, your family may not be around, and your friends have their own battles. Back home in the UK there's a system behind you, with healthcare and education taken care of. Here there isn't. The sooner you realise that, the better off you'll be. More in [retiring in Pattaya](/articles/retiring-in-pattaya) and [healthcare in Pattaya](/articles/healthcare-in-pattaya).

## Would I still come? Absolutely

Coming here was one of the best decisions I've ever made, even though I never really decided it. I came for a golf holiday, and the longer I stayed, the more I enjoyed it.

I often tell people that if they love a month or two here as tourists, living here is even better. As a tourist everything is new, but you don't really know your way around or how things work. Living here has its downsides, but on the whole it just keeps getting better.
