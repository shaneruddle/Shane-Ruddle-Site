You often hear that most expats who retire to Pattaya leave within two years. That isn't what I've seen in 20 years here. When people decide to stay, they stay for as long as they can.

## Money moves people, but not home

Some people do find it isn't as cheap as they expected and run short of money. But that rarely sends them back home, because there are very few places cheaper than Thailand. Vietnam, perhaps.

What usually happens is that people move further out. If Bangkok is too expensive, they come to Pattaya or Hua Hin. If Pattaya is too expensive, they move to the countryside, often parts of Isan, where their budget goes further. They don't leave Thailand. They just adjust. More in [is Pattaya expensive?](/articles/is-pattaya-expensive) and [retiring in Pattaya](/articles/retiring-in-pattaya).

## Visas aren't the reason

There's a visa for almost every situation here, and the retirement visa is relatively easy to get and renew. More in [visas in Pattaya](/articles/thai-visas-in-pattaya).

## The real reason: health

The main reason retirees go home is health. Healthcare here can be expensive, even with insurance. If your policy doesn't cover a particular problem, or you hit its limit, going home for free treatment makes sense. More in [healthcare in Pattaya](/articles/healthcare-in-pattaya).

It's tied up with family, too. When you're unwell, you may get better care back home, but it's also good to be near your family.

## My advice

If you're planning to retire here, the money side is more flexible than people think, because you can always move somewhere cheaper. Health is what to plan for. Get the best health insurance you can afford, understand exactly what it covers and its limits, and have a plan for what you'd do if something serious happened.
