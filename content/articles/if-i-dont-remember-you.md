If you meet someone who served you on holiday and they don't remember you, don't take it personally. It happened to me recently, the other way round, and it was a little embarrassing.

## The customer I couldn't place

I had a meeting recently with a man about a business matter. I'd been chatting with his wife beforehand, and didn't put two and two together, even though I'd met her before too.

When he arrived, it was obvious he knew a lot more about me than I knew about him. He knew about my property agencies, because he'd recently listed a property with us. I couldn't place him at all.

When someone recognises me from my past, it's usually golf. So I asked if he was still playing. He said he'd never played golf. Oh dear.

So I asked how we knew each other. He'd been a [Pattaya Rent a Car](/articles/16-years-of-pattaya-rent-a-car) customer for years, more than 10 years ago. I said something like "oh yes" and moved the conversation on.

## Why it happens

Normally I'm pretty good with people, so it doesn't happen to me often. But it used to happen a lot when I managed the Sportsman. People would come back for their two-week holiday after a year away and remember everything we'd talked about. They remembered me not because I'm memorable, but because I was the manager. Meanwhile, I was meeting 10 to 15 new people every day.

My life has also changed a lot over the last 10 years. These days I'm rarely customer-facing, so faces from back then fade even faster.

## It isn't personal

When you go on holiday, you tend to remember the waiters, the bar staff and the people you meet. But people in hospitality, or anyone dealing with the public, meet so many people that they can't remember everyone.

That doesn't mean you weren't important to them. Your holiday stands out in your memory because it's a holiday. For us, it's just another working day.

## If you work in hospitality, names matter

That said, if you work with the public, remembering names is really important. Whether you manage a golf club with members, run a gym or work behind a bar, it makes people feel special, and it makes a real difference to whether they come back.

I was always very good with names and faces back in the day, and I still am, especially with regulars. Even when you're meeting lots of people every day, it's worth trying to connect the dots so you remember them. Use whatever memory tricks work for you: link someone's face to a person you know better, or tie their name to something it reminds you of.

So if someone doesn't remember you, give them a gentle reminder of how you know each other. They'll probably be glad you did.
