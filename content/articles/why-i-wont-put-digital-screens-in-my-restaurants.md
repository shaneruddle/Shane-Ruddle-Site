Over the years I've been approached by two or three companies wanting to digitise my restaurants, one of them run by a friend: digital screens, ordering from the table, the lot. It's efficient and innovative, and I still wouldn't do it.

## Efficient, but

When I was in China recently, that's how almost every restaurant worked. It's very efficient. In McDonald's in Thailand now, you can order on a machine, wait for your number and collect your food. You barely speak to anyone except to say thanks. (More on that trip in [Silent Shanghai, noisy Bangkok](/articles/silent-shanghai-noisy-bangkok).)

I never want my restaurants to get to that stage.

## The human touch

In my restaurants, I encourage staff to get to know the customers. If we went fully digital, we'd have very few staff, if any, and that would disappear.

I think it's one of the main reasons people come to Cajun Life Cafe and Hemingways: the staff know their names, and they know the staff's names. That's part of the experience. (Here's [why remembering names matters](/articles/if-i-dont-remember-you).)

## Technology, yes. Replacing people, no

I'm all for using technology to make things more efficient behind the scenes. But in the restaurant business, I don't think I'd ever replace my staff with it. The people are part of what customers are paying for.
