Yes. In 2026, Pattaya is a great place to raise a family, and in my experience it's very safe, with the roads being the main risk. I've brought up my own kids here, and the city has changed enormously in the 20-plus years I've lived here. When I arrived, I'd have had real reservations: there wasn't much for children to do and only a handful of schools. Today there's plenty of both.

## Is Pattaya safe for children?

Very safe, in my experience. In over 20 years here, I've never felt my kids were at risk from crime.

The real danger is the roads. If you drive your children around in a car, you're in good shape. If you use a motorbike, everyone needs a helmet and you need to be an experienced rider. Either way, keep your wits about you. Pattaya isn't risk-free, but compared with parts of the West, I'd call it very safe.

## Doesn't the nightlife affect family life?

Not really, because families don't live near it. The nightlife is in the city centre, where the tourists are. Most families live in East Pattaya, where it's barely noticeable. Just as in any big city, you wouldn't raise children right in the middle of the busiest area, and here you don't have to.

## What's the best area in Pattaya for families?

East Pattaya, for three reasons. (I've compared [every area of Pattaya](/articles/best-area-to-live-in-pattaya) separately.)

- **Schools.** Most of the international schools are there.
- **Space.** It's mainly houses and villas. With a family of three or four, you need room, and family-sized condos are hard to find.
- **Activities.** Much of what kids do is on that side of town. My kids go to Planet Football there, and most of the golf driving ranges are there too.

## What is there for kids to do?

Plenty, and far more than 20 years ago. There are water parks, theme parks and zoos, all of a high standard, safe and reasonably priced. On top of that, there's football, golf, padel and tennis: pretty much everything kids would do at home.

## Which international schools are in Pattaya, and what do they cost?

The choice is now excellent, and some of the international schools are exceptional. The ones I'd mention are:

- **Rugby School Thailand,** arguably the best
- **Highgate International School Thailand,** a brand new school that opened in August 2026
- **Regents International School Pattaya,** one of the longest-established
- **Tara Pattana International School,** where my kids go, a little closer to the city centre but still in East Pattaya

Fees depend on the school and your child's year group. As of 2026, expect roughly ฿300,000 a year at the lower end to over ฿1 million at the top. Check each school's current fee schedule directly. I've written a fuller [parent's guide to international schools in Pattaya](/articles/international-schools-in-pattaya).

## What are schools here really like?

Be realistic: in my opinion, the education system in the West is more advanced. But the atmosphere in schools here is much better. Over the years, I've spoken with plenty of teachers who've taught both in the West and in Thailand, and they nearly all say they prefer teaching here. Children are more respectful, more willing to listen and learn, and the schools feel positive, friendly and family-oriented.

## What do children gain from growing up in Pattaya?

A different perspective on the world. My kids are in classes of around 14 or 15, with Thai, Chinese, Indian, Malaysian, Russian and English classmates. You'd find diversity in parts of the UK, but here it's genuinely multicultural, every day.

## What's the hardest part of raising kids here?

The heat. The weather never stops you doing anything; there are no rained-off matches or freezing winters. But because it's hot all the time, it can sap kids' enthusiasm. My children will still play football, but on a scorching afternoon they're not always as keen.

## When might a family leave Pattaya?

At university age. Bangkok has some very good universities. Still, for most international kids, I think the West has more to offer for higher education than Pattaya does, and that's usually when families think about moving on.

## My advice to parents thinking of moving

Do your research, and go in with your eyes open. Pattaya has its pitfalls, like anywhere. Look at the schools and visit them, rent in East Pattaya before you commit to buying, and plan how your kids will get around safely. Get those right, and I think it's a fantastic place to bring up children.
