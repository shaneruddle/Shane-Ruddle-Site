I think the medical care here is first class, and the customer service especially is absolutely first class. But my main advice on healthcare in Thailand is simple: get health insurance.

## Which hospital do we use in Pattaya?

We've always used Bangkok Hospital Pattaya. When I first arrived, it was one of the only international hospitals here, so naturally I went there, and I've gone there ever since.

## What are the main hospitals in Pattaya?

Bangkok Hospital Pattaya, on Sukhumvit Road, is the one most expats know, and it's where we go. Other private hospitals foreigners use include Pattaya Memorial Hospital and Pattaya International Hospital, and Banglamung Hospital is the main government hospital for the area. Private hospitals have English-speaking staff and international departments; government hospitals are far cheaper, but expect longer waits and less English.

For anything minor, there are small private clinics all over the city, and pharmacies can help with a lot without a prescription.

## When do we go to Bangkok?

Only occasionally, when we need a specialist that Bangkok Hospital Pattaya doesn't have, such as a children's dentist or a children's optician. Then we go to Bumrungrad in Bangkok.

Both our children were born at Samitivej Hospital in Sriracha. We looked at three or four places, and it wasn't about price. We really liked the doctor there, and that's what decided it.

## How much does a hospital visit cost in Pattaya?

A standard hospital visit is normally between ฿2,000 and ฿3,000, depending on whether you need medication.

## Why is health insurance so important in Thailand?

There are a lot of stories in Thailand of uninsured patients who can't pay being turned away by private hospitals and sent to a government hospital instead. Sometimes that can be critical. Getting insurance really can be life-saving, so that situation never happens to you.

Even with insurance, you're not necessarily out of the woods. Every policy has a limit, and depending on what's wrong, a claim can skyrocket quickly. Having insurance doesn't automatically mean you're fully covered, so check your limits. It's the main reason I've seen [retirees leave Pattaya](/articles/do-retirees-leave-pattaya).

## What should you check in an expat health insurance policy?

Read the policy before you buy, not when you claim. The things I'd look at:

- **The yearly limit.** A serious illness or an accident can run into millions of baht. A low limit can run out fast.
- **In-patient and out-patient.** Some cheaper policies only cover you once you're admitted. That's fine if you're happy paying for small visits yourself.
- **Pre-existing conditions.** Most policies exclude them, at least at first. Be honest on the form, or a claim can be refused.
- **Age.** The older you are, the harder and more expensive cover is to buy, and some insurers won't take new customers past a certain age. If you're planning to retire here, buy it before you need it and keep it going.
- **Your visa.** Some visas, such as the retirement O-A, need a policy with a set minimum cover. Check what your visa needs before you choose. More on my [retirement visa page](/thai-visas/retirement-visa).

A local broker can compare policies for you, and doesn't usually cost you anything extra.

## Who do we insure with?

My whole family is insured with Pacific Cross, an international insurer, through a local broker, Macallan Insurance, founded by Jack Levy. Most of my friends insure through Jack's company too.

Whether you're staying long term or even short term, get health insurance before you need it.
