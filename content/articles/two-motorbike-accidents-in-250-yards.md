On a short drive along Thepprasit Road one rainy morning, I saw two motorbike accidents within about 250 yards of each other. It isn't unusual. If it's raining and you're on the road for half an hour in Pattaya, you can almost guarantee you'll see one. Motorbikes here are dangerous, and I want people to take that seriously.

## What I saw

The first one happened just ahead of me. I was two cars back and didn't see the impact. I heard it, then saw an older Thai man flung from his motorbike across the road. It looked like it had happened at a reasonable speed. When he hit the ground he had no helmet on.

A little further on was a powerful motorbike ridden by a foreigner. He seemed okay, and he was running back to help. I don't know what happened or whose fault it was. I only know what it looked like, and it looked horrific.

About 250 yards later there was a second accident. A Thai woman was lying at the side of the road with her motorbike on its side. Someone was already helping her and had put a cloth over her to keep her out of the rain. She was moving, and she still had her helmet on.

Two accidents, one stretch of road, one wet morning.

## Rain makes it far worse

Rain turns Pattaya's roads into something else. They get slippery, visibility drops, and drivers don't slow down enough. That's when you see the most accidents, and it's when you most need to think twice about getting on a bike.

## My advice: don't rent a motorbike

I'll be upfront. Pattaya Rent a Car also rents out motorbikes, through Pattaya Rent a Bike. Even so, my personal advice is not to rent one.

We try not to rent bikes to people with little or no experience, but it isn't always easy. We have to take people's word when we ask, and not everyone is honest about it.

If you've never ridden a motorbike before, don't learn in Thailand. And even if you're a confident rider at home, remember it's not just about you. It's everyone else on the road.

## If you do ride

- **Take it steady.** Don't be overconfident, however good you think you are.
- **Keep your head on a swivel.** Expect cars, bikes and people to do the unexpected.
- **Wear a helmet every time.** Even if you're only going to 7-Eleven and it feels like a hassle.
- **Get personal insurance.** Rental insurance usually covers the bike, not you. Check that your travel insurance covers motorbikes, and whether it needs you to hold a motorbike licence and wear a helmet.
- **Be extra careful in the rain**, or better still, don't ride in it.

## Expect the unexpected

I've written before about [crossing the road](/articles/expect-the-unexpected-crossing-the-road-in-pattaya) and about [minibuses](/articles/minibuses-in-thailand-speak-up). This is the same story. Thailand is a wonderful place, but the roads are where it can go badly wrong. If you're not experienced, don't ride. If you do, ride as if every car on the road hasn't seen you.
