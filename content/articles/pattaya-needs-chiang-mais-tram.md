Chiang Mai has just done something I've been suggesting for Pattaya for years. On 18 November 2026, it launched the Chiang Mai Tram by Lannatique, an electric bus styled like a tram, running a 15-stop loop around the old city. A single journey costs 20 baht, and a one-day hop-on, hop-off pass is 99 baht.

I really hope it's a success, and I hope City Hall here is paying attention.

## We don't need a tram in the sky

Whenever a monorail for Pattaya comes up online, my answer is the same: we don't need one. A monorail would be expensive, very disruptive and take years to build. It would also mean acquiring a lot of land.

What Pattaya needs is exactly what Chiang Mai has just introduced: an electric, tram-style bus that simply loops the main roads. One loop along Pattaya Beach Road and Second Road, and another along Jomtien Beach Road and Second Road. Hop-on, hop-off passes, or single trips for 20 baht.

It's an absolute must, and Chiang Mai could be the perfect pilot scheme.

## The baht bus problem

The real challenge here is the baht buses. They've always been part of Pattaya, and they employ a fair number of people. I think that's the main reason there would be reluctance, because the obvious result of a tram system is that the baht buses would be needed far less. I've written before about [Pattaya's baht buses](/articles/pattayas-baht-buses) and why something cleaner and more organised would be better.

## What I'd do about the drivers

Hire the good ones to drive the trams. Realistically, though, there would be far fewer trams than baht buses, so maybe only around 10% of drivers would be re-employed that way.

Beyond that, it's a bit like the conversation about AI. When technology changes, and the change is better for the environment and for the city as a whole, you can't refuse to do it just because some jobs will go. Thailand has relatively low unemployment, so most drivers could find other work.

Imagine holding back for years out of fear, only to finally do it and find the drivers simply moved on to other jobs, with no uproar, no pushback and no campaigning.

This isn't a criticism of anyone. It's a suggestion for City Hall to consider.

## The other side

Baht buses are flexible and go where fixed routes don't, and many drivers have built their livelihoods around them over decades. A fixed tram loop wouldn't replace that overnight, and losing even part of that income would hit real families. Any change would need proper consultation, a fair transition for drivers, and probably a period where both run side by side.

## My take

Pattaya doesn't need a billion-baht project in the sky. It needs a simple, clean, cheap loop along the roads we already have. Chiang Mai has shown how. I hope we follow.
