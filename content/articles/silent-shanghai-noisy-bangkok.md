I was in Shanghai and Shenzhen recently, and the first thing I noticed was the silence. Then I came to Bangkok, and the noise hit me straight away. China's streets are cleaner and quieter. Bangkok's feel alive.

## Shanghai and Shenzhen: almost silent

On the roads in Shanghai and Shenzhen, it felt like 95% of the vehicles were electric. The traffic is nearly silent. It's a little eerie.

You could easily say that's great. It's better for the environment and better for your ears, and it's probably where the rest of the world is heading. (I've compared the two countries more broadly in [Thailand vs China](/articles/thailand-vs-china).)

There is a downside, though. We nearly got clipped a couple of times by electric scooters we simply didn't hear coming.

## Bangkok: noise everywhere

Bangkok is the opposite. Tuk-tuks roar past, motorbikes weave through the traffic, and if there are any electric vehicles, you'd never know, because the petrol and diesel engines drown them out.

There are people everywhere. The pavements are full of stalls and tents you have to negotiate your way around. It's messy, it's dirty and it's noisy.

## Why I prefer the chaos

But it's Thailand, and it feels alive. After a few days of near-silence in China, walking back into the noise of Bangkok felt like coming home.

I'm sure Thailand will go electric eventually, and the air will be better for it. I just hope it never gets so quiet that it loses that buzz, the kind you get from [Pattaya's baht buses](/articles/pattayas-baht-buses) and [street food carts](/articles/pattayas-street-food-carts).

I've written more about what that silence could mean on Thai roads in [if electric motorbikes take off in Thailand, make them noisy](/articles/if-electric-motorbikes-take-off-in-thailand).
