I'm sat in a coffee shop overlooking Pattaya Bay, and right in front of me is the Waterfront condo, still standing in its derelict state. It's the clearest warning I know for anyone buying off-plan in Pattaya: you don't own anything until you hold the title deed. ## What happened

This was before I got involved in property, so I may not have every detail right. The Waterfront Suites and Residence sits at the south end of the bay, near Bali Hai pier at the foot of Pratumnak Hill. It's 53 storeys, with 312 units that foreigners and Thais bought for between ฿4 million and ฿10 million. Construction was halted in 2014.

As I understand it, the question is whether the land title was ever properly issued, with the city saying the building encroaches on public land. The project had its approvals and an environmental impact assessment, and then the authorities changed their position. It's been tied up in legal disputes for over a decade.

## Why it would have been so hard to take

I didn't know anyone who'd put money in personally, but it shows the risks better than anything.

Most failed projects in Pattaya never really get off the ground. This one did. The structure was up. The rendering and the internals were still to do, but you could see the building. If you'd bought a unit and walked past that frame, you'd have thought your money was relatively safe, and that most of the off-plan risk was behind you.

It wasn't. You don't know until the building is completely finished.

## Even finished isn't enough

There are two other condos in that area that were fully completed, structure and internals. But buyers couldn't transfer units into their names, because the developer owed money to the bank. Effectively, the bank owned the project and wouldn't release the title deeds until the debt was settled.

So even if the building is complete, until you have that title deed in your name, there's still a risk.

## What I'd check before buying off-plan

- **The developer's reputation.** Go with developers who have a good track record of finishing projects.
- **Their sell-through.** Look at how quickly their previous projects sold. That's where developers usually get into trouble.
- **The title deed.** Don't treat a finished building as a finished deal until the unit is transferred into your name.

In my experience, developers fail in one of two ways. Either the project is trouble from the start, or they go in with the best intentions but don't sell enough units, and run out of money to finish. ## Knock it down

I don't think the Waterfront can be finished now. It's been standing so long that locals almost block it out, but it's a real eyesore.

The bay itself looks better than it has in years. The pavements and walkways are much improved, the beach has been widened, and there's the Pattaya sign on the hill that the city is rightly proud of. And right beside it, you've got this.

## The latest

It's moving again. After a 31 October deadline to clear the area passed, Pattaya City removed barriers put up by the condo's management and reclaimed what it says is public land around the building. The city says it will go back to the Land Department for a final, binding ruling on the title. If the title was improperly issued, the owner will be ordered to demolish it, and if they don't, the city can start demolition itself.

Will it actually come down? Honestly, I'd be surprised. There's been talk of demolition for years and it's never happened. Plenty of unfinished buildings just stay up forever. There are a few in Bangkok, and a low-rise one just off Thepprasit that's been there over 20 years.

If it weren't in such a prominent spot, I think this one would stay too. But the city must be very keen to get rid of it, and the fact it's taken this long tells you how complicated the case must be. I hope this is the time it finally gets pushed through, because it's a glaring reminder of how wrong things can go here.
