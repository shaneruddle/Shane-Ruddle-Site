The courses in Thailand are excellent, but for me golf here is hotter, slower and, once you add everything up, more expensive than in the UK. That's a big part of why I hardly ever play now.

## My golf background

I started playing at 15, and for the best part of nine years before I came to Thailand I played almost every day. At my best I was a plus one handicap, which means shooting 71 on a par-72 course just to play to my handicap.

I played county golf for Somerset and Dorset, played university golf, and was lucky enough to play for English Universities in a match against Scotland. I then turned pro and enrolled on the PGA's three-year training programme, but I was about a year in when I moved to Thailand, so I never finished it or qualified as a teaching pro. I let the membership lapse, because there was no benefit to renewing it here. So I've played a lot of golf in both countries, and I've stopped playing regularly for about the last 10 years.

## The courses are different

The golf courses here are good, but they're all man-made, parkland-style courses. Back home I grew up on links and heathland golf, so it took time to adjust.

The biggest change was around the greens. A lot of greens here are elevated. In the UK, you'd take out an eight or nine iron and bump and run the ball onto the green. Here, nearly everything around the green is a flop shot. The pace of the greens took some getting used to as well.

## Grainy greens and caddies

The greens here are very grainy. You can read a putt as breaking right to left, but if the grain runs the other way it holds the ball up. You only really learn that with local knowledge.

That's where caddies come in. In the UK we don't have them. Here, [they're compulsory at most courses](/articles/caddies-in-thailand-should-be-optional). For higher handicappers, a caddy definitely helps. At my level, the main help was reading those grainy greens, and a good caddy is worth their weight in gold.

Unfortunately, most aren't good. Many are just bag carriers, which is fine. They're someone to chat to, and you can practise a bit of Thai with them, which is fun at first. But a poor caddy can make for a long, dull round.

## The heat

In the UK you hardly think about the weather in terms of your body. Here you have to manage it: stay hydrated, eat during the round, and pace yourself. I find it too hot to play comfortably, which is one reason I don't play much now.

## Is it cheaper in Thailand?

Most people assume golf in Thailand is cheap. Green fee for green fee, it often is. But once you add the caddy fee, the caddy tip and often a cart, it adds up. Carts are optional in the UK and usually unnecessary, but here a lot of people take one because of the heat, and some courses make them compulsory. In my opinion, golf here works out more expensive than in the UK.

I'm biased, mind you. As a PGA trainee I never paid green fees in the UK, because the card gave me reciprocal access to courses, so I played some great courses for free. Paying for golf in Thailand was always going to feel expensive.

## It takes all day

This is the one that really stopped me. Most courses around Pattaya are at least 30 minutes out of the city, and most are 45. Add time before your tee-off, a five-hour round, a shower and the drive home, and it's easily eight hours.

In the UK, most people live within five kilometres of a golf course. I always did, wherever I lived. Door to door, a round would take about four hours. Here, it's a whole day, and with businesses to run, that's hard to justify.

## Where I'd play around Pattaya

If you're coming on a golf holiday and want the best courses:

- **Siam Country Club.** It has several courses, and you could play all of them. If you only play one or two, make one of them Waterside.
- **Chee Chan Golf Resort.**
- **Laem Chabang International Country Club.**

More on courses, costs and the best time to play in [golf in Pattaya](/articles/golf-in-pattaya).

## Will I ever play properly again?

Probably, but I think it would take moving back to the UK. If I did, I'd be back into golf 100%. The other possibility is retirement, when I'm looking for ways to fill my days. Until then, golf in Thailand just isn't a good use of my time.
