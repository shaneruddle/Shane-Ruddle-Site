When I first came to Pattaya more than 20 years ago, Walking Street was the centre of the nightlife scene, and I don't mind saying I was a regular. I'd go maybe once a week.

I remember friends who'd been here longer telling me they hadn't been down there for years. In my twenties, that seemed bizarre to me. Then, five or six years later, I stopped going too. It got a bit boring, and as you get older your lifestyle and priorities change.

## Back after ten years

I hadn't been down Walking Street at night for well over ten years. Then, at the fireworks festival this week, I walked through it at about nine o'clock, the latest I'd been there in a decade.

It was a very different Walking Street from the one I remember.

## Who's there now

The first thing I noticed was the crowd. There were lots of families, and visitors from all over the world: India, the Middle East, Russia and China. Twenty years ago it was mainly Westerners. That simply reflects how Pattaya's tourism has changed.

## A sightseeing walk, not a night out

The atmosphere has changed with it. It used to be a nightlife scene, where people went into the venues for their night out. Now it feels more like a tourist attraction. Tour groups walk up and down behind a flag, twenty or thirty people at a time who've just got off a bus to experience it.

Most of the activity is now on the street rather than inside the businesses. And the businesses have changed too. There are far more restaurants, live music venues, shops and even fast food chains.

## Better or worse?

It depends who you are. Personally, it doesn't bother me. I don't go there any more, and I don't have a business there.

But if I did have a business on Walking Street, I'd definitely think it was a change for the worse. People are walking up and down sightseeing, but they're not spending. They might buy a coconut or a bottle of water from 7-Eleven, but they're not spending thousands of baht on a night out.

## My take

Walking Street hasn't died. It's turned into something else: less a night out, more a sight to see. That might be good for Pattaya's image, but it's a lot harder for the businesses that depend on people actually coming inside.
