AI was supposed to give us all time back. It hasn't worked out that way for me. I'm getting far more done than ever, but I'm also working more, and my brain rarely gets a rest. Someone posted about this online recently, and it summed up exactly how I feel some days.

## The boring work was a rest break

Before AI, my working day had a rhythm. Some of it was thinking, strategy and decisions. A good chunk of it was mundane work: relabelling images, adding internal links, tidying things up.

I never thought much about the boring part. It was just part of the process. But that work was giving my brain a breather. I was still working, but I wasn't making decisions. It recharged me for the next round of thinking.

Now AI does most of the mundane stuff. What's left is the decision-making, all day, one call after another. And that is exhausting in a way the old days weren't.

## You can do everything now, so you do

The other side is that there's hardly anything you can't do yourself anymore. As a business owner, I used to hand off graphic design and some marketing. Now I can do it myself with AI. (Here's [how I use AI in my businesses](/articles/how-i-use-ai-in-my-businesses).)

So over the last year I've found myself doing more work than before, not less. To be fair, I'm also getting an awful lot more done. But the promised extra free time hasn't appeared. The extra capacity has simply filled up.

## Not tied to a desk, but never switched off

There's a flip side. I'm no longer stuck at my computer. A lot of my work now happens on the go. As I talked this through, I was driving through traffic, which wasn't an option before.

That's freedom of a kind. But it also means I'm not really switching off. I'm just working in a different place. (See also [do I ever switch off?](/articles/do-i-ever-switch-off))

## What helps

I haven't changed much yet. I still take holidays. I've been thinking about meditating again, which I used to do a lot. It's a good way to slow your thinking down and give your brain the rest that boring work used to provide. If you're feeling the same, it's worth a try.

## The thinking can go too

Here's the part some people won't like. If you really lean into AI and start to trust it, you realise it can take a lot of the thinking off you as well, not just the mundane work.

Is there a risk? Of course. But a risk compared to what? Take a blog post. With AI, I can get it done, though there might be a few inaccuracies. The alternative often isn't a perfect post. It's no post at all.

Most of what I do isn't so critical that a small error is the end of the world. I'd rather get things done, even if they aren't perfect.

That said, I still check the things that really matter, like contracts, money and anything legal, myself. I'm still the orchestrator, and the important decisions are still mine. Lean in where mistakes are cheap, and keep your own judgement where they aren't.
