Sometimes you do something to solve one problem and end up creating another. This is a small example, but a good one.

## Why I sent the email

A couple of staff had recently left one of my property businesses, and I wanted to make sure customers knew exactly who was on our team. The main reason was to protect them. I didn't want anyone sending money to someone who wasn't on the team, or to anyone falsely claiming to represent us.

So I sent an email to our customers introducing the current team. I was in it too, as the owner.

## How it backfired

A couple of people who read it hadn't realised I owned the company. One of them was a customer who'd had a complaint with us in the past.

The moment he realised I was the owner, he got in touch with me directly and explained the problem. We knew each other, and he'd let it slide before, but now he thought, why not just give me a call?

And to be fair, we were in the wrong. The company ended up paying him 12,000 baht.

## Would I send it again?

Absolutely. Protecting customers from paying the wrong people matters far more than 12,000 baht. It was just a typical backfire.

## My take

When you fix one problem, expect it to bring up another now and then. And if an old complaint resurfaces and you were in the wrong, the best thing is to put it right.
