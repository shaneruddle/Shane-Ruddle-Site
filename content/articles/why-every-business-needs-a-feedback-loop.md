The businesses that improve are the ones that listen. Across my restaurants and Pattaya Rent a Car, we have a few simple ways of getting feedback, and the most important part isn't collecting it. It's acting on it.

## How we get feedback

**A little form with the bill.** In the restaurants, there's a short feedback form in the folder when customers pay. It isn't pushed in anyone's face. They can fill it in if they want to. The managers read them every day to see if anything needs acting on, and because customers leave their details, the managers often contact them directly for more context. Nobody writes an essay on those little forms.

**Reviews.** We read our reviews too. Most are positive, so there isn't often something to put right, and when there is, there's not always enough detail to understand what happened. It's harder to get hold of someone from a Google review, but it isn't impossible, and we do reach out.

**The staff.** We encourage our staff to tell their managers when customers complain or keep asking about something. Sometimes I'll go into a business myself and ask the staff directly. They're the boots on the ground. They hear the questions and the complaints first.

## What we've changed because of it

**New dishes.** Customers sometimes tell us they love a particular dish somewhere else and wish we did it. That's how beef stew ended up on the menu at Hemingways Pattaya.

**More seats outside.** At Hemingways Pattaya, we recently made the outside area bigger and the inside smaller. That was purely down to feedback. More people wanted to sit outside in the evening and watch the world go by, and we didn't have enough seats out there.

**A simpler booking process.** At Pattaya Rent a Car, we looked at the questions people kept asking and answered them up front. Some of those answers now sit right on the rental form, so the process is transparent and nobody has to dig around for information.

## My take

Feedback is only useful if it closes the loop: collect it, read it, follow up, and change something. Make it easy for customers to tell you, listen to your staff, and don't be afraid to make big changes when the feedback keeps pointing the same way.
