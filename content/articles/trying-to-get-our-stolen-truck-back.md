When one of our trucks was stolen through [a romance scam](/articles/the-romance-scam-that-cost-us-a-truck), we lost about ฿800,000. A few months later, another ฿60,000 went trying to get it back. If something of yours is ever stolen in Thailand, here's what not to do.

## Where we were

The truck, a Toyota Revo, had a GPS tracker. But thieves use signal jammers these days, and the last location we had showed it was still in Thailand. After that, nothing.

We went to the police, but because the woman who rented it was also a victim, there wasn't much they could do. And because of how our insurance worked, there was no payout. The person who took the truck was someone she knew and had invited into it, so the insurer treated it as a breach of contract rather than a theft. The whole cost came back to Pattaya Rent a Car.

What usually happens to stolen cars here is that they're driven across a border, often with some sort of arrangement there. The plates or chassis numbers get changed, or not, and the car gets driven until the engine dies.

## The message

About three months later, my manager was still convinced the truck was in Thailand. She posted about it in one of the Facebook groups where car dealers and rental companies share information on stolen vehicles.

A man got in touch. He said he had our Revo. He'd paid a few hundred thousand baht for it, and he admitted he knew something wasn't right, because the price was so low.

His offer was this. If we wanted the truck back, he'd drive it down to Chonburi from another province, but we'd have to pay him ฿10,000 up front for the trip. When we saw the truck, we'd pay him another ฿190,000 to cover what he'd paid.

It was a risk. But ฿200,000 sounded a lot better than ฿800,000. I agreed to front the ฿10,000 and told my manager she could pay the rest once she'd actually seen the truck.

## The meeting

She went to meet him at a local Tesco Lotus, on time. Then he messaged her.

He said he didn't trust that she hadn't brought the police. So he'd park the truck somewhere and tell her where it was. She could go and check it before paying the ฿190,000. But first, he wanted an extra ฿50,000, just to tell her where it was.

She called me. I told her plainly not to do it. It was clearly a scam, and if she paid, it was her own decision.

She paid anyway, with her own money. The phone went dead. We never heard from him again.

## What I did about it

That left me in a difficult spot. We'd already lost a truck, and now my manager had made a second mistake trying to make up for the first, after I'd told her not to.

I decided not to simply cover the ฿50,000. But I didn't want her carrying the whole loss in one go either. So I lent her the money, and we agreed she'd pay it back in small monthly amounts over several months.

## The lessons

- **Assume anyone offering to return stolen property for cash up front is scamming you.** Real buyers who've been sold a stolen car don't usually come forward asking for money.
- **Never pay before you see what you're paying for.** Not the transport fee, not the "extra to tell you where it is". Once you've paid, you have no leverage.
- **Watch out when the demands keep changing.** The moment the deal moves, "just a bit more and you're nearly there", walk away. That's how they hook you.
- **Involve the police** if someone claims to have your stolen property.
- **Hope makes you vulnerable.** My manager wanted that truck back so badly that she ignored what was obvious. Scammers count on exactly that.

In the end, the romance scam cost us a truck. The hope of getting it back cost another ฿60,000.
