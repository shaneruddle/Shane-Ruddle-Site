I once sold a bar to a keen buyer at my asking price. A few months later, the landlady sold the building, the new owner pushed the rent up, and the buyer decided I'd planned the whole thing. I hadn't. He ignored my advice, paid the price, and apologised years later. ## How I came to own it

The Golf Club was a bar and restaurant in LK Metro. It was owned by three or four guys who'd bought it mainly as somewhere to hang out. They were comfortably off and weren't really trying to make money from it.

When COVID hit, they decided not to reopen. But if someone wanted to have a go and they could get something for it, why not? I knew them, so I bought it, and ran it as The Golf Club for about a year.

In that time I built a good relationship with the landlady. She was a very nice lady and very sympathetic during COVID, and gave me a really good deal on the rent. More in [how my businesses got through COVID](/articles/how-my-businesses-got-through-covid).

## Selling it

After about a year, for a few different reasons, I decided I wanted out. I advertised it online and got only one inquiry. But he was very keen from the first contact, and paid pretty much my asking price.

## The twist

Three or four months after he bought it, the landlady, who had never mentioned it before, decided to sell the building. The new owner came in and put the rent up significantly.

The buyer thought I'd orchestrated the whole thing. I absolutely hadn't. It was pure coincidence.

## The advice he ignored

I told him I had nothing to do with it. I also told him the new landlord couldn't simply cancel his existing lease and start a new one. Under Section 569 of the Thai Civil and Commercial Code, a lease isn't ended when the property is sold. The new owner takes on the old owner's rights and duties towards the tenant, so the remaining term has to be honoured. Two caveats: a lease longer than three years needs to be registered at the Land Office to be enforceable beyond three years, and extras such as renewal options may not bind a new owner.

But because he was already upset with me, he ignored that advice. He ended up in all kinds of trouble and paid a lot more for the lease than he needed to.

Years later, he came back and effectively apologised, once he'd found out the new owner was the one in the wrong.

## The lesson

Don't believe everything you're told. Don't assume someone knows more about how things work here just because they're local. That isn't always the case. And not everyone's morals and ethics line up with yours.

If you come across someone with a good reputation for being honest in business, don't throw that away because something feels off or someone else tells you otherwise. Don't assume the worst of people, but don't assume the best of them either.

If you're buying a business, check the lease properly: how long it runs, whether it's registered, and what happens if the building is sold.
