The latest figures show foreign arrivals to Thailand down around 5% this year. Most of that drop is Chinese visitors. In my opinion, the reason underneath it is safety, and China's own travel warning backs that up. Unless Thailand takes safety seriously, these incidents will keep happening, the bad press will keep coming, and the numbers won't recover.

## It's mainly about China

The Ministry of Tourism's figures for 1 January to 14 November 2026 show 26.76 million foreign arrivals. Revenue was almost unchanged at around ฿1.3 trillion, so the people who are coming are spending more.

The big change is China. Chinese visitors used to make up such a large share of arrivals that when their numbers fall, the whole total falls with them. Chinese arrivals dropped from about 6.7 million in 2024 to around 4.5 million in 2025, and for a while Malaysia even overtook China as the biggest source market. (I looked at the wider picture in [why tourism in Thailand is down](/articles/why-tourism-in-thailand-is-down).)

My sense is that China is also encouraging its people to holiday at home. That's not something Thailand can control.

## What the warning actually said

In October, Chinese arrivals fell again after the Chinese embassy in Bangkok issued a safety advisory. Some people read that as "Thailand is unsafe". It wasn't.

The warning was about fake job offers, kidnappings and people being lured towards scam compounds near the Myanmar border. A well-known Chinese actor was famously lured into one of these. China's ambassador said the advisory was preventive and "in no way directed at Thailand".

But that's the point. Even a targeted warning knocks confidence, and confidence is what tourism runs on.

## Quieter, but not for everyone

Pattaya definitely feels quieter. You notice it on the roads, where the Chinese tour buses used to be. (More in [Pattaya is quieter this year](/articles/pattaya-is-quieter-this-year).)

My own businesses don't feel quieter, because Chinese tourists were never our main customers. And honestly, a city that isn't overrun is a nicer place to be. Depending on your business, this could actually be a better time than previous years.

If you run speedboats from the bay to Koh Larn, a big seafood restaurant or a tour company, it's a different story. You'll be feeling the pinch far more than me and my friends.

## Safety is the real issue

Here's what worries me more than the headline numbers. If it's not scam centres, it's road safety. If it's not that, it's romance scams, stories of police corruption, or the current crackdown on [nominee companies](/articles/nominee-companies-in-thailand).

Some of these stories go around the world. This summer, two young Russian siblings, aged 22 and 17, were murdered in the Pattaya area. On 16 November, a fire swept through a building in Patong, Phuket, and killed 11 people, with the cause still under investigation. The same day, a Russian woman was hit and killed on a pedestrian crossing in Jomtien. (I wrote about that in [expect the unexpected: crossing the road in Pattaya](/articles/expect-the-unexpected-crossing-the-road-in-pattaya).)

The frustrating thing is that day to day, Thailand is a very safe country. Once you live here, the idea that it's dangerous just doesn't match what you see. Violent crime against visitors is rare. The bigger risks are the avoidable ones. You're unlikely to be attacked, but you might be electrocuted by faulty wiring, or hit on a zebra crossing. That's where things need to change. (See also [is Pattaya safe?](/articles/is-pattaya-safe))

When safety goes unchecked, the bad publicity follows. Foreign governments and embassies pick up on it, issue warnings, and visitors choose somewhere else. That hits the economy hard, and it takes years to win that trust back.

To be fair, Thailand is acting on some of this. There have been crackdowns on scam compounds along the border, and most visitors have a safe, brilliant time here. But it needs to keep going, and it needs to be seen to be going.

## My verdict

Tourism numbers are down, and I believe safety concerns are a big part of why. China's warning shows that clearly. As long as avoidable tragedies keep making international news, governments will keep issuing warnings and people will keep choosing somewhere else.

If Thailand wants people to come back, and wants tourism that lasts, it needs to look seriously at safety. Not just to bring the Chinese back, but for visitors from everywhere. Get that right, and the numbers will look after themselves.
