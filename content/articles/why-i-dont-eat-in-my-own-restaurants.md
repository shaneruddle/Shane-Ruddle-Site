I own several restaurants, but I rarely eat in them. I'd rather eat in someone else's. It's more relaxing, nobody brings me complaints over dinner, and I usually learn something. More in [where a restaurant owner eats in Pattaya](/articles/where-a-restaurant-owner-eats-in-pattaya).

## I don't want to be disturbed while I eat

I don't generally like customers to know I'm the owner. When I sit down to eat, I just want to eat. Most people understand that, and would think it rude to interrupt anyone mid-meal, owner or not. But not everyone does.

More than once, I've been halfway through my food when a customer came over to raise a complaint. I can't remember what that particular complaint was. I just remember thinking it was inappropriate, and deciding afterwards that I'd eat in other people's restaurants from then on. More in [why the owner isn't the best person to fix your problem](/articles/why-the-owner-isnt-the-best-person-to-fix-your-problem).

## I can't switch off in my own place

After years in the restaurant game, I notice things the moment I walk in. The music is wrong, or too loud. A light bulb is out. A customer has walked in and nobody has greeted them yet.

I notice those things in anyone's restaurant. In someone else's, I can let them go. In my own, I can't relax until they're fixed, so I'm watching the room instead of enjoying my meal. More in [do I ever switch off?](/articles/do-i-ever-switch-off)

## I learn more eating elsewhere

Eating out somewhere else is a chance to experience another restaurant, good or bad. If it's good, I might learn something I can bring back. If it's bad, it's reassuring to know nobody gets it right all the time.

## I'm not the one to judge the food anyway

To be honest, I don't know enough about food to check the quality properly. I know what I like, and I can tell if a dish tastes different from last time. But I can't cook. I'd struggle to make beans on toast with cheese on top, and that's about the extent of my food knowledge.

## Customers do the quality checking

Instead of me or my partners going in to test the food, we rely on our customers. In most of our restaurants, every customer gets a feedback slip with the bill, which takes a minute to fill in.

The managers read them. If something needs following up, they follow it up. If there's an obvious mistake, or the same issue keeps coming up, we change it. Otherwise there'd be no point printing them and asking people to fill them in.
