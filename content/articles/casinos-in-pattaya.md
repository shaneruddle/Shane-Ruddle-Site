Every few years the idea of legal casinos in Thailand comes back. The latest version, the Entertainment Complex Bill, was approved by the cabinet in 2025, shelved after protests, rejected by a Senate committee, and is now being talked about again. My view is simple. If it happened, Pattaya would be better off, and it's the obvious place for it. But I genuinely don't think it ever will.

## Why it would be good for Pattaya

They're not talking about just a casino. The plan has always been a whole complex, with big hotels and everything that comes with them. The investment would be massive, and there'd be a lot more development on the back of it. Condo prices might go up a little too. More in [the Pattaya property market](/articles/pattaya-property-market).

It would also draw in a lot of visitors from big Asian markets where people travel to gamble. With tourism down at the moment, that matters. More in [why tourism in Thailand is down](/articles/why-tourism-in-thailand-is-down).

## It would help Thailand compete

Plenty of Thais have long crossed the border to gamble in Cambodia's casinos. A resort here would keep some of that money in the country, and might even tempt some gamblers away from places like Macau.

## Where I'd put it

Out of town, because it would be a big complex. Somewhere between Pattaya and U-Tapao airport makes a lot of sense. People could fly straight into U-Tapao, stay three or four days, and fly home.

## The risks, and the Singapore model

The big worry is people gambling money they can't afford to lose. I've seen what a gambling problem can do: I once had a manager whose gambling became a real issue. More in [hiring and firing in Pattaya](/articles/hiring-and-firing-in-pattaya). Gambling is already popular here, just look at how widely the lottery is played. Without the right safeguards, I'd fear people gambling their pay away and some turning to crime to feed the habit.

That's why I think it would have to follow the Singapore model, which is built around tourists. Singaporean citizens and permanent residents pay a hefty levy just to walk in, S$150 a day. That keeps out people who can't afford it and are just chancing their luck.

## Why I don't think it will happen

In the 20-odd years I've been here, the idea has been floated so many times and it has never got very far. Thailand is a democracy, and a law like this has to get through a lot of opposition. The current tourism slump might give the parties in favour a chance to push it ahead, but I have no reason to believe it will ever actually happen.
