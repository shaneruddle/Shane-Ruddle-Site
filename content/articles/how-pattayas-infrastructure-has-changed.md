Driving along Jomtien Beach Road on a sunny morning recently, I was struck by how much it has changed. After more than 20 years here, Pattaya is a very different place, and mostly for the better.

## The private sector led the way

When I first arrived, there weren't really any shopping centres. A lot of Pattaya's biggest improvements since then have come from the private sector: new schools, new hospitals and new shopping centres.

## Better roads

The public sector has improved the roads. Some, like Jomtien Second Road, simply didn't exist when I arrived. The road between Pattaya and Jomtien was basically a dirt track. Opening that up has made Jomtien far more accessible.

## Wider beaches

The beach improvements are more recent. In Jomtien, and in Pattaya too, they've widened the beach a lot. Where it might once have been around 15 metres deep in places, much of Jomtien beach is now around 50 metres wide after the first phase of sand replenishment, and a second phase is under way.

It looks great, and it means the city can host events on the beach. The International Fireworks Festival is on this weekend, there was a jazz festival earlier this month, and there are more beach volleyball courts.

## Pavements, cables and greenery

The pavements have improved, although there's still room for more. Some of the overhead cabling has gone underground, with plenty still to do. They've also added greenery in places, which makes a real difference.

## Still a long way to go

It's definitely improving, and improving all the time. But in terms of really beautifying the whole area, I'd say we're less than halfway there.

This isn't a criticism. It's encouragement to keep going.

## My take

If you haven't been to Pattaya for a few years, you'll notice the difference. If you've never been, the Pattaya you've heard about may not be the Pattaya you find.
