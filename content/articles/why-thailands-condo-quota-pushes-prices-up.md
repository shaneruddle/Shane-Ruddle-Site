Foreigners can only own up to 49% of the floor area in a Thai condo building. As I understand it, the idea is to stop foreign money pushing prices up. In Pattaya, I think it's doing the opposite, and leaving a lot of empty condos behind.

## Think like a developer

Imagine you're a developer. You can sell 49% of your building to foreigners, and in Pattaya that's where most of the demand is. The other 51% has to go to Thai buyers, and if that demand isn't there, those units are hard to shift.

You still have to pay for the whole building. So the foreign units, the ones people actually want, end up carrying the cost. In popular buildings the foreign quota sells out, and foreign-quota units sell at a premium, especially on resale.

To be fair, a developer can't simply charge whatever they like. Buyers set the price in the end. But the restriction squeezes foreign demand into fewer units, and that pushes the price of those units up.

## Building more just to get more quota

Here's the bit that really doesn't make sense. Developers I know are building more projects simply to meet foreign demand. Every new building gives them another 49% to sell to foreigners.

But every new building also comes with another 51% of Thai-quota units. And in Pattaya, a lot of those are just sitting empty.

So there are an awful lot of unsold condos here, yet developers keep building, because they need more foreign quota to sell. (More in [is Pattaya overbuilt?](/articles/is-pattaya-overbuilt))

## What I'd change

Simple: allow 100% foreign ownership. The empty Thai-quota units sitting in Pattaya today would sell, and with that much existing stock to sell, there'd be far less reason to build more. Any new development could sell 100% of its units to whoever wants them, so developers wouldn't need to keep building just to get more quota. I think you'd end up with less development, not more.

## The other side

Supporters of the quota argue it protects Thai buyers in places like Bangkok, where local demand is strong and foreign money could price people out. They also see it as keeping Thai buildings majority Thai-owned. And a bigger quota might push prices up in the most popular buildings, at least in the short term.

*I'm not a lawyer or financial adviser. This is my opinion from running property agencies in Pattaya, not legal or financial advice.*

## My take

A rule meant to keep prices down is, in Pattaya at least, pushing foreign prices up and leaving Thai units empty. It's time to look at the quota again.
