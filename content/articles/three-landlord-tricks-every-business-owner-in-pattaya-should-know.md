If you rent a commercial property in Pattaya, your landlord holds far more cards than you might think. I was reminded of this recently talking to a business broker I've known for nearly 20 years. Outside the first three years of your lease, a landlord can make your life very expensive. So before you sign anything, make sure you're dealing with someone reasonable.

## Why the three-year lease matters

Most commercial leases here run for three years. A lease longer than that has to be registered at the Land Office to be enforceable beyond three years, so in practice most are written as three years with an option to renew, and the renewal is up to the landlord.

Plenty of landlords start out fair. The rent is normal, they're getting a sensible return, and everyone's happy. The trouble starts when that three years is up, or when you want to sell.

## Trick one: charging to transfer the lease

Many contracts include a clause saying the landlord must approve any change of ownership or transfer of the lease. That sounds reasonable. But some landlords use it to demand a huge fee when you come to sell your business.

The broker told me about one landlord who recently asked for a million baht to sign the lease over. The seller had no real choice. Without the landlord's blessing, there's no lease for the new owner, and without a lease, there's no sale.

## Trick two: key money at renewal

The second trick comes at the end of the first three years. Some landlords suddenly introduce key money, a lump sum you have to pay just to renew for another three years.

Because the renewal isn't guaranteed, they can more or less name their price. You've spent three years building the business, and walking away means losing everything you've put in.

## Trick three: big rent rises at renewal

Some rise in rent at renewal is fair. But some landlords push it up well beyond inflation, knowing you've invested too much to leave. Again, outside that first three years, there's very little you can do except negotiate or walk away.

## How to protect yourself

- **Choose your landlord as carefully as your location.** A reasonable landlord is worth more than a slightly better spot.
- **Trust your gut.** If anything about the landlord feels off, back out before you sign.
- **Get renewal terms in writing.** Try to agree the renewal rent, or a cap on increases, in the original contract.
- **Check the transfer clause.** See what it allows the landlord to charge, and try to limit it.
- **Have a lawyer read the lease** before you commit.

I'm not a lawyer, and this isn't legal advice. But in Pattaya, once those three years are up, the landlord holds all the cards. Pick one you can trust.
