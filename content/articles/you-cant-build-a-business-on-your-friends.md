One of the most common traps I see people fall into is thinking their friends will keep their business going. They won't. Friends will come once or twice to support you, but they'll only keep coming back if what you offer is as good as, or better than, the alternatives.

## The bar that was going to be full of friends

A friend of mine was about to open a bar. He knew a lot of people in Pattaya, and I remember him telling me he was confident it would be a success because of that.

It wasn't. At the start his friends came in to support him. But over time, the service and the food weren't as good as other places they could go, and they slowly stopped coming. They'd still pop in now and then to show their support, but nowhere near as often as he'd expected.

## The shoe on the other foot

I'm guilty of the same thing, and I think that's the important part.

I really like a couple of the guys who own one of the padel facilities here in Pattaya. But I don't play there. I play at another one, because I prefer its facilities.

It's nothing personal. I haven't stopped liking them. It's just that when it comes down to it, I go where I think is better. And I think that's true of most people. Eventually, our own needs win out over the wish to support a friend.

## It doesn't matter how big your network is

This goes beyond friends. Even if you're an influencer with tens of thousands of followers, if your product or service isn't up to scratch and someone else's is better, people will go elsewhere.

## I've taken it personally too

To be honest, I've been on the other side of this as well. A good friend of mine used to come down from Bangkok at weekends, and he'd often choose other people's restaurants over mine.

I took it personally. I know I shouldn't have, but it's hard not to. Business is tough enough, and when your friends go elsewhere, it feels like they're making it tougher. Without saying a word, they might be telling you something needs to improve. Or they might not be thinking about it at all.

## Convenience wins

Often it's not even about quality. Another good friend of mine runs an expat shop. I don't use it, simply because it isn't on my normal route and going there means going out of my way.

You could argue that he's my friend, so I should make the effort. And at first I did. But we're lazy creatures, and we take the path of least resistance. If that means another expat shop that's on my way, that's eventually where I end up. It's just how people are.

## My take

You can't rely on friends. If your product or service isn't up to scratch, it doesn't matter how many people you know.
