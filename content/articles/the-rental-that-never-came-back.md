In the early days of Pattaya Rent a Car, we lost two cars and a motorbike to someone I knew. More than 10 years later, I've never seen her or the vehicles again. It was the first time anything like that had happened to us, and it stung. ## How it started

Car rental companies here often work together. If we don't have a car available, we ask another company, and they do the same with us. That still happens today.

This woman ran her own small rental business. I knew her because she was the girlfriend of a friend of mine. She asked to do some business together, and for months it worked well. We'd rent her a car, she'd rent it on to her customer, and everybody won.

## Two cars and a motorbike

One low season, she asked for a car on a monthly contract. She'd always paid on time, so we said yes. Then she said that car was out and she needed another, so we gave her a second car, and later a motorbike too.

For a few months she kept paying. Then the payments started arriving later and later. We chased her to make sure she was chasing her own customers. Then the excuses started: her customers weren't paying her.

So we asked for the vehicles back. Then came radio silence. By then she'd also split up with my friend, so even that link wasn't much help. She became harder and harder to reach, then went completely off grid. We never recovered the two cars or the motorbike.

## Why it stung

Part of it was the money. Part of it was that it was the first time it had happened. And I have a business partner at Pattaya Rent a Car, so I had to explain it. His natural question was: I understand losing one car, but why two?

I took it personally, because I knew her through a friend. I spent a lot of time riding around the city on my motorbike looking for her and the cars, without any luck.

## The police and the insurance

We did go to the police. But in my experience, they don't show much interest in recovering rental cars. Maybe they've learned that once a car's gone, plates get changed, chassis numbers get filed off, and cars disappear over the border.

Our insurer, Viriyah, has its own recovery team, and in a different case they did get one of our cars back. They take it more seriously than the police. But they couldn't find these.

And insurance didn't pay out. We'd handed over the keys under a rental contract, so in the insurer's eyes it wasn't theft. It was a broken contract, and in theory the vehicles could still come back one day.

## What I think really happened

I genuinely don't believe she took the cars intending to steal them. I later heard she'd run into serious money trouble, and I think someone was leaning on her. She probably felt trapped, and this was her way out.

## What changed

Honestly, not much. You never really know who's going to do this, especially when circumstances change partway through a rental that can last months. The main difference is that we're more aware it can happen. Before that, we had no idea what it looked or felt like.

If you run a rental business, trust is part of the job. But remember that people's situations can change, and the warning signs are usually late payments and excuses.
