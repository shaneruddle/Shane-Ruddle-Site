In my opinion, Pattaya is very, very safe, as long as you keep your head down and don't go looking for trouble. The few times I've been in trouble myself, I wasn't looking for it. ## A cigarette butt and a machete

I was riding my motorbike through Pattaya one day, stopped at traffic lights behind a car with its windows down. The driver flicked his cigarette butt out into a hedge.

When the lights changed and I went past him, I shouted in English, asking why he'd done that, and pointed to make sure he knew what I meant. He then tried to run me off the road. He was in a car and I was on a bike.

Luckily I could accelerate away. There was a U-turn coming up, so I braked hard into it and came back on the other side of the road. As I passed him going the other way, he was hanging out of the window, showing me a machete. He wasn't waving it around. He was just letting me see it. That was the end of it, but it made the point.

## A horn, a fist and a punch

A friend of mine was driving back from Bangkok when a bus cut him up. As he went past, he beeped a few times and shook his fist at the driver.

A bit further on, they stopped at a set of lights. The bus pulled up right beside him, and the driver got out, despite having a busload of tourists on board, and started shouting. My friend speaks good Thai and didn't back down. He got punched in the face, fell to the ground, and was so dazed he couldn't drive. He had to call a friend to come and help him.

## Poke the bear, expect an overreaction

That's the lesson. Thailand is very safe, but if you poke the bear, expect an overreaction, out of all proportion to whatever started it. That isn't unique to any one group of people, and it can happen anywhere. But I've seen it enough times here to take it seriously.

A lot of it, I believe, comes down to face. In Thai culture, losing face is a big deal, and being called out in public, even over something small, can feel like a real humiliation. That's where I think the overreaction comes from. Shouting at someone or gesturing at them in front of others isn't a minor thing here.

There's another side to it too. In my experience, consequences don't always follow here the way they would in the West, so some people may feel they can get away with more. Put those together, and a beep of the horn can turn into something much worse.

## Learn to walk away

If you just walk away from situations and take the high road, you'll almost never have any trouble here.

That takes practice, especially when you're not the one in the wrong. Someone cuts you up, flicks litter, or pushes in, and every instinct says to call it out. Don't. Let it go, and get on with your day.
