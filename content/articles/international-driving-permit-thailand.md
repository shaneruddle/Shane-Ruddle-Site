Yes. If you're visiting Thailand and plan to drive, bring an International Driving Permit (IDP) along with your licence from home. You can usually rent a car without one, but at a police checkpoint you'll often be fined ฿500 if you don't have an IDP or a Thai licence. I've run Pattaya Rent a Car since 2010, and this is the question I'm asked more than any other before people fly.

Here's what you actually need, what gets checked, and the one mistake that can leave you uninsured.

## What is an International Driving Permit?

An IDP is a small booklet that translates your licence into several languages in a standard format. It isn't a licence on its own. It only works alongside your licence from home, so you carry both, plus your passport.

Thailand recognises permits issued under the two international road traffic conventions, the 1949 Geneva convention and the 1968 Vienna convention. You don't need to understand the difference. Just tell whoever issues it that you're going to Thailand, and they'll give you the right one.

## Can you drive in Thailand on your licence from home?

For a holiday, legally, a licence from a country that signed those conventions is recognised, and that includes the UK, the USA and Australia. We rent to people on their licence from home every day, and our insurance covers them.

The catch is the police. If you're stopped and only have your licence from home, you'll often be fined ฿500 for not having an international or Thai licence. Whether some officers don't know the rules, or it's just an easy fine, I can't say. These days you can't pay it on the spot either: you have to go to the police station. An IDP costs a few pounds or dollars and saves the argument, so my advice is simple: get one before you come.

## Where do you get an IDP?

You get it in your home country, before you travel. You can't get one once you're in Thailand.

- **UK:** over the counter at participating PayPoint shops. Thailand uses the 1949 permit for UK drivers.
- **USA:** from AAA, with a passport photo and a short form.
- **Australia:** from your state motoring club.
- **Europe and elsewhere:** usually your national motoring club or licensing office.

Be wary of websites selling "international driving licences" online. Thailand recognises the official convention permits from your own country, not a laminated card from a website.

## Do you need an IDP for a motorbike or scooter?

You need more than an IDP. An IDP only covers the vehicles your licence from home covers. If your licence doesn't include the motorbike category, neither does your IDP, and you're riding unlicensed.

That matters most when something goes wrong. Most of the accidents I hear about involve bikes, and travel insurers are very unlikely to pay for an accident when you weren't licensed to ride. A broken leg and a hospital bill in Thailand is an expensive way to find that out.

So if your licence is car only, rent a car, or use Grab and the [baht buses](/articles/pattayas-baht-buses). If you do ride, wear a helmet every time. I've written more about this in [scooter or car in Pattaya](/articles/scooter-or-car-in-pattaya).

## When should you switch to a Thai licence?

If you live here and you're here to stay, get a Thai driving licence. People often say after about six months, but the unwritten rule is simpler: once Pattaya is home, do it.

With your licence from home, there's no driving test. You watch a video on Thai traffic laws and do a few quick physical tests, and an agent can hold your hand through it for around ฿2,500 to ฿3,000. Once you have it, a checkpoint is nothing to worry about, and it works as ID at the bank and in hotels so you don't have to carry your passport. I've written the whole process up in [getting a Thai driving licence in Pattaya](/articles/thai-driving-licence).

## What to carry when you drive

This is what I tell every customer who picks up a car from us in Pratumnak:

1. Your licence from home, the physical card.
2. Your International Driving Permit, or your Thai licence if you live here.
3. Your passport, or a copy, if you don't have a Thai licence.
4. The rental agreement and the rental company's phone number.

Keep them together in the glovebox or a bag that goes everywhere with you. If you have an accident, call an ambulance or the police first if it's an emergency; otherwise phone the rental company before you do anything else. I've explained why in [driving in Pattaya](/articles/driving-in-pattaya).

## The short version

Bring an IDP. It costs very little, it takes ten minutes to get at home, and it turns a police checkpoint into a two-minute stop instead of a trip to the police station. Make sure your licence covers what you plan to ride. And if you're moving here, get a Thai licence and stop worrying about it.
