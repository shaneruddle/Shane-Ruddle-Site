Everyone kept telling me Vietnam was the new place to go, so I've been six times: Phu Quoc, Da Nang, Hanoi, Ha Long Bay and Nha Trang. I've always enjoyed it. But for me, it doesn't compare with Thailand. Thailand has everything.

## Why Thailand has everything

Thailand has the infrastructure, and it's still fairly cheap compared with other places that have the same. It has real history and culture, the monarchy, the temples. It has the food, the weather and the people. Somewhere like Dubai is great, but it's man-made. Thailand isn't. More in [living in Pattaya](/articles/living-in-pattaya).

## Nha Trang, "Vietnam's Pattaya"

This year I went to Nha Trang, which people call Vietnam's answer to Pattaya. When I got there, I wondered if I'd come to the wrong place. It's far less developed. Maybe people are comparing it to Pattaya 20 years ago, and for some that's the attraction.

For me, it wasn't as clean as Thailand, which is saying something, and there didn't seem to be much to do. It is cheaper, and the hotels are very well priced. But the vibe just isn't the same. More in [was Pattaya better in the good old days?](/articles/was-pattaya-better-in-the-good-old-days)

## Hospitality

The people I met in Vietnam were friendly and helpful. But in my experience, Thai hospitality is on another level. Thais are more welcoming and more service-minded, and you feel it everywhere you go.

## Who should go

I'd recommend Vietnam to anyone for a holiday. It's inexpensive and a nice country to visit. But for me it's a visit, not somewhere to live. The infrastructure isn't there yet, and I don't see the same opportunities.

It depends on your stage of life. If you're young, you could get in early and see the benefits as it develops over the next 20 years. I'm 45, so I'd be 65 by then. And Thailand already has the things that matter if you live here, like international schools and international hospitals.
