People often ask what my days look like with seven businesses. The honest answer is that no two are the same, but here's roughly how they go.

## 6am: up early

I'm usually up at 6am. That's not a flex. I'm in bed by 9pm and asleep by half past. Sadly, the first thing I do is check my phone for messages and emails. I've tried to stop, but it's a hard habit to break.

## The morning

Then it's a coffee and, most days, a walk along the beach. If I've slept a bit later, I skip the walk and get ready to take my kids to school.

## Breakfast at Hemingways

Most mornings I work from Hemingways Jomtien. It's quiet in the mornings and it's where I like to have breakfast. I usually catch up with Robert, the manager, but we only talk business about 5% of the time. He's very capable and I trust him to run it, so we talk about politics, technology, whatever comes up.

## Messages, not calls

I keep on top of the other businesses mainly by message. I don't like phone calls, and I've told my managers and everyone I deal with daily: don't call me unless it's urgent. More in [how I run seven businesses](/articles/how-i-run-seven-businesses).

## What I actually work on

At the moment most of my time goes on AI systems. I look at the problems each business has and see whether AI can solve them, especially streamlining admin. I also spend a lot of time on marketing. More in [how I use AI in my businesses](/articles/how-i-use-ai-in-my-businesses).

## No fixed finishing time

My day isn't structured. I try not to get pulled into things, but sometimes I'm the best person, or the only person, to solve a problem. Some days I work right up until bed. Other days I stop at two or three in the afternoon and go and play padel or [go for a run](/articles/staying-fit-in-pattaya).

## The best part

Every day is different, and I meet a lot of new and often interesting people. I love the flexibility: if my kids have a football match, I can just go. I don't have to ask anyone's permission.

I also like that the work I do now is non-critical. The businesses run without me day to day. What I add is the cherry on top: not strictly needed, but it makes things better.
