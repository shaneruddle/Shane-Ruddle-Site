The most useful business advice I've had recently didn't come from someone who thinks like me. It came from a friend who sees businesses through completely different eyes. The lesson: successful people are often successful for very different reasons from you, and that's exactly why it's worth listening to them.

## Asking for help with Lakeside

Hemingways Lakeside has been a tough one. A friend of mine, a very successful local property developer I've known for close to 20 years, lives in that area. When I bumped into him at Hemingways Jomtien, he said something along the lines of, "If you ever need any help, let me know."

He's a busy man, but I took him up on it. I messaged him and asked if he'd meet me at Lakeside and tell me what he would do with it.

## "Do you know anything about feng shui?"

We covered a lot of ground, but the way he opened the conversation has stuck with me. He asked, "Do you know anything about feng shui?"

I looked at him a bit doubtfully. He followed up: there's something in it. What he meant was that design, and the way a place is laid out, matters.

I'd never really thought about that. I'm not a design person. I'm a systems person. It had never occurred to me that Lakeside might not be working partly because of how it's laid out.

## What I changed

After that conversation, I opened up the front of Lakeside.

To be honest, it hasn't seemed to make much difference yet. But that isn't really the point.

## Different people, different strengths

What I took away is that people come at business from completely different angles, even when you assume you have similar traits.

Take a restaurant. One owner might focus on building a team and relationships with customers. Another doesn't care much about any of that and puts everything into marketing. Another says, "I know food, so we'll have the best food." All of them can be successful, for completely different reasons.

I naturally look at systems. He naturally looks at space and design. Neither is wrong. But I'd never have seen what he saw.

## My take

When you're stuck, ask someone successful who thinks differently from you, not someone who thinks the same. Even if their idea doesn't fix the problem, it will show you something you'd never have noticed on your own.
