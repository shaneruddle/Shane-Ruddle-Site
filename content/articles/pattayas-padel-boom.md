A year ago, Pattaya had really only one place to play padel. Now there are five venues and well over 20 courts. I've watched the whole boom from the court.

## Why I started playing

I took up padel because I wanted to meet new people. I've always met people through sport: football, golf, even running to some degree.

I looked at tennis, badminton, squash, and golf again, and none of them really appealed. I knew padel was a very sociable sport, so I gave it a try. Straight away, I realised how addictive it is. Unlike golf, and maybe tennis, you can pick it up quickly, which I think is a big part of the attraction. I started playing regularly and, sure enough, met some new people.

## You could see the cogs turning

When I started, there was just one venue with two courts. It wasn't hard to get a game, but it was busy, and you could tell people loved it.

You could also see the business people on court with the cogs turning: hold on, there's something here. Even people who weren't particularly business-minded were talking about opening more facilities. I knew it was only a matter of time.

Sure enough, another venue opened at Mabprachan Lake, then one in Jomtien, then one in Naklua, and recently another in Jomtien. That's five in about a year, and one of them alone has six courts.

## Have we already peaked?

I think we probably have. I don't think there's room for many more.

That won't stop people trying. I understand the thinking: there are five venues, they all have customers, so if I open one and do it better, those customers will come to me. It might work out that way. But as I've said before, once people form habits, it's much harder than you'd think to pull them away from places they already go.

## My take

Padel is a brilliant way to get fit and meet people, and Pattaya now has plenty of places to play. If you're thinking of opening another venue, though, be realistic about how hard it is to win players from the courts they already love.
