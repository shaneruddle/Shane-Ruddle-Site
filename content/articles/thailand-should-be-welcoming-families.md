Thailand's population is shrinking and ageing, and encouraging people to have more babies isn't working, here or anywhere else. Thailand already courts retirees, digital nomads and migrant workers. I think it's missing a trick: it should be trying to attract families who want to stay for 30 or 40 years, and giving them a clear path to do it.

## The problem

Thailand now has fewer births than deaths, and its birth rate is among the lowest in the world. Like a lot of countries, it's tried to encourage people to have more children. Like a lot of countries, it's struggling to make that work.

That leaves migration as the other option. The question is who to attract.

## Who Thailand is targeting now

The current approach focuses on retirees, digital nomads, wealthy long-stay visitors and migrant workers from neighbouring countries. They all bring something. But retirees don't add to the working population, and many nomads move on after a year or two. (More on the options in [Thai visas in Pattaya](/articles/thai-visas-in-pattaya).)

## Why families

Families are different. Parents in their 30s with young children are here for the long haul. Their children grow up here, go to school here, make friends and form relationships here. By the time they're adults, they're fully integrated. That's exactly the kind of young, settled, tax-paying population an ageing country needs.

## What stops families settling

The biggest barrier is work. A family of four needs an income, and finding work here as a foreigner has always been hard. The only route that feels anything like secure is running your own business, and even that isn't very secure. Beyond that, it's mostly teaching.

Then there's belonging. I've lived here for over 20 years, and I still feel like a foreigner. I don't really feel like I belong. For a family deciding where to spend the next 30 years, that matters.

## The children

This is where it hits home. My own children were born here. But when they reach working age, many jobs will still be closed to them, because those jobs are reserved for Thais.

Children born and raised in Thailand by long-term foreign residents should have a clear path to permanent residency, and ideally the option of citizenship. And they should be free to work in the country they grew up in.

## What I'd do

- **Create a clear long-term path for families**, from a family visa through to permanent residency, with clear rules and timescales.
- **Make it easier for parents to work legally**, not just through running a business.
- **Give children raised here a route to residency or citizenship**, and the right to work.
- **Attract people who'll put down roots and contribute**, rather than people passing through.

## The other side

There are real arguments against. Many Thais worry about jobs, rising property prices and protecting Thai identity, and the reserved occupations exist to protect local workers. Any country has the right to decide who becomes a citizen, and opening up citizenship is a big step that most countries take slowly. Some would also argue that Thailand should focus on making it easier for Thai families to have children before looking abroad.

## My take

None of the options is ideal. But if births keep falling, Thailand will need more young, settled people from somewhere. Families who want to make a life here, and whose children already think of it as home, are the obvious place to start.
