One of my landlords, who's also a friend, told me recently that at the third-year renewal he's going to deal with his tenant directly. He was just letting me know. I understand the temptation, but I think most landlords who do this don't see what they're giving up. And the tenant usually loses out most, without realising it.

## Why landlords do it

I get it. If your rent is high, paying out a month's commission every year hurts. After the first year, when the early teething problems are sorted, a lot of owners stop seeing what the agency does. The introduction is done, the tenant is in, so why keep paying?

When a landlord goes direct, the tenant rarely gets a break on the rent. It's just the landlord keeping the commission. That's their choice to make, but it has consequences for both sides.

## What the agency actually does

We deal with owner and tenant problems every single day. Most landlords never hear about them, because we sort them out before they get that far.

Here's one from this week. A condo building is switching its internet from copper to fibre and charging every owner for it. The tenant didn't want to pay because it was the building's decision. The owner didn't want to pay because he felt he only needed it because of the tenant. They sensibly agreed to split it 50-50.

Then the tenant paid about 35% instead of half. The owner got annoyed, words were exchanged, and now the owner is refusing to pay even his half. So the tenant faces having no internet, over a small bill that turned into a row.

We'll sort it out. But it's a perfect example of what happens without someone in the middle. Small things escalate fast when it's just two people who are both annoyed.

## Tenants, think about your deposit

This is the part nobody talks about.

When we manage a rental, we prefer to hold the deposit ourselves. It's not about control. A neutral third party holding the money makes disputes much easier to settle. If the landlord holds it, he naturally has the upper hand.

Some landlords insist on holding it, and we can't refuse. But once the agency is out of the picture, the tenant has nowhere to go. They often don't think about it until the end of the lease, when they ask for their deposit back and the landlord doesn't want to return it. That's when they realise there's no one to help. (I wrote about a case like that in [renting direct in Pattaya](/articles/renting-direct-in-pattaya).)

So if your landlord calls to say he's dealing with you directly from now on, find out who has your deposit and what contract you're on. And make sure you understand that the agency is no longer involved in anything that happens. That's why I always remind landlords to tell their tenants clearly.

## A better middle ground

If a landlord has been with us a few years and doesn't want to pay a full month on renewal, talk to us. We look at the tenant, the age of the building and how much maintenance there's been. If it's been a fairly easy year, we'll often meet halfway and charge half a month.

For that, the landlord keeps our contract and a middleman to deal with problems, plus some legal protection. The tenant keeps protection over their deposit. I think that's the sensible option for everyone.

The trouble is, a lot of owners and tenants don't think about any of this until the problems start. By then, it's usually too late.
