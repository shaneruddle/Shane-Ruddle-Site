Pattaya's baht buses are a good way to get around, but only because there's no real alternative. In my opinion, they're the worst possible form of public transport, and Pattaya deserves better. More in [getting around Pattaya](/articles/getting-around-pattaya).

## Why I'd replace them

- **They pollute.** Most of them are old diesel pickup trucks.
- **They're inefficient.** There are too many of them, and most of the time they drive around with hardly anyone in the back.
- **They're dangerous.** For passengers, for other drivers, and for anyone on a scooter behind one.

## How they're dangerous

When a driver takes a private hire, which they're not supposed to do but often do, they tend to drive very fast to finish the job and get back on their route.

In the back, you're sat side-on to the traffic with no seat belt. People hang off the back step. Over the years there have been accidents where passengers have fallen off the back, or tried to get off just as the driver pulled away.

And on the road, they brake suddenly whenever they spot a possible fare. If you drive in Pattaya, you learn to leave a big gap behind them. More in [driving in Pattaya](/articles/driving-in-pattaya).

## Almost too convenient

To be fair, they're very cheap, usually ฿15 a ride, or ฿20 for longer trips, and very convenient. In the city, you rarely wait more than a couple of minutes.

But I think that's almost too quick. I've travelled enough to know that with public transport, you expect to walk to a stop and wait a little. You don't need a ride within a minute, wherever you're standing, even at a junction.

I'd rather see far fewer of them, running fuller, with proper stops where people gather, and even queue, to get on. Instead, we have this haphazard flagging down anywhere. For me, it's chaos, and it's unnecessary.

## What I'd like instead

Almost anything other cities do would be better: trams, big electric buses, a hop-on hop-off network. Something cleaner, safer and more organised.

## The other side

The baht buses do a lot of things well. They're about the cheapest way to get around any tourist city I know, and for many locals and visitors on a budget they're essential. Thousands of drivers make their living from them, and any change would have to look after those families.

It's also not the drivers' fault. They work within the system they've been given. Replacing it would take a lot of planning and money from the city, and plenty of people would argue the current system works well enough. I just think Pattaya, with its new beach road and walkways, deserves public transport to match.
