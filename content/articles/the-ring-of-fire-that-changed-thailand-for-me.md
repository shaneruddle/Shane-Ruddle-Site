On my first trip to Thailand, I set myself on fire jumping through a ring of fire on a beach. It hurt, I still have the scars, and it was one of the best things that ever happened to me. Without it, I'm not sure I'd have come back.

I was about 21 or 22, backpacking with two friends. I've written about that trip in [how an internet café search brought me to Pattaya](/articles/how-an-internet-cafe-search-brought-me-to-pattaya). This is the part I left out.

## The ring of fire

We were on Koh Phangan, at a beach bar called the Drop In Bar, which I believe is still open today. Every evening they brought out a ring of fire. First the staff would jump through it, and they even had a motorbike ride through a bigger one. Then it was the customers' turn.

Two members of staff held the ring up on wires, and everyone sat out on the sand drinking, with music playing. People ran up and jumped through, and the crowd cheered every time.

So I had a go. I jumped through, no problem. Then they raised it, and raised it again, until I was the only one who could still get high enough. By then they were standing on stools, and there were a hundred-odd people cheering me on.

Eventually it got too high. Someone got in the way of my run-up, I didn't get enough lift, and I grabbed the bottom of the ring to try to pull myself through. The whole thing came down on top of me, and I caught fire.

## Strangers to the rescue

I was badly burned. My hands were red raw, and I still have scars on my arm, my back and my foot. At the time I didn't even realise I'd been hurt anywhere else. The skin on your hands is far more sensitive, so that's where all the pain was.

I wasn't with my friends that night. It was complete strangers who saw what happened and got me to the local hospital. I was in so much pain they had to give me an injection to calm me down.

## Alone on Koh Samui

There was no international hospital on Koh Phangan, so the next day I was transferred to Koh Samui.

I didn't want to ruin my friends' holiday. It was my mistake, not theirs, and one of them had already booked to go diving on [Koh Tao](/articles/koh-tao-from-pattaya). So we split up: one went diving, one stayed on Koh Phangan, and I was on Koh Samui on my own, bandaged up.

## How it changed Thailand for me

This is the part that surprised me. Suddenly, people wanted to talk to me.

Partly it was the bandages: everyone wanted to know what had happened. But mostly, I think, it was because I was on my own. Before, I'd been walking into restaurants with two women, and people assumed one of them was my girlfriend. Now I was a young man on his own, and I got a lot of attention from Thai women, more than I'd ever had in the UK.

It might make me sound a bit insecure, but I enjoyed it. I liked being looked after, and I liked that people were interested in me. I left Thailand having seen a completely different side of it from the one I'd had as a backpacker, and that was one of the reasons I came back.

## Good comes out of bad

I definitely wouldn't jump through it again. But the lesson has stayed with me, and it's proved true many times since.

When you're in the middle of something bad, whether it's lying in a hospital bed in pain in a foreign country or going through a tough time at home, it can feel like everything's against you. But again and again in my life, some really good things have come out of bad experiences.

That burn on a beach in Koh Phangan is a big part of why I've now lived in Thailand for over 20 years.
