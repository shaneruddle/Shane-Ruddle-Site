I'm from England, and I've lived in Thailand for over 20 years. Every so often it strikes me how one-sided the rules are. A Thai can set up a company in England, own all of it, and even buy land. As an English person in Thailand, I can't own my own business outright without jumping through hoops.

## What a Thai can do in England

A Thai national can generally own 100% of a UK company. There's no rule saying a British partner has to hold the majority. They can also buy property in England, freehold included, although non-UK residents pay a bit of extra stamp duty on homes.

The hard part is living there. To move to England and run the business yourself, you need the right visa, and those aren't easy to get. The main route for founders, the Innovator Founder visa, needs your business idea to be endorsed by an approved body as new, innovative and able to grow.

## What an English person can do in Thailand

Here, the Foreign Business Act stops foreigners holding the majority in most service businesses, including restaurants, property agencies and car rental. Foreigners can't own land either. There are routes around some of this, like a Foreign Business Licence or BOI promotion, but they're limited and they aren't automatic.

## The Americans got a better deal

Americans are in a different position. Under the US–Thailand Treaty of Amity, they can own most Thai businesses outright, with exceptions such as land, transport, communications and banking.

So part of me thinks the UK should be in the same camp. If Thais can invest in our country and own land, why can't we have some kind of reciprocal arrangement here?

## But they're very different countries

At the same time, I understand why it isn't that simple. The UK and Thailand are completely different countries.

The UK welcomes foreign investment and immigration for all sorts of reasons. Thailand is a lot more protectionist. And Thailand is still a developing economy, while the UK is a long-established one. It's understandable that Thailand wants to protect its own businesses.

As I wrote in [my piece on the OECD and foreign ownership](/articles/thailand-oecd-and-foreign-ownership), I don't actually want a free-for-all. But a fairer, reciprocal deal for countries that already open their doors to Thais seems reasonable to me.

## The other side

Many would point out that the comparison isn't really like for like. A Thai who owns a UK company still can't live or work there without a visa, which is its own big hurdle. And countries rarely set their rules on a tit-for-tat basis. Thailand's restrictions reflect its own economic priorities, not a judgement on any one country.

*I'm not a lawyer. This is my opinion as a business owner, not legal advice.*

## My take

I don't expect Thailand to throw its doors wide open. But if Americans can have a special arrangement, it's fair to ask why the British can't. A bit of reciprocity would go a long way.
