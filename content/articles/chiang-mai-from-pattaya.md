I've been to Chiang Mai five or six times. The first was before I even lived here, backpacking around Thailand with a couple of friends between my first and second years at university. It's the perfect contrast to Pattaya, and the natural end of a road trip north through [Ayutthaya](/articles/ayutthaya-from-pattaya) and [Sukhothai](/articles/sukhothai-from-pattaya).

## How it differs from Pattaya

Chiang Mai is inland, so there are no beaches. What you get instead is a chilled-out atmosphere. It feels much more Thai than Pattaya, where there are so many foreigners, though it has plenty of digital nomads and backpackers too, a younger crowd from all over the world. If there's a bar scene like Pattaya's, I've never seen it.

It's also low-rise, with hardly any tall buildings, and very spread out. More in [Pattaya for digital nomads](/articles/pattaya-for-digital-nomads).

## Getting there and around

I've always driven. It's a long way, well over 800 km, so it's best done as a road trip with stops along the way. You can fly instead, and there are trains and buses from Bangkok, but Chiang Mai is a big, spread-out city, so having a car makes it much easier, and the traffic isn't bad. If you need a car, [Pattaya Rent a Car](https://pattayarentacar.com) can help, and before you go, read [driving in Pattaya](/articles/driving-in-pattaya) and [renting a car in Pattaya](/articles/renting-a-car-in-pattaya).

## What I did

On my last trip we trekked up Doi Suthep, the mountain that overlooks the city. We went for New Year to escape the chaos of Pattaya over that period. Honestly, the fireworks and celebrations were a bit flat, but it was still lovely to be there.

## When to go

It's noticeably cooler than Pattaya, so the cool season around New Year is a great time if you want Thailand without the heat. Avoid the burning season, usually around February to April, when smoke from crop burning can make the air quality very poor. More in [the best time to visit Pattaya](/articles/best-time-to-visit-pattaya).

## Go the extra mile: Chiang Rai

If you've come as far as Chiang Mai, carry on to Chiang Rai, about three hours further north, and see the White Temple, Wat Rong Khun. If you're in that part of the country, it's a must.
