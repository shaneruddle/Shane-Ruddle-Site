Staff stealing is wrong, and it happens far more often in business here than most owners like to think. But I believe part of the fault lies with the owner. If you make stealing easy, you have to expect some of it. The only real way to stop it is to cut down the opportunity.

## The bar owner who'd never done a stock take

A good friend of mine sold his beer bar to someone with less experience. A few months later, the new owner was complaining that he wasn't making much money. My friend got involved to help, and quickly found the staff were stealing from him.

His first question was: when did you last do a stock take? The answer was never.

If you never count your stock, you have to expect some of it to walk. That doesn't make it right, but it does make it predictable.

## What I do to limit it

In my businesses we use a POS system that logs every transaction. Each cashier has their own login, so nobody can do anything under someone else's name, and everything is tracked.

There are still ways round it. If someone really wants to steal, they'll find a way. The aim is to make it as hard as possible.

## "I was only borrowing it"

One thing I've come across more than once: staff who take money and genuinely seem to think they've done nothing wrong, because they meant to pay it back. When they're caught, the reaction is: what's the big deal? I was only borrowing it.

Whether they really meant to repay it, you never know. Either way, my answer is the same. Borrowing without telling anyone is stealing.

## What I do when I catch someone

It depends on the circumstances. Over the years I've done all three: sacked people, had them pay the money back, and, in one recent case, involved the police.

The last person I sacked asked me to reconsider. In their eyes, they'd always intended to pay it back. We could have put things in place so they weren't in a position to do it again. But once the trust is broken, you may as well move them on.

## Follow up the references

This is the mistake I'd most like other owners to avoid.

We took on someone who had been accused of stealing at another business. She told us she was the victim: three people had been involved, the other two were responsible, and she'd been let go unfairly. My manager believed her, and so did I.

Six months later, we found she was stealing from us. It was actually my manager's suggestion to involve the police, not mine. Given her history, I backed it, mainly in the hope that it would stop her doing it again somewhere else.

If someone is going to handle money, ask around and follow up their references properly. A phone call to a previous employer can save you a lot.

## Checks and balances

The other thing that works is having trusted staff keep an eye on new ones. In the last two cases where we caught someone stealing, it wasn't me who found it. I'm not there day to day. It was their colleagues and my managers who came to me and said: we've caught someone stealing, what do you want to do?

If you've built a team that cares about the business, they'll often spot it long before you do.

## My advice

- **Do regular stock takes.** If you never count, you'll never know.
- **Use a POS system with individual logins**, so every transaction is tied to a person.
- **Check references** for anyone handling money, and ask around.
- **Have trusted staff oversee new ones**, because your team will often catch it before you do.
- **Act quickly** when you find it. Once trust is gone, it rarely comes back.

Stealing is never acceptable. But as an owner, it's your job to make it difficult.
