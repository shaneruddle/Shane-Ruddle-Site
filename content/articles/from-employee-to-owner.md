One of my staff recently told me quietly that she respects what I've built and wants to do the same one day. It was a lovely thing to hear. But the honest truth is that being an owner isn't for everyone, and it never happens overnight.

## The conversation

She'd noticed that I run several businesses, and she wants to be like that.

It wasn't the right moment to give her proper advice, so I kept it vague: keep working hard, and you never know. This is the advice I'd give her, and anyone else, given the chance.

## Not everyone is cut out for it

The longer I've done this, the more I've realised you have to be a certain type of person to own businesses, especially several. You have to learn to delegate. You have to keep going when things go wrong. Persistence beats resistance.

From the outside, being an owner can look easier than being an employee, and on a busy night in the restaurant, maybe it is. But there are a lot of downsides people don't see. (See [no one cares about your business like you do](/articles/no-one-cares-about-your-business-like-you-do) and [when being the owner doesn't feel worth it](/articles/when-being-the-owner-doesnt-feel-worth-it).)

## It doesn't happen overnight

You can't go from employee to owning seven businesses. I certainly didn't.

I went from employee to employee with a business on the side, the Pattaya Job Centre. Then came the Pattaya Guide. Then I jumped to three businesses. Over the years I added some, sold some, and some failed.

Now I have seven, and not every one of them is thriving. That's been a 20-year evolution. (The full story is in [from bar manager to seven businesses](/articles/bar-manager-to-7-businesses), and [what selling 12 businesses in Pattaya taught me](/articles/what-selling-12-businesses-in-pattaya-taught-me).)

## My advice

If you're an employee who wants to follow that path, be patient, and take it one step at a time. Start small, keep your job while you learn, and build from there.
