When people think of romance scams in Thailand, they usually picture the classic story: a sick buffalo, a sudden family emergency, a request for money. But romance scams come in many forms. One of them cost my car rental business a truck, and the woman who rented it had no idea she was part of a scam.

## Why we'd tightened our checks

At Pattaya Rent a Car, we'd had cars stolen before. Over time, we noticed patterns in who was behind it. Stolen cars in Thailand are often driven across a border, which is far easier for some people than others. So we added extra checks for renters who fitted the patterns we'd seen.

The scammer in this case seems to have known exactly that.

## How the scam worked

A woman came in and rented a truck from us. Nothing about her raised any concern, because nothing about her was suspicious. She genuinely believed she was renting a car for a holiday.

What we found out later was that she'd been drawn into a romance scam. A man had won her over, and the two of them had agreed to take a trip together within Thailand. He told her they'd need a car, and sent her money to go and rent one.

The plan all along was for her to rent the vehicle, and for him to take it from her and steal it. She was the one whose name was on the contract. He kept his distance from the rental, and he walked away with the truck.

We never got that truck back. That's a story in itself: [trying to get our stolen truck back cost us even more](/articles/trying-to-get-our-stolen-truck-back).

## A victim, not a thief

The part that stays with me is that she didn't think, at any point, that she was being scammed. She was left with a broken relationship and her name on the contract for a stolen vehicle. He walked away with the truck.

## The lesson

Romance scams aren't always about someone asking you for money. Sometimes they ask you to do something that seems completely normal, like renting a car, opening an account, or signing something in your name.

So if you've met someone new, especially someone you haven't known long:

- **Be wary of anything done in your name for their benefit.** Renting a car, signing a contract, borrowing money or taking out a loan.
- **Ask why they can't do it themselves.** If the answer doesn't quite add up, trust that feeling.
- **Take your time.** Scammers rely on things moving quickly, before you've had a chance to think.

It's easy to say "don't be gullible". The truth is, these people are good at what they do, and they choose people who trust them. The best protection is a healthy bit of caution, especially early on.
