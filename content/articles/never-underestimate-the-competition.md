One of the most common mistakes I see in business is underestimating the competition. I've watched plenty of people do it, and if I'm honest, I've done it myself.

## My own mistake

When I took on Hemingways Lakeside, I underestimated the competition. It wasn't arrogance. I simply figured that if I worked harder than everyone else, I'd naturally take some of their market share.

That absolutely hasn't happened.

What I learned is that when a new competitor arrives, it can wake up the existing businesses. If someone has taken their eye off the ball, a new rival can be exactly what spurs them on, especially when it's a threat to their livelihood. The competition around Lakeside smartened up.

## I underestimated loyalty too

The bigger thing I underestimated was loyalty: how well established businesses hold on to their customers.

Some of it is friendship with the owner. Some of it is habit. A couple of the restaurants nearby have been there a very long time, and people build routines around places like that. They make friends with the other regulars, too. Breaking that habit is much harder than most people think.

## One bad experience isn't a business plan

This applies more widely. Don't go into business just because you had a bad experience somewhere and think you could do better.

Maybe you could have handled that one situation better. But there's usually a lot more going on than one isolated bad experience, and it doesn't mean you could run the whole business better than they do.

## What I'd do now

Honestly? I wouldn't do it. If I were up against well-established businesses, I'd stay out of that market. People underestimate how strong those businesses are, and how hard it is to change customers' habits.

If you're determined to try, you'd need to:

- Really differentiate yourself, so you're not just another version of what's already there.
- Know a lot about marketing, and go hard on it, perhaps with some guerrilla-style marketing.
- Be persistent and stubborn, and willing to stay the course, because it could take years to win loyal customers away.

## My take

Don't assume working harder is enough. Respect the businesses that are already there, especially their regulars. Unless you can genuinely offer something different and stick at it for years, pick a different market.
