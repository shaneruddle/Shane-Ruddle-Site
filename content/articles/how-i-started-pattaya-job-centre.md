One of the first businesses I ever started in Pattaya came out of a simple problem: I couldn't find staff.

## The sign in the window

Around 2007, I was managing the Sportsman, a restaurant on Soi 13. The owner told me we needed to find some waitresses. So I asked the obvious question: how do you normally do it?

He pointed at a sign in the window. I went outside and looked at it from the road. It just said, in Thai, that we were looking for staff and what the salary was. That was it.

I asked what else he did. Nothing, apart from asking the other staff if they knew anyone. Not many people walked down that soi, so I couldn't see how a sign would ever find us anyone. Coming from the UK, I asked: isn't there a job centre here? He said no, nothing like that.

## Struggling to find staff

I left it there, but I kept thinking about it. Then I really did struggle to find staff, and that looked like a reflection on me. It seemed as though I wasn't doing my job properly. In reality, there just wasn't any way of finding staff beyond people you already knew or referrals.

So I thought: it can't be that difficult to set up a little job centre.

## Copying the UK, in a shop window

I looked online at how job centres worked in the UK. People walk in, pick up little cards from a board, and take them over to someone to talk about the job.

I did something similar, but in the window, a bit like a real estate office: each job on a card, with the company logo and as much information as we could fit.

This was back when not everyone had the internet or a smartphone. Plenty did, but not everyone. One of the first things we noticed was people looking for maids. A maid wasn't going to go online to look for work, so how would she ever find out the job existed? A window on the street solved that.

We put the word out that anyone looking for work could come in and look at the adverts, and it grew from there.

## Who paid

We never charged job seekers. It didn't feel right, or even ethical, to charge someone who's looking for work. The job is supposed to pay them, not the other way round.

Employers, on the other hand, were struggling so much to find staff that they were more than happy to pay. So that's the model we used.

## Where it is now

I put about ฿200,000 into setting it up, and my girlfriend at the time ran it. When we split, I gave it to her, and she still runs it today. Twenty-odd years on, it's still going. I don't know how much longer it will last, but it's still there.

## My take

Some of the best businesses come from fixing a problem you're struggling with yourself. If you can't find something, chances are plenty of other people can't either.
