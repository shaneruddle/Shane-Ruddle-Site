Stories of people falling from balconies come up in Pattaya far too often. I don't know the details of individual cases, and this is only my own view, but after more than 20 years here I have a few theories about why, and some thoughts on what could help.

## Older buildings, older railings

The first is the buildings themselves. In some older condos, I don't think much thought went into balcony safety.

In one condo I lived in, the railings ran horizontally, which meant they could easily be used like a ladder. With children around, that was something we were very aware of. I've also viewed condos where the balcony barrier was far too low. High up, it felt genuinely scary. You can easily imagine someone tall losing their balance and going over.

## Drink and drugs

The second is intoxication. Drinking, and smoking cannabis in rooms, is pretty common here. When people aren't in control of themselves, accidents happen.

## People who are struggling

The third is harder to talk about. Some people come to Thailand and absolutely love it. Then, for whatever reason, it comes to an end. They may have to go home and don't want to. They try different things, and nothing works out.

I think loneliness is often part of it. Some people here are struggling far more than anyone around them realises.

## What could help

**For building and condo owners:** safety netting. You often see it on older buildings where families with children or elderly relatives live. If someone trips or falls, the net catches them. Low or climbable railings are worth looking at too.

**For anyone feeling low:** reach out to someone. I know that's easier said than done. Support here isn't always well known, but it does exist, and there are plenty of people in Pattaya who would gladly help if you ask.

## My take

Some of these tragedies come down to old buildings, some to a bad night, and some to people who were quietly struggling. Safer balconies would help with the first two. For the third, we can all keep an eye out for people around us, and anyone having a hard time shouldn't be afraid to ask for help.

*If you're struggling, you're not alone. In Thailand you can call the Samaritans of Thailand on 02 113 6789, or the Department of Mental Health's free 24-hour line on 1323. In an emergency, call 1669.*
