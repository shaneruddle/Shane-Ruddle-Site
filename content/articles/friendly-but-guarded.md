Everyone in Pattaya real estate knows each other, and we're all friendly. But ask another agency owner how business is going, and you'll rarely get a straight answer. We never really know how the other agencies are doing.

## A chat in Starbucks

Recently, in Starbucks, I spotted another real estate agency owner I recognised from Facebook, so I went over and introduced myself. I asked how business was going at the moment, and he was very vague.

So I brought up the [nominee crackdown](/articles/is-the-nominee-crackdown-really-about-the-oecd), which is the hot topic right now. Again, vague.

It reminded me of agents and owners I know far better. They're always friendly, because that's part of the job. We build relationships for a living. But they're very guarded about what they say and what they let on.

## You never know the truth

You could walk into an agency that looks busy, ask the owner, and be told it's quiet. More often it's the other way round: people say they're much busier than they are. It's natural, and maybe a bit of ego. Nobody wants to be the one admitting it's tough.

## The honest one

The exception is another agency owner I've recently started playing padel with. He's very open. He told me his agency is having a worse time now than it did during COVID. (More on that in [confidence has gone from Thailand's property market](/articles/confidence-has-gone-from-thailands-property-market).)

Honestly, that's what I'd expect to hear in this market, far more than someone telling me they're flying.

## Where I stand

I'd like to think I'm more honest than most about how the market is. But I don't go into specifics. I'm not telling anyone exactly how many deals we're doing either.

## It's just part of the game

It can feel a bit two-faced. But I don't think it hurts the industry. It's the same in most businesses: you're nice to people, you want to come across as helpful, open and transparent, and you still keep your most important cards close to your chest.

If you're a buyer or seller, it's worth remembering. When an agent tells you the market's booming, or that they're rushed off their feet, take it with a pinch of salt. Ask for evidence: recent sales, actual prices achieved, how long properties have taken to sell.
