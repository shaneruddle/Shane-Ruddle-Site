Back in the West, traffic wardens aren't exactly loved. I used to think they were unnecessary, just going around fining people more or less indiscriminately. Funnily enough, after more than 20 years in Thailand, I've found myself thinking more than once that Pattaya could really do with some.

## A free-for-all

When nobody enforces parking, everyone is left to their own devices. I'd never park somewhere dangerous, but that doesn't mean everyone else on the road thinks the same way.

Take a busy soi near the restaurants I know well. You could drive down it at any time of day and see people parked on the junction and on the red-and-white kerbs where you're not supposed to park. Some park right at the entrance to the soi, which is genuinely dangerous, simply because it's next to the restaurant they're going to.

It amazes me how little thought people give to where they park. Parking a bit further down the road is the right thing to do, but it's inconvenient and you're out in the weather a little longer. So people don't.

To be fair, after a lot of complaints, officials have started clamping and fining people there. That's a good start.

## Bikes on the pavement

It isn't just cars. Motorbikes park on the pavement all the time, and it shows how bad habits spread.

One bike parks on the pavement, and everyone else thinks, "Okay, that must be fine then." A few months later, it's normal for everyone to park there. Then a family comes along with a pushchair and can't get past between the bikes and a lamppost, so they have to go out into the road and back onto the pavement. Nobody thinks much of it, but it's a real inconvenience, and it isn't safe. It's the same pattern I wrote about in [how one person's experience becomes the norm](/articles/how-one-persons-experience-becomes-the-norm).

## Rules are pointless without enforcement

That's always been the problem here in Thailand, and it seems especially true in Pattaya. They paint the red-and-white markings on the kerb, and then nobody enforces them. So what's the point?

## What I'd do if I were mayor

If I were mayor of Pattaya, I'd bring in proper parking enforcement, for cars and motorbikes, with wardens on foot, bicycles and motorbikes.

I'd also see it as a way to raise revenue. If you're driving a car, you're generally better off than the average person. Fining people who park dangerously or illegally is a fair way to raise money that can go back into the city.

This isn't a criticism of anyone. It's a suggestion. When rules aren't enforced, people assume anything goes. A bit of consistent enforcement would make the streets safer and easier for everyone, especially pedestrians.

## Let residents report it

The way I'd do it is to involve the community. Publish a number and tell people: if you see someone riding on the pavement or parking on the red and white, call us. We'll come down, and if they're still there when we arrive, they get a fine.

The caller wouldn't get anything for it. It would just be a good Samaritan thing. Bangkok tried paying people for this a few years ago, offering informants half the fine for reporting motorbikes on pavements. I don't think you even need to pay. Most people want to see the city improve, and there are definitely enough of us who'd happily pick up the phone if someone keeps parking where they shouldn't.

## The other side

Enforcement only works if it's fair. There need to be enough legal places to park, clear signs and markings, and fines that are applied consistently, not just to some people. Otherwise it risks feeling like a revenue grab rather than a safety measure, which is exactly how many people see traffic wardens back home.

## My take

I never thought I'd say it, but I'd welcome traffic wardens in Pattaya. A little enforcement would go a long way.
