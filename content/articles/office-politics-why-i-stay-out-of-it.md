Office politics happens in pretty much every workplace, and mine are no exception. In my experience it mostly comes down to two things: gossip and jealousy.

## Jealousy

In the office, if someone is doing well, they're normally congratulated. But if they're doing so well that they make everyone else look bad, that creates tension.

It isn't just offices. In the restaurant business, when a member of staff gets a lot of attention from customers, it can put other people's noses out of joint.

## When work friends become family

Thai people are very friendly, and a couple of my offices have almost a family atmosphere. Staff often go out and socialise together.

That's lovely in many ways, but it has its problems. It's not just what happens in the office any more. It's also what happens on a night out: who likes who, who doesn't, and so on. All of that comes back into work with them, and naturally it leads to gossip.

In my experience, friendships made at work here often carry on into people's free time. In the West, I think the line between social and professional tends to be a bit firmer.

## Why I stay out of it

Personally, I work very hard not to get involved in any of it. It's actually one of the reasons I don't spend long in any one office.

I keep things very professional, and I don't socialise with my staff. At a staff party, I might turn up for 10 or 15 minutes to say hello and maybe make a little speech, but I won't stay and drink. I don't agree with it personally, and it just doesn't fit me.

## My advice

Stay out of office politics as much as you can. I give my staff the same advice, though I know it's easier said than done when you're in among it every day.
