My advice to anyone in business, anywhere, is to keep a low profile. If you're doing well, however you define that, you don't need to shout about it. In Thailand, I think that matters even more.

## Sharing isn't showing off

There's a difference between getting in front of people and showing off. With this site, I'm trying to reach more people and share what I've learned. That's very different from shouting from the rooftops about how successful you are, or how you want to be seen.

I'm not even sure why some people do it. Maybe they think it brings in more business. But I think you have to be a bit humble about your success.

## Why it matters more in Thailand

It's human nature for some people not to like seeing others do well. Unless they're successful themselves, someone else's success can feel like a reminder of how they're doing.

Add to that the fact that, as foreigners, we're guests in someone else's country. Many Thais are protective of their country, their industries and their businesses. They want you to be successful, because it means you're employing Thais and the country benefits. But they definitely don't want you showing off, driving around in flashy cars, or telling them how things should be done in an arrogant or aggressive way.

## Plenty of naysayers

Pattaya has another reason to keep your head down. There are plenty of naysayers here, people who haven't done so well themselves and are quick to knock anyone who has. Quietly getting on with it gives them less to aim at.

## What it looks like in practice

It comes down to the car you drive, the clothes you wear, and how you talk.

I do drive a reasonably nice car, a Mercedes. It says I'm doing fine, but it isn't flashy. The same goes for everything else. The better way is simply to let the results speak for themselves.

## My take

Be proud of what you've built, but you don't need to advertise it. Stay humble, respect that you're a guest here, and let your work do the talking.
