You can never tell which customer will leave your car at the other end of the country. I've run Pattaya Rent a Car for more than 15 years, and the ones who do it are almost never the ones you'd expect. More in [the rental that never came back](/articles/the-rental-that-never-came-back).

## The family who asked about Phuket

A few years ago a family of four asked before booking whether they could drop the car in Phuket, and whether we had an office there. We don't. We're Pattaya Rent a Car, with one office in Pattaya, and we told them clearly that we don't offer that.

They rented anyway. Near the end of the rental they messaged to ask if they could keep the car a couple of extra days. My staff said no problem, we'd take the extra days out of the deposit.

A couple of days went by and we heard nothing. When we finally got hold of them, they told us they'd left the car in their hotel car park, down near the Thai–Malaysian border, around Hat Yai.

## Sixteen hours back

The deposit didn't come close to covering it, as it had already gone on the extra days. One of my staff flew down. Luckily the car had GPS, so finding it was easy. Then he drove it back. It took him about 16 hours.

That's as far from our office as you can almost get. Even the far north past Chiang Mai wouldn't be a 16-hour drive. Of everything customers have done to us over the years, and some of it has been pretty outrageous, that one sticks in my head.

## It keeps happening

We never got a penny back. The pattern is usually the same: no social media presence, an email address that doesn't bounce but never gets a reply, and our number blocked. It makes you think it isn't their first time.

This year alone we've had a car left in Hua Hin and two or three left at the airport. One of those was another ordinary family of four. Whenever my staff tell me about one, I ask who the customer was, how old, where from. And every time I'm surprised by how normal they seemed.

## How we handle drop-offs

We don't do one-way rentals. Cars come back to our office in Pattaya. If someone wants to drop at the airport, we'll reluctantly collect it there for a charge, but it has to be arranged in advance, and really it has to be someone we know.

Otherwise we'd rather you dropped the car in Pattaya and we'll organise a taxi to the airport. It saves us money and hassle, and it saves you a bit of money too. ## Why not just take a bigger deposit?

People say: take a credit card, or take a bigger deposit. A bigger deposit doesn't really work. Most car rental agencies in Pattaya and across Thailand take around ฿5,000, some ฿8,000 for bigger cars, and I don't think doubling it would stop someone who's already decided to walk away.

The big international companies take credit cards. We can take one too, but we also accept cash, because not everyone has a credit card. We didn't want to lose good customers by insisting on one. It's part of what makes us different from the bigger players.

So the honest answer is that there's no real way to remove the risk. The customers who do this aren't who you'd expect, and if we treated everyone as a suspect we'd let down the many who bring the car back on time.
