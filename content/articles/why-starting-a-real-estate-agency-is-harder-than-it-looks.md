Starting a real estate agency in Pattaya is cheap and easy. Making one work is a different story. I met someone recently who's finding that out the hard way, and it's a trap I see a lot of agents walk into.

## Why so many people have a go

A young agent I know was doing well at another agency. He started in rentals and spent his last couple of years there in sales. The office became too toxic for him, so he left and set up on his own. I can completely see why.

The barrier to entry is very low. An office costs around ฿15,000 a month in rent. You can get a desk, a chair and a computer for under ฿30,000 to ฿40,000. All in, you could be up and running for no more than ฿100,000 to ฿150,000.

There's no licensing for real estate agents in Thailand, so you don't have to worry about that either. You could literally wake up one morning and decide to open an agency.

And when you're an agent, you see the deals flowing and the agency taking the lion's share of the commission. It's easy to think: I could do this myself. More in [working as a property agent in Pattaya](/articles/working-as-a-property-agent-in-pattaya).

## Six months without a deal

When we met, they told me they hadn't done a single deal in about six months. They're not looking to sell up, but it isn't working, and they're looking at their options.

I think he went in with distorted expectations, maybe a little arrogance. He'd been doing sales, so he assumed he'd open an agency and the sales would flow in. Only now, six months on, has he realised they probably need to start doing rentals. You start at the bottom and work your way up. You can't go straight in at the top.

## Why rentals come first

I can see why people avoid rentals. There's a lot more work for much less money, and there's the aftercare: the tenant and owner issues that carry on after the deal, which you don't get so much with a sale.

But for a new agency, rentals do several things:

- **They bring listings.** And a rental often comes with a sale tag as well.
- **They let you prove yourself.** It's a chance to show an owner how professional you are and build a relationship.
- **They get you known.** Every rental is someone else who's heard of you.
- **The deals come more easily.** Rentals are easier to close than sales.

You could almost see them as a loss leader, even though you do actually make money from them. You have to do rentals to get the sales.

## The chicken and the egg

The other big problem is listings. He had a couple of dozen. Across our agencies we have around 13,500. It takes years to build up a decent inventory.

And without listings, you struggle to get leads. Without leads, owners have little reason to list with you. It's chicken and egg.

## Standing out in the crowd

Then there's standing out. You're competing with companies that have been around for 20 or 30 years. If you're doing exactly what everyone else does, just smaller and newer, it's no wonder you find it hard.

My advice was that you need a real niche: a marketing angle nobody else has, or a particular type of customer you go after better than anyone. Generic doesn't work against the established names.

## You have to spend on marketing

The other thing I told him, and I'd tell any startup, is that you have to spend money on advertising. A new agency has no organic reach. The website is new, so it doesn't rank. There's no reputation yet and no repeat business.

At Alan Bolton we don't need to spend as much, because we've been around a long time and have that reputation. But if you're new to the market, you have to overspend on advertising. That's where I think they're going wrong. More in [marketing a small business in Pattaya](/articles/marketing-a-small-business-in-pattaya).

## Cheap to start doesn't mean a good idea

Just because it doesn't cost much to set up doesn't mean it's a great idea. From the outside it looks simple: an office, a few listings, a share of the commission. From the inside, it's years of building stock, leads and a name, starting with the rentals nobody wants to do and a marketing budget you can't skip.

If you're an agent thinking of going it alone, ask yourself what makes you different, and how you'll get found, before you sign the lease.
