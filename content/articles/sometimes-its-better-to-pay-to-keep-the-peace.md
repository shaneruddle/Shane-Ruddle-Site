A good rental agent is a mediator, not a messenger. We learned that the hard way recently, when a ฿500 disagreement between a landlord and a tenant escalated so badly that we ended up paying ฿3,000 ourselves just to calm everything down. It was money well spent.

## A ฿3,000 upgrade

A condo building decided to upgrade its internet from copper wire to fibre optic, and charged each unit owner a fee of ฿3,000.

The owner of one of our managed units had a tenant in it. Since the tenant would benefit too, the owner suggested splitting it 50-50, ฿1,500 each. It seemed fair, and the tenant agreed.

Then, for whatever reason, the tenant decided he'd only pay ฿1,000. When the owner asked why he was being short-changed ฿500, the tenant's messages got aggressive.

## Where we went wrong

This is the part I've told my staff about many times. Landlords and tenants don't talk to each other directly. They talk to us. So if a tenant is angry with a landlord, it's our job to make sure the landlord doesn't feel the full force of it. We soften the messages, pass on the point, and leave out the heat.

Instead, our agent simply screenshotted each person's messages and sent them to the other. Neither of them realised their words were being passed on as they were. Effectively, they were having a row with each other through us.

It turned a molehill into a mountain. The owner, having read all the abuse, said he now refused to pay a single baht towards the internet. And the TV the tenant wanted replaced, which was very old, he wasn't replacing that either.

## Paying to keep the peace

My manager had to step in and calm things down. In the end, we paid the ฿3,000 for the internet upgrade ourselves.

That kept both sides apart, took the heat out of it, and protected a landlord relationship and a tenancy that were worth far more to us than ฿3,000.

I first mentioned this dispute, while it was still going on, in [when a landlord decides to go direct](/articles/when-a-landlord-decides-to-go-direct). This is how it ended.

## The lesson

- **Be the buffer.** When you're in the middle, never just forward messages, and certainly never screenshot them. Pass on the facts, calmly.
- **Small amounts can cause big fights.** This started over ฿500.
- **Know when to pay.** Sometimes covering a small cost yourself is cheaper than losing a client, a tenant, or days of your team's time.
