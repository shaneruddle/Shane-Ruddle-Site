I was driving along South Pattaya Road recently when I noticed a huge new structure going up that I'd known nothing about. From the hoardings, it looks like a new show venue.

What struck me first is that even when the wider economy feels like it's going backwards, there's still major investment being pumped into Pattaya all the time.

## Just up the road, an empty theatre

But here's the odd part. Not far away is the Kaan Show theatre. It was a big, impressive production. I went to see it when it first opened, and it was a very good show. It only ran for a couple of years before it closed during COVID, and it has sat empty for more than six years since.

Now, just up the road, someone is building a whole new venue from scratch.

## Why not repurpose what's already there?

I honestly don't understand why they wouldn't take over the existing theatre rather than build another one.

I think a lot of it comes down to planning. In the West, a big project like this would go through planning permission, and someone would probably ask: do we really need a new building, or could we repurpose one that's already there? Here, those questions don't seem to get asked.

## Make developers give something back

I remember back in England, when Tesco wanted to open a new supermarket, the local council leaned on them to improve the roads around it. Partly to make sure access in and out was safe and efficient, but also as a kind of deal: if you want to come in here and make money, the area and the local people need to benefit too. Not just the jobs, but better infrastructure. In England that's done through planning agreements, often called Section 106 agreements.

That seems sensible, logical and even business-minded to me. And it just doesn't seem to happen here. A massive shopping centre can open with one tiny entrance and exit, clog up the traffic around it, and not improve the infrastructure one bit.

## A suggestion

This isn't a criticism of anyone. Investment in Pattaya is a good thing, and I'm glad people still believe in the city. But it would be worth looking at how we can bring empty venues back into use before building new ones next door, and asking big developments to improve the roads around them. It would save money, cut waste and stop good buildings going derelict.

## My take

The fact that people are still building big in Pattaya says a lot about its future. I just hope someone also finds a use for the venues we already have.
