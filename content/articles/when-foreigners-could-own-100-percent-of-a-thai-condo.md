Most people buying in Pattaya know the rule: foreigners can own up to 49% of the floor space in a condo building. What fewer people know is that for five years, foreigners could own up to 100%. I wanted to understand how that happened, why it ended, and what it means today.

## Before the crash: 40%

In the 1990s, the foreign quota wasn't 49%. It was 40% of the total unit space in a building.

## 1999: A crisis-era change

After the Asian financial crisis, Thailand was left with a lot of empty, newly built condos. In April 1999 the government amended the Condominium Act to bring in foreign money.

It did two things:

- **It raised the normal foreign quota from 40% to 49%.** That's the limit we still have today.
- **It allowed foreigners to go above 49%, up to the whole building,** in qualifying condos, as a temporary measure.

## The conditions

Not every building qualified. According to the Land Department, the building had to:

- be in Bangkok, a municipal area or another local administrative area, such as Pattaya City
- sit on land of no more than five rai, including the common areas
- have at least 40 units
- have been registered at least a year before the foreigner applied to buy
- not be in a military safety zone

The buyer also had to bring the money in from abroad.

So Pattaya was included. As far as I know, there was never a published list of buildings. Any condo that met the conditions qualified.

## Why it ended

Here's what surprised me. It wasn't reversed because the government decided 49% was better. The rule was written with a five-year limit from the start, and it ended in April 2004.

I couldn't find an official reason for not extending it. My guess is the market had recovered by then. The 49% quota, on the other hand, was the permanent change, and it was actually an increase.

## What it means today

Foreigners who bought under the five-year rule were allowed to keep their units, even above 49%. And foreigners who receive those units from them can keep them too.

That means there are buildings out there, including in Pattaya, that are more than 49% foreign-owned. If you want to know about a specific building, the juristic office or the Land Office is the place to ask.

*I'm not a lawyer. This is my understanding of the history, not legal advice. If you're buying in a building like this, get a lawyer to check the foreign quota before you pay anything.*

## My take

Thailand has allowed foreigners to own whole condo buildings before, to fill empty units after a crisis.
