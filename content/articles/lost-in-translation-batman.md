When you don't speak fluent Thai, you often don't really know what's going on in your own business. Small misunderstandings can have big consequences, and sometimes you never even find out what you did. I've learned that the hard way, twice. ## The day "Batman" nearly cost me a member of staff

In the early days of Pattaya Rent a Car, I walked past one of my staff on the way to the bathroom. This was long before COVID, but for whatever reason he was wearing a face mask. So as I went past, I said "Batman" and carried on walking.

By the time I got back, my manager came over to tell me he was ready to quit. I had no idea why. It turned out he'd misheard me. To him, it sounded like "pak maa", which literally means "dog mouth". It's quite an insult. And because he was wearing a mask, he thought I was suggesting he had it on to hide his mouth.

I explained I'd said "Batman" because of the mask, and we all had a good laugh about it. He still works for me today. But it could easily have gone the other way, over something completely innocent.

## The best friends who hated each other

Years ago, when I was working at the Sportsman, there were two girls on the team I was sure were best friends, just from the way they were with each other. I worked with them six days a week.

At a staff party, after a few too many drinks, the two of them ended up in a real physical fight. When it had calmed down and I asked around, the other staff told me they hated each other. I'd got their relationship completely wrong, despite seeing them nearly every day.

## Why it happens

The language barrier is bigger than most people think. Even if you pick up some Thai, many staff here also speak Isan, the language of the northeast, among themselves. They can be having a whole conversation right in front of you and you'll have no idea what's being said. More in [learning Thai](/articles/learning-thai).

## What I do now

In every one of my businesses, there's a foreign manager who runs things and handles what a Thai national can't. And there's also a Thai manager. The foreign manager relies heavily on the Thai manager to pick up the nuances and what's really going on among the staff, the things we'd otherwise miss because of the language.

## My advice

Assume you don't know the full picture, because you probably don't. Have someone you trust on the team who understands the language and the culture, and listen to them. It will save you a lot of confusion, and maybe a good member of staff.
