I'm all for giving people jobs, and a good caddy is worth every baht. But I've always thought compulsory caddies in Thailand should be optional, at least for golfers who know what they're doing.

## How it started, or so the story goes

The story you'll hear everywhere is that as farmland was turned into golf courses, the government insisted that courses employ local people who'd lost their farm work, and caddying was the way to do it. I've always believed that's how it came about, though I've never seen it confirmed by an official source.

Whatever the origin, I understand the idea. If a course takes away people's livelihoods, giving them work makes sense.

## Often, they're just carrying the bag

The problem is what you actually get, especially at some of the cheaper, three-star courses. A lot of the time, it's someone who just carries your bag, with no real help on the course.

At courses with compulsory carts, it's even more obvious. Your bag goes on the back of the cart, the caddy sits in the cart, and all they do is hand you the club you ask for.

The first few times, it's part of the experience. Once you're past that, especially if you're a decent golfer who knows the course, you don't really need one. (I've written about the good caddies, and the ones who help you read Thailand's grainy greens, in [golf in Thailand vs the UK](/articles/golf-in-thailand-vs-the-uk).)

## It can double the cost of a round

These days it doesn't affect me much. I hardly ever play, and I've got a bit more money than I used to. But when I was scraping by, I'd play a course where the green fee was ฿300. The caddy fee was ฿400, and then I'd tip around ฿300 on top.

So having a caddy cost more than twice as much as playing the round of golf.

## What I'd change

I'd make caddies optional, but not for everyone.

If a tourist turns up and says they don't want a caddy, they won't know where they're going, and they may not even be able to drive the cart. I get that. So I'd have some kind of pass, maybe for residents or golfers who've passed a simple test, that lets you go without a caddy.

You could still pay a couple of hundred baht for the privilege, and that money could go into a fund for the caddies. It would still be far cheaper than paying a caddy fee and a tip, and nobody loses their job.

## The other side

Plenty of golfers love the Thai caddy system, and for many visitors it's a highlight of a golf trip. A lot of people, mostly women from rural communities, earn their living from caddying. Making caddies optional could put some of those jobs at risk, which is exactly why any change would need to protect them.

## My take

If you've played a course 20 times, you're a good golfer and you're riding in a compulsory cart, being forced to take a caddy along just doesn't sit right. Give regular golfers the choice.
