When a property deal doesn't go a buyer's way, some buyers blame the agency and take their business elsewhere. But the agent doesn't own the property. The seller has the final say. Here's a recent case that shows why punishing the agent usually hurts the buyer too.

## The deal

A buyer viewed a condo with one of our agents. He was happy with the price and didn't ask for much of a reduction. But he didn't have the funds yet, and wanted to complete in six months' time.

That didn't suit the seller. The only way it could work was with a significant deposit. The seller wanted at least 20%. The buyer only wanted to put down 10%.

## Why 10% wasn't enough

The unit was part-way through a renovation, and the buyer wanted a say in how the rest of it was finished. In his eyes, putting down 10% meant he was definitely taking it.

The seller saw it differently. Six months is a long time, and circumstances change. If she customised the renovation to suit him, and he then walked away, 10% wouldn't cover the cost of undoing it or the time lost. A bigger deposit was her protection. I think she was right.

## Going direct, then going elsewhere

The buyer went back to his home country and started contacting the seller directly. That was fine. The seller was open about it and wasn't trying to cut us out. But she held firm on the deposit, and the deal fizzled out.

Then we found out the buyer had gone to see other units in the same building, through another agency. We had those units listed too. We could have shown him every one of them.

It happens more and more. A buyer doesn't get the deal he wanted, so he takes his business somewhere else, as if the agency were to blame.

## What I'd say to buyers

- **The agent isn't the seller.** We negotiate on your behalf and try our best, but we don't own the unit. The final decision is the seller's.
- **A bigger deposit is fair on a long completion.** If you want months to pay, and a say in the work, expect to commit more up front.
- **If one unit doesn't work, ask about others.** Your agent may well have other units in the same building, or something better nearby.
- **Be a little sympathetic.** Most agents have done a lot of work for you by the time a deal falls through.

Walking away from an agent who knows what you want, and who already has the alternatives, doesn't punish the agent nearly as much as it slows you down.
