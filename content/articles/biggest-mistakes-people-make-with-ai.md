I use AI every day across my businesses, and I've made a few mistakes along the way. Here are the biggest ones I see. (If you're just starting, read [how AI will change small businesses in Pattaya](/articles/how-ai-will-change-small-businesses-in-pattaya) first.)

## Thinking it's a fad

The biggest mistake is not trying it at all. Some people think AI is a fad that will fizzle out, so there's no point learning anything about it. That's underestimating exactly what it is.

## Trusting it blindly

The next mistake is believing everything it says and never questioning it. AI tends to be sycophantic: it agrees with a lot of what you come up with. Run a business idea past it, and it may well tell you it's fantastic. That doesn't mean it is.

You have to prompt it the right way. Ask it to play devil's advocate, or to make the counter-argument.

## A test you can try

Tell one AI about a business idea and listen to it tell you how great it is. Then go to a different AI, say you've come up with the same idea, and ask it for every reason why it won't work. You'll get two completely different arguments, and both will be very convincing. That's why you should never take its first answer as the final word.

## Being careless with keys and passwords

I'm not too worried about privacy. I use Claude and ChatGPT, and I trust they have the right safeguards in place. But you still need to be sensible.

When I first started using Gemini, Google's AI, we left an API key exposed, and somebody took it and used it. Google refunded me the usage, but it was a lesson. Be careful where you put API keys, passwords and anything similar. These days AI will often warn you not to do it, but don't be silly about it.
