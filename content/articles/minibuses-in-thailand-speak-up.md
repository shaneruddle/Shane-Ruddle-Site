I've been in plenty of minibuses in Thailand where the driver went far too fast and drove recklessly. A few times, I said nothing. Most tourists don't either, because they're worried about the reaction. But if you don't say something, you could end up in a ditch.

## The trip to Kanchanaburi

A while back, I took my family from Pattaya to [Kanchanaburi](/articles/kanchanaburi-from-pattaya) by minibus. The driver was speeding from the start, and his driving was erratic. It looked like he was nodding off at the wheel.

In the end, I lost it a bit and shouted out to ask if he was okay. He pulled the minibus over and got quite aggressive. It turned out he wasn't falling asleep. He was looking down at his phone, which is just as bad.

He didn't like being called out, and it ended up souring our whole two-day trip.

On the way back, he drove like an idiot again, until we were about 50 km from Pattaya. Then, suddenly, he started driving sensibly. Maybe he thought it would earn him a tip. It didn't. We got out, said goodbye, and that was that.

## Why minibuses are so risky

Take a look at any minibus you come across in Thailand. I'd bet it has scrapes and dents all around the outside. You see minibus accidents all over the country.

In my experience, the drivers generally aren't vetted, and many aren't particularly good drivers. And the incentives are all wrong. The faster they get you there, the sooner they can pick up another fare or have a rest. They always seem to be in a rush, and safety rarely comes first.

## What passengers can do

- **Don't book some guy off the internet.** If you take a minibus because of cost, go through a proper company, so you have someone to complain to.
- **Speak up early, and calmly.** Ask the driver to slow down before it gets out of hand, rather than waiting until you snap like I did. But do it privately, so he doesn't lose face. That was my mistake: I called him out in front of my family and a minibus full of people. Ask for a toilet break, then have a quiet word with him on the side. If he starts driving more sensibly, thank him for it at the next stop. If you're really worried, offer a tip for a slower, safer drive.
- **Wear a seatbelt** if there is one.
- **Report a dangerous driver** to the company afterwards, with the date and time.

## My advice: rent a car

I'll be upfront: I run a car rental company in Pattaya, so take this however you like. But my honest advice is to [rent a car](/articles/renting-a-car-in-pattaya) and drive yourself. I'd say that even if I didn't own one.

You control the speed, you stop when you want, and you're not putting your family in the hands of a stranger who's in a hurry. (Here's what to expect when [driving in Pattaya](/articles/driving-in-pattaya).)

If you do take a minibus, choose a proper company, and don't be afraid to speak up. It might feel awkward, but it's a lot better than the alternative.
