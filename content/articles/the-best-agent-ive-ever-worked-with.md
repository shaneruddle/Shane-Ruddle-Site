The best agent I've ever worked with doesn't wait to be handed anything. She takes the platform we give her and runs with it. That's the whole lesson of this piece: be proactive, not reactive.

## Lowering my expectations

I've been in real estate for about five or six years now. From the start I had a picture in my head of what a good agent looks like and how they work. I never really found one. Maybe my expectations were too high, so over time I lowered them.

Then one agent came along who does the job exactly how I'd do it if I were in her shoes. I won't name her, but out of the close to 20 agents across our agencies, she's consistently the top performer.

## What she does differently

**She uses her downtime.** Over the years we've given all our staff a list of things to do when it's quiet. Most ignore it. She's the only one who's consistently active, especially on Facebook.

**She responds fast.** When an enquiry comes in, she gets a phone number and calls. Her response time is far shorter than anyone else's.

**She finds her own stock.** She goes through our system looking for properties that are coming up for rent or could be sold, and then markets them herself.

**She has a personal brand.** She isn't shy about putting herself on camera doing her job, and she gets thousands of views. Every time she closes a deal, she celebrates it on social media so people know she's successful.

**She works in stages.** From enquiry to closed deal is a long process. A lot of agents look at it as one big mountain. She breaks it down: make contact, qualify whether the property suits the client, follow up, book the viewing, and so on. Each stage is a small, clear job.

**She doesn't get knocked back.** She's passionate about the industry and hungry for deals. When one falls through, she isn't fazed. She just gets on with the next.

## Reactive versus proactive

The mistake I see most agents make is being reactive. They join us and think: great, I'm in the door, now give me leads and help me make money. They expect it laid on a plate.

She flips that. Her attitude is: here's the platform, here are the foundations, here's a reputable company behind me, and here's what I'm supposed to do. Then she cracks on.

That applies to anyone in any business, not just property. The company can give you the tools, but nobody can do the work for you. Take control of it. Be proactive over reactive. Be more like her.
