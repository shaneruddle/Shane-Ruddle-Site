I read a comment online recently that said if a business replies to your email at midnight, you can guarantee it's the owner. I could definitely relate. Nobody cares about your business as much as you do.

## The mistake owners make

A lot of owners make the mistake of expecting their staff to care as much as they do, especially at the start, when they're putting their heart and soul into it.

But most staff are there for a salary. They don't really care about your long-term plans, because most of them don't have long-term plans with you. Their plans are usually short term. If they do end up staying for years, it's often not because they planned it. That's just how things worked out. (More on [when being the owner doesn't feel worth it](/articles/when-being-the-owner-doesnt-feel-worth-it).)

## You're on your own

It's really about realising that you're alone on this journey. Nobody understands what it's like unless they've done it themselves, and you can't expect them to. You can't expect anyone to understand what it feels like to lose money.

It's tempting to think that if you tell your staff the business is losing money, they'll pull together. Mostly, they won't care, and why would they? Even if you got to the point where you couldn't pay wages, they'd put up with it for a month and then go and work for someone else.

## Not everyone, but nobody as much as you

This isn't true of everyone. I absolutely have staff who care about the business. Just not as much as the owner does, which is obvious and completely natural. ([The best agent I've ever worked with](/articles/the-best-agent-ive-ever-worked-with) is a good example of someone who did care.)

## Depressing and freeing

Once you really accept it, it's a little depressing. You're in it alone, and the responsibility is yours.

But it's also freeing. Otherwise you're living under the illusion that your employees are in it for the long term and care about the business the way you do. Let that go, and you can stop being disappointed, and get on with running it.
