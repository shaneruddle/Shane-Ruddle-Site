There are months when being the business owner doesn't feel worth it. Last month, in a couple of my businesses, some of my top staff took home more money than the business did. When that happens, even for a few months of the year, you start to wonder whether you've got this the wrong way round.

## My granddad's advice

I always remember my granddad saying that if he had his time again, he'd have just worked for somebody else. He'd been an entrepreneur in the UK, and he said he'd have avoided all the stress and responsibility. Let somebody else deal with it.

For most of my career I didn't really understand that. Some months, I do.

## The costs nobody sees

When business is quiet and profit is thin, all the costs that come with employing people start to stand out:

- **The cost to hire.** Finding the right person takes time and money.
- **The training.** You invest in someone and get them up to speed.
- **Then they leave.** And you start again.
- **The cost to fire.** Letting someone go isn't free either.
- **The liability.** I recently learned that in Thailand the business is legally responsible for staff mistakes towards customers. That's a risk most people don't think about. More in [twenty years in business and I've only just learned this](/articles/i-only-just-learned-this-about-my-staffs-mistakes).

Add that up in a bad month and it can feel like the system favours the employee over the employer.

## More protection, more cost

Thailand has brought in a lot more protection for employees over the years: minimum wage rises, severance pay, social security. That's a good thing, and it was always going to happen. But it does mean owning a business here is becoming a lot more costly.

It's not just the rules, it's how they're enforced. In the past, if a member of staff made a mistake, people used common sense: you made the mistake, so you put it right. Now the answer is more often "the law says the employer pays", even when the business itself did nothing wrong. The protection was always there on paper. It just wouldn't really have been an issue before. More in [hiring and firing in Pattaya](/articles/hiring-and-firing-in-pattaya).

## The upside and the downside

The other side is that when times are good, the upside is significant, a lot more than anyone would earn working for someone else. As an owner, your upside is uncapped.

But so is your downside. Your staff get paid whether the month was good or bad. You only get what's left.

## Why the owner has to take the lion's share

That's the real point. Over the year, the business has to make sure the owner takes home a lot more than the employees. That isn't greed. It's payment for all the unwritten expenses and the unwritten risk that nobody sees, and that employees understandably never think about.

If you're running a business and that isn't happening, something needs to change. And if you're an employee wondering why the owner takes so much, it's because they're the one carrying the risk when things go wrong. More in [paying for my staff's mistakes](/articles/paying-for-my-staffs-mistakes).
