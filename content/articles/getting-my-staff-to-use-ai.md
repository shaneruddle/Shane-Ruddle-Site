The hardest part of bringing AI and new technology into my businesses isn't the technology. It's the people. My answer, in the end, is simple: persistence beats resistance. More in [how AI will change small businesses in Pattaya](/articles/how-ai-will-change-small-businesses-in-pattaya).

## People don't like change

The biggest pushback is just that people don't like change. I'm not sure it's unique to AI. If someone has done a job the same way for ten years, as some of my staff have, anything new that makes them stop and think meets resistance.

## A real example: digital contracts

At both property agencies, every contract is a little different. We start from a template, then owners, tenants and buyers want clauses added or taken out. Agents were adding things themselves, which left the company exposed, and neither management nor I were happy with it.

So we moved contracts out of Google Docs and into our own back-end system, with one source of truth so nothing slips through the cracks. We also brought in digital signatures. Agents had been spending ages printing contracts, getting them signed, then photographing or scanning them to send on.

There was major pushback. The staff didn't like it, and even some customers weren't keen, though it saved everyone time and hassle. More in [AI for property agents](/articles/ai-for-property-agents-in-pattaya).

## How I get it done

I introduce changes slowly. There's always pushback at first, because people don't see the benefits yet. I keep going, and after a while I switch off the old way, so there's no choice. I've found forcing the change is the only thing that really works.

Afterwards, quite a few staff have admitted the new way is better. I wouldn't say they thanked me, but they came round.

## "But I'm not that busy anyway"

A common reaction is: why do I need to save five or ten minutes on this? I'm not that busy anyway.

My answer is that it's only partly about saving time. It's about being more productive, and above all about saving brain space. If something can be automated or improved with technology, do it. Even if that leaves you sitting around with time on your hands, that's thinking time, to work out how to do things better.

## My advice to other owners

Persistence beats resistance. This technology isn't going anywhere, and it's only going to get better and more widely used. If you don't persist, you may not be in business in a few years.

With really reluctant staff, I'd lay it out plainly. The way we work is changing. If we don't get on board and our competitors do, there's a real chance this company won't survive, and then everyone's looking for a new job.
