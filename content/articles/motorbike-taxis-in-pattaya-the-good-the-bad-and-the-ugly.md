Motorbike taxis are part of Pattaya life. They're cheap, quick and everywhere. But like most things here, there's a good side, a bad side and, occasionally, an ugly side.

## The good

They're convenient and cheap. You'll find them on most major junctions, which also makes them useful in another way. They're often first on the scene at accidents, and they help report serious crimes. In Pattaya, they've always acted as a kind of lookout.

When I first came here, I used them a lot, especially after a couple of drinks. Back then there was no Grab or Bolt, and Pattaya never really had meter taxis. You still rarely see them today. Motorbike taxis were the only real option.

## The bad

There's very little real regulation. Even if drivers have some kind of licence, some of them aren't particularly good or safe riders. Some don't maintain their bikes very well either. When a trade isn't properly regulated, you get some bad drivers.

## The ugly

A recent incident in Bangkok shows the ugly side. A pedestrian on a zebra crossing asked a motorbike taxi driver why he was parked on it. The driver got off his bike and started fighting him. It happened in Bangkok, but I can easily see it happening here.

My feeling is that because the authorities have encouraged motorbike taxi drivers to act as lookouts, a few of them take it too far. They start to believe they have more authority than they really do, and sometimes they use it.

## My tip: book through an app

These days, if I needed a motorbike taxi, I'd book it through Grab or Bolt. Many local drivers are on those apps too, so you're not taking business away from them.

You pay digitally, the price is confirmed upfront so there's no haggling, and you don't have to explain where you're going. That helps, because some drivers are new to the city and don't know their way around. With the app, they just follow the map. ## What I'd do if I were mayor

This is a suggestion, not a criticism. If I were mayor of Pattaya, I'd introduce a city-run motorbike taxi app that mirrors the Grab model. Every driver would be registered, regulated and reviewed by customers. Prices would be transparent, with no haggling. I'd even consider making it the only way to book in the city, instead of Grab and Bolt. With the way the world is going, I don't see why not.

## The other side

Not every driver or passenger is comfortable with smartphones, and plenty of drivers have built their living on regular customers at their stand. Government-run apps also don't always match the quality of the big platforms, and pushing Grab and Bolt out would reduce choice. Any change would need to bring the drivers along with it.

## My take

Most motorbike taxi drivers do a useful job, and the city is better with them than without them. But proper regulation, and an app that keeps everyone accountable, would cut out the bad and the ugly.
