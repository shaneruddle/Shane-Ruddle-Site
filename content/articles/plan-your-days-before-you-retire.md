Most people who retire here don't struggle with the culture. They struggle with finding things to do. That isn't unique to Thailand. It happens to retirees everywhere. The answer is to plan how you'll fill your days before you get here.

You'll plan the money, the visa and the healthcare anyway. This is about something people often forget: staying active, healthy and sociable, having a purpose, and staying out of the bars.

## Plan your 16 waking hours

Get a pen and paper and write down what you'll actually do with the roughly 16 hours a day you're awake. Note how long you think each thing will take.

A typical Monday might be breakfast with friends, a couple of hours of padel, a massage, and a film in the evening. Write down everything you think you'll do in retirement, then keep adding to it.

If you find it hard to fill the days, that's your cue to think outside the box and decide which hobbies you're going to take up.

## Start your hobbies before you retire

Once you've settled on a couple of hobbies, like golf or padel, start playing them before you retire.

Otherwise you arrive, take up golf, realise it's not for you, and suddenly have an extra 20 hours a week with nothing planned. That's exactly how people slip into the bar scene.

## Do a trial run

This is a must. I can't imagine anyone wakes up one day and decides to retire to Thailand without ever having been. Almost everyone comes for an extended holiday first, and they should.

Two or three months isn't the same as living here full time, but it gives you a real feel for the weather, the culture and how easy or hard it is to make friends, depending on the kind of person you are. You can also see how other retirees live, and whether they're the sort of people you want to spend time with.

## Try more than one place

I recently met someone who did this really sensibly. He was sure Thailand was where he wanted to retire, but not which part. So he spent six months travelling around the four main places: Hua Hin, Pattaya, Phuket and Chiang Mai.

In the end he settled on Hua Hin, mainly because of the kind of friends he was making there. As much as I love Pattaya, that's the right way to choose: by the people and the life you'll actually have.

## My take

Don't just plan your retirement money. Plan your days. Fill those 16 hours with things that keep you active, social and relaxed, and test them before you commit.
