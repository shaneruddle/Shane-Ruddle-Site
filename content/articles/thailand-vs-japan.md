I haven't been everywhere, but of all the countries I've visited, Japan is the only one I'd put in the same league as Thailand. If I had my time again, it would be right up there. In the end, though, Thailand still edges it for me.

## What puts Japan in the same league

**The people.** They're superb: well-mannered, respectful, accommodating and genuinely nice. For me, hospitality is a big thing when you visit a new country, and Japan has it.

**The food.** I'm biased because I love Japanese food, the sushi and the ramen. But even if it's not for you, there are plenty of other options.

**The price.** It's very good value at the moment, because the yen has fallen against most major currencies over the last few years.

**Variety.** It's a big country with everything from hot summers to some of the best skiing in the world. I've skied in Niseko, and it's exceptional.

## Why Thailand still wins for me

**The weather.** Skiing is fantastic, but I wouldn't want cold weather for more than a couple of weeks. I'd rather be a bit too hot than too cold. I'll take a comfortable 35 degrees.

**English.** In my experience, English isn't as widely spoken in Japan, so day to day it's a little easier to get around and do business here. More in [learning Thai](/articles/learning-thai).

**It's home.** I've been here so long that I know how things work. I'm biased, and I admit it.

## Working there might be easier

When I was skiing in Niseko, a lot of the people serving us were Australians, many of them presumably on working holiday visas. My impression is that working in Japan can be easier for some foreigners than working in Thailand, where many jobs are reserved for Thais.
