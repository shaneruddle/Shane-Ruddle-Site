I've said before that I don't actually want a blanket free-for-all on foreign ownership. But what I'd like and what I think will happen are two different things. Here's what I expect Thailand to change as it tries to join the OECD, and what I'd hope for on property.

## Thailand is the outlier

As I understand it, Thailand is one of the most restrictive countries for foreign investment and ownership compared with the OECD's current 38 members, if not the most restrictive. The OECD itself has told Thailand to reduce those barriers, especially in services. If it wants to join, that has to change.

And it's already starting to. This year the Cabinet approved exempting a handful of fairly specialised activities, such as services between companies in the same group and some financial and telecoms businesses, from needing a Foreign Business Licence. It's small, but it's a start.

## Prediction 1: The easing starts next year

I think Thailand will start easing the restrictions in 2027. Other countries that joined the OECD made big reforms during the accession process, before they were granted membership, not after. With Thailand targeting 2028, the changes need to come soon.

## Prediction 2: A Vietnam-style approach

I think Thailand will move towards something like Vietnam's model. There, foreigners can generally own 100% of a business unless it's in a specific restricted sector.

So my prediction is that 100% foreign ownership will become the norm for most businesses, with restrictions kept in certain industries.

## My hope for property: 100% foreign-owned condos

This one is a hope, not a prediction. On property, there are two changes that would make sense to me.

One is longer leases, say 50 years. But I think a longer lease could be open to manipulation in some way.

The one that makes the most sense to me is allowing 100% foreign ownership of condo buildings. I've never understood why we can't. Condos go up into the sky. They don't spread out across the land, and you're not buying the land itself. I've written more about this in [why Thailand's condo quota pushes prices up, not down](/articles/why-thailands-condo-quota-pushes-prices-up).

It's even happened before. After the Asian financial crisis, from 1999, Thailand allowed foreigners to own more than 49%, and up to 100%, of qualifying condo buildings for five years. I've written about [when foreigners could own 100% of a Thai condo](/articles/when-foreigners-could-own-100-percent-of-a-thai-condo).

## The other side

OECD membership doesn't automatically require Thailand to scrap its restrictions, and the 2028 target could slip. Reforms may well start with sectors like technology and professional services, leaving restaurants, property agencies and car rental until much later. And any move on property is likely to face strong opposition.

*These are my predictions and opinions, not legal or financial advice.*

## My take

Change is coming, and I think sooner than many people expect. Whether that's good news for existing foreign business owners like me is another question. See [my piece on the OECD and foreign ownership](/articles/thailand-oecd-and-foreign-ownership) for where I stand.
