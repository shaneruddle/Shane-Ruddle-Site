Most people assume that if a business is for sale, it isn't making money, and if a business closes, it never made money. I'm here to say that isn't always true.

## The business everyone wrote off

A number of years ago, I bought a business that I already knew quite well. When it closed, everyone who didn't know the background assumed it simply hadn't made money. I knew it had. It failed because of the owner's personal circumstances, not the numbers.

When it came on the market, I snapped it up. Partly because I knew the real story, and partly because there were no other takers. We were making a profit very early on.

You only get that kind of insight by living here, networking and knowing a lot of people. Most people would never have known.

## Why good businesses close

In my experience, there are a few common reasons a business that makes money still closes or gets sold.

**Personal problems.** Sometimes the owner's personal life simply takes over, and the business suffers or closes, even though it was profitable.

**A relationship ends.** I've seen businesses fall apart when the owner and their partner split up. The business was fine. The relationship wasn't.

**The owner leaves Thailand.** It doesn't happen often, but it does happen. I've seen a real estate agency sold not because it wasn't making money, but because the founder decided to go back to England. That might be because parents are getting older, to educate the children back home, or simply because they've had enough of Thailand.

## Many never even get sold

The other part of the story is that a lot of these businesses never come up for sale at all. Often it's too messy to sell, so the owner just closes it down. The business disappears, everyone says, "Oh, it didn't work out," and nobody gets the chance to ask why.

## How to tell the difference

If a business does come up for sale, the only real way to know is due diligence. Ask the question, probe, do your research and investigate. Most of the time, you can simply ask the owner why they're selling, and then check whether the answer stands up.

## My take

Don't assume a closed or for-sale business was a bad one. Sometimes the best opportunities are good businesses with a personal story behind them. You just have to be close enough to hear it.

*This is my own experience, not financial advice.*
