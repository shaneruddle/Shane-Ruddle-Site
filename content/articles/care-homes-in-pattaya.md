A care home in Pattaya costs from about ฿30,000 to ฿80,000 a month, depending on the room, the level of care and how much medical support is on site. That's a fraction of what residential care costs in the UK, the US or Australia, and it's one of the reasons more retirees here are planning to stay for the long term rather than going home when they need help.

I've lived in Pattaya for over 20 years, and I've watched plenty of friends move from living independently to needing a bit of support. Here's what I'd want to know if it were me or my family.

## How much does a care home in Pattaya cost?

Expect to pay somewhere between ฿30,000 and ฿80,000 a month. At the lower end you're usually looking at a shared or simpler room with help for daily living: meals, laundry, medication reminders and someone on hand day and night. At the higher end you get a private room, more staff per resident, nurses on site and care for more complex needs, such as dementia or recovery after a stroke.

Ask exactly what the monthly fee covers. Things that are sometimes extra include:

- Doctor and hospital visits, and transport to them
- Medicines and medical supplies
- Physiotherapy
- One-to-one care at night
- Laundry, toiletries and outings

A cheaper headline price can end up costing more once the extras are added, so get the full monthly cost in writing.

## What's the difference between a care home and a live-in carer?

A care home gives you a room, meals and staff around the clock in one place. It suits people who need regular help, or whose family isn't nearby to check on them.

A live-in or daily carer comes to your condo or house instead. Many people prefer it, because they keep their own home, their routine and their neighbours. It works well if you need help with a few things each day rather than nursing care. You'll need space for a live-in carer, and you're the one who has to arrange cover for days off and holidays, unless you use an agency that does it for you.

Plenty of people start with a carer at home and move into a care home later if their needs grow. There's no wrong answer. It's about what keeps you safe and still feels like your life.

## What should you look for when you visit?

Visit more than one, and go at different times of day, including a mealtime. Things I'd look at:

- **Staff.** How many carers are on at night, do any of them speak English well, and is there a nurse on site or on call?
- **Medical links.** Which hospital do they use in an emergency, and how quickly can they get someone there? Pattaya has good private hospitals, which I cover in [healthcare in Pattaya](/articles/healthcare-in-pattaya).
- **The residents.** Do they look cared for and engaged, or left in front of the television all day?
- **Cleanliness and smell.** You can tell a lot in the first minute.
- **Food.** Do they cook Western food as well as Thai, and can they handle special diets?
- **The contract.** What happens to the fee if your stay in hospital is long, how much notice do you need to give, and what deposit do they hold?

Talk to families of current residents if you can. They'll tell you what a brochure won't.

## Does health insurance cover care homes?

Usually not. Most expat health insurance policies cover treatment in hospital, but not ongoing long-term care in a home. Check your policy wording, and don't assume. If your policy has an upper age limit, find out now what happens when you reach it, because it's very hard to get new cover once you're older or have a condition.

That's why I always tell people to plan the money side early. If you know care could cost ฿30,000 to ฿80,000 a month on top of your usual living costs, you can work out now whether your pension, savings or the sale of a property would cover it. ## What about your visa?

Moving into a care home doesn't change your visa. If you're here on a retirement extension, you still need to meet the money rules each year and renew it, and you still need to do your 90-day reports. Most care homes will help with this or know an agent who will, but ask before you move in.

The care home also becomes your address, so a new TM30 needs filing for it. My guide to [TM30 and the 90-day report](/articles/tm30-and-90-day-report-pattaya) explains how that works, and the [retirement visa guide](/thai-visas/retirement-visa) has the money rules.

## How to plan ahead

The best time to think about care is when you don't need it. A few things I'd sort out while you're well:

1. **Write down your wishes.** Where you'd want to be cared for, and who should make decisions if you can't.
2. **Make a will.** A Thai will for anything you own here is worth doing, and it saves your family a lot of trouble.
3. **Know your money.** Work out what you could afford each month, and where it would come from.
4. **Tell someone.** Make sure a friend here and your family at home know who your doctor is, where your papers are and who to call.
5. **Visit a couple of homes.** Even if you don't need one for years, you'll know what's available and what it costs.
